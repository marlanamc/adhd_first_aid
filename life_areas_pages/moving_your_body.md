## 🏃‍♀️ Moving Your Body

Exercise helps ADHD, but starting can feel **mentally impossible**. You might want to move, but get stuck in **decision fatigue**, **time confusion**, or a lack of motivation until it's too late. The struggle isn't laziness, it's about building momentum when your brain resists activation.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
**Movement** doesn't have to be a **workout**. It can be a **stretch**, a **dance break**, a **walk around the block**. **ADHD bodies** hold **tension**, and movement helps **release it**. You don't need **motivation**. You need **motion**. **Start small**. You deserve to **feel better** in your body.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're not going to **think your way out** of **stagnation**. **Move. Your. Body.** You don't need a **plan**, a **gym**, or a **perfect outfit**, you need to **break the inertia**. **Sweat**. **Walk**. **Stretch**. **Do something**. **Now**.

### 🧠 Why Moving Your Body Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Really want to exercise… but never start
  - Waste time deciding what workout to do
  - Get overwhelmed by clothes, logistics, or the weather
  - Skip it entirely if your day doesn't go as planned
  - Feel guilty for being "lazy," even though you're exhausted

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes it hard to plan, prioritize, or follow through
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** leads you to over- or underestimate how long it will take
  - 💥 **Low dopamine** makes boring or repetitive exercise hard to initiate
  - 🔁 **Overwhelm and decision fatigue** cause shutdown before starting
  - 🧠 **Working memory issues** make multi-step workouts feel disjointed

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Movement

- **Reframe "exercise" as "any movement"**: Aim for motion, not perfection
- **Start with interest**: ADHD brains respond better to fun, novel, exciting activities
- **Break it way down**: "Put on sports bra" is a valid first step
- **Use visual cues**: Leave sneakers by door, yoga mat rolled out
- **Movement is medicine**: You're regulating your brain, not punishing yourself

### 🛠️ Strategies for Moving Your Body

#### 🎯 Reframe "Exercise" as "Any Movement"

- **Forget rigid routines**: Aim for motion, not perfection
- **Start with interest**: Dance, martial arts, hiking, fun beats forced
- **Use YouTube for instant options**: "5-minute stretch," "chill yoga," "dance workouts", no thinking, just hit play
- **Stack movement into your day**: Take stairs, stretch during Netflix, walk while on phone

#### 🧱 Break It Down

- **Try micro-movements**: "Put on sports bra," "Step outside," "Stretch for 30 seconds"
- **5-4-3-2-1 method**: Interrupt overthinking and just begin
- **2-minute rule**: If it takes less than two minutes, do it right now

#### ⏰ Time + Environment Support

- **Set movement timers**: 5-15 minutes to start small
- **Use visual cues**: Sneakers by door, yoga mat rolled out
- **Wear sensory-friendly clothes**: Uncomfortable waistband can derail everything

#### 👯 Add Accountability

- **Body double**: With friend, partner, or pet, even over video or text
- **Join a class**: Built-in structure equals less mental load
- **Track progress visually**: Wall calendar stickers, habit apps, "Moved Today" list

### 🧭 Movement ≠ Punishment

- "Movement is medicine", you're regulating your brain, not punishing yourself
- Progress beats perfection, even one minute of motion shifts your energy
- Your body deserves care, not shame, you don't have to earn rest or movement
- Celebrate wins, danced in the kitchen? That counts. Moved at all today? Amazing.

> Motion creates momentum. And momentum makes everything else easier.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> You don't need to overhaul your life, you just need to create tiny cracks in the wall of inertia. The first step is the hardest, but it's also the one that gets you free.
