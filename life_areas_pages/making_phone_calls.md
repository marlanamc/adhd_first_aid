## ☎️ Making Phone Calls

Phone calls can trigger **executive dysfunction**, **social anxiety**, and **rejection sensitivity**. ADHD brains may freeze before dialing, avoid voicemails, or forget to follow up. It's not flakiness, it's a real barrier rooted in discomfort, memory lapses, and unpredictable timing.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Phone calls can feel like **confrontation** or **performance**, especially when you can't script or prepare. **ADHD and anxiety** make this hard. Write a **quick outline**. Practice aloud. You're allowed to feel **awkward** and do it anyway.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You've put this off **long enough**. **One call** is standing between you and **peace of mind**. You don't need the **perfect script**, you need a **dial tone**. Pick up the phone and **get it done**. 60 seconds of discomfort beats another week of **dread**.

### 🧠 Why Phone Calls Are Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Keep meaning to call but never do
  - Forget what you needed to say once someone answers
  - Get overwhelmed by voicemails or scheduling
  - Freeze when you hear "please leave a message after the tone"
  - Feel judged, awkward, or unsure how to end the call

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** turns dialing into a 12-step task
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** means you avoid calling until it's "too late"
  - 💥 **Low dopamine** makes boring tasks hard to initiate
  - 🔁 **Rejection sensitivity and phone anxiety** create emotional overload
  - 🧠 **Working memory lapses** make it hard to keep your thoughts straight

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Phone Calls

- **Prepare without over-preparing**: A few bullets beats a perfect script
- **Give yourself permission to be imperfect**: Awkward calls still count as wins
- **Use your body**: Movement, fidgets, and headsets help you focus
- **Follow up immediately**: Don't trust your memory to handle action items
- **Start small**: One call builds momentum for the next

### 🛠️ Strategies for Phone Calls

#### 📝 Before the Call

- **Write down your key points**: Just a few bullets
- **Jot down what you need from them**: Info, action, decision
- **Gather what you might need**: Account #, calendar, pen
- **Overestimate the time**: Give yourself a buffer
- **Set the vibe**: Quiet space, fidget toy, tea, whatever helps
- **Decide on a "win"**: What's the smallest successful outcome?

#### 📞 During the Call

- **Use a headset or speakerphone**: So you can take notes or move
- **Take notes as they talk**: Don't rely on memory
- **Repeat info back**: "So just to make sure I got it…"
- **Be direct**: You don't owe a backstory
- **It's okay to say**: "Can you repeat that?" or "Do you mind sending that in writing?"

#### ✅ After the Call

- **Transfer action items immediately**: To your planner, calendar, or to-do app
- **Send a follow-up email**: If it's work-related or has moving parts
- **Reward yourself**: Even with a stretch or quick break
- **Check it off**: Done is done!

#### 🧠 Getting Yourself to Dial

- **Use a script template**: "Hi, my name is ____. I'm calling about ____. I had a question about ____."
- **Pair with something pleasurable**: Tea + phone call, fidget + phone call
- **Body double**: Have someone sit with you while you dial
- **Set a timer**: Just commit to making one call
- **Schedule a call window**: Same time, same place in your day
- **Start with a voicemail**: If it helps you build momentum

### 🧭 Phone Calls ≠ Performance

- A bad call isn't a bad day
- You don't have to sound perfect, you just have to show up
- You are allowed to feel uncomfortable and still take action
- Every phone call made is proof that you can do hard things

> A 2-minute call might clear a week of dread.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> You're not lazy. Your brain just needs a little scaffolding. Give yourself permission to dial messy, and hang up proud.
