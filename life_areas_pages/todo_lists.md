## ✅ To-Do Lists

To-do lists can feel like a trap. You write them down, then avoid them completely, or get overwhelmed by where to start. ADHD brains crave structure but struggle with **follow-through**, **decision fatigue**, and **reward delay**. You're not unmotivated, you just need a system that works with your mind, not against it.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Your **to-do list** isn't working because your brain doesn't operate in a **straight line**. **Break things down**. Use **verbs**. Only write what you _**can actually do today**_. Make it **ADHD-proof**. You're not **bad at tasks**, you're bad at **bad lists**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
Stop **rewriting** the same **15 things**. Your to-do list is a **graveyard of guilt**. **Simplify**. **Prioritize**. **Do the damn task**. Stop **organizing** your tasks and start **executing**.

### 🧠 Why To-Do Lists Are Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Forget what you were just about to do
  - Have a list so long it feels impossible to start
  - Make a list... and then never look at it again
  - Freeze when there are too many options

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** affects prioritizing, sequencing, and getting started
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** makes it hard to estimate how long things take or when to do them
  - 💥 **Dopamine dysregulation** makes boring tasks feel impossible to start
  - 🔁 **Working memory struggles** mean you forget steps or details
  - 🧠 **Perfectionism and overwhelm** can lead to shutdown or avoidance

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly To-Do Lists

- **Externalize everything**: Your brain is not a storage unit, write it all down
- **Break it way down**: "Clean kitchen" becomes "Unload dishwasher"
- **Limit the list**: Focus on 3–5 important things per day
- **Add activation cues**: Context, reminders, and dopamine boosts
- **Use support systems**: Body doubling, accountability, community

### 🛠️ Strategies for To-Do Lists

#### 🧠 Externalize Everything

- **Brain dump regularly**: Get all tasks, worries, and ideas out of your head
- **One capture system**: Notebook, app, or voice notes for all incoming tasks
- **Checklists for recurring tasks**: Morning routine, packing lists
- **Sticky notes at point of performance**: Door, mirror, laptop

#### 📋 Simplify Your Task List

- **Break it down**: Turn big tasks into tiny, clear steps
- **Limit the list**: 3–5 important things per day, ADHD brains hate long lists
- **Priority system**: Color-coding (🔴 must do today, 🟢 this week, 🔵 later)
- **"Someday/Maybe" list**: So ideas don't clutter your main list

#### 🕰 Use Time to Your Advantage

- **Time blocking**: Schedule focus time, buffer time, and rest
- **Visual timers**: Time Timer, app, or kitchen timer to stay grounded
- **Overestimate time**: Build in extra time to reduce stress
- **Add urgency**: Fake deadlines, gamify your list, pair with something fun

#### 🚦 Create Activation Cues

- **Add context**: "Email landlord" → "Find lease, email landlord before 3pm"
- **Set reminders**: Phone alarms or calendar alerts for specific tasks
- **Start with 5 minutes**: To overcome initiation resistance
- **Pair with dopamine boosts**: Music, movement, or post-task reward

#### 👯‍♀️ Add Support and Accountability

- **Body doubling**: Work beside someone (in person or virtually) for momentum
- **Accountability partner**: Share your goals with a friend
- **ADHD coaching**: For personalized strategy building
- **Community**: Join ADHD groups to share wins and get support

### 🧭 To-Do Lists ≠ Productivity Performance

- You're not forgetful or lazy, your brain is wired for a different kind of planning
- If you wrote down a task today, that's a win
- If you crossed off one item, that's a win
- If you gave yourself grace instead of shame, that's a huge win

> Your list is a tool, not a test.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> The fact that you're reading this and trying makes you incredibly capable. You're not bad at tasks, you just need systems that work with your brain, not against it.
