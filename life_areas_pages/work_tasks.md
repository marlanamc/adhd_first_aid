## 💼 Work Tasks

Work with ADHD can feel like constantly playing catch-up. Even when you know what to do, **initiating**, **focusing**, and **finishing** can be daily battles. It's not about laziness or competence,  it's about navigating a work world not built for neurodivergent brains.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not **lazy at work**,  you're fighting **executive dysfunction**, **unclear priorities**, and maybe **RSD**. **Break tasks into steps**. Use **body doubling**, **timers**, or **visual boards**. Your brain needs **structure**, not **shame**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
Your **job** isn't going to **do itself**. You don't need to "**feel ready**",  you need to **start**. **Open the task**. **Do the next step**. **Keep your commitments**,  or you'll keep facing **consequences**.

### 🧠 Why Work Tasks Are Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Have 30 browser tabs open and forget what you were doing
  - Avoid starting a boring or complex task until it's too late
  - Get distracted halfway through a project and never return
  - Forget what you agreed to do in a meeting

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** impacts prioritizing, starting, and finishing tasks
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** causes poor time estimation and last-minute panic
  - 💥 **Motivation** depends on urgency, novelty, or interest, not importance
  - 🔁 **Shame and negative self-talk** can shut you down or make it hard to ask for help
  - 🧠 **Working memory failures** mean ideas and to-dos vanish without reminders

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Work Tasks

- **Externalize everything**: Brain dumps and single capture systems reduce overwhelm
- **Break things way down**: "Finish presentation" becomes "Find chart," "Write intro"
- **Time block with buffers**: Schedule more time than you think you need
- **Lean on support**: Body doubling, accountability, and accommodations help
- **Redefine productivity**: Some days are deep work, others are just showing up

### 🛠️ Strategies for Work Tasks

#### 🌍 Externalize Everything

- **Brain dump daily**: Offload every to-do, worry, and idea to reduce overwhelm
- **Single capture system**: One main place for all tasks (Notion, Google Keep, paper pad)
- **Alarms and timers**: Multiple reminders to check in, start tasks, or switch activities
- **Sticky notes and checklists**: Visual cues at the point of action

#### ✅ Prioritize and Break Things Down

- **Top 3 daily priorities**: Don't overload your day, focus on the most important
- **Chunk the big stuff**: Turn "Finish Presentation" into tiny tasks
- **Color code your list**: Red = Urgent, Yellow = Needs action, Blue = Nice to do
- **Ask for clarification**: Don't waste time guessing expectations

#### ⏰ Manage Time in ADHD-Friendly Ways

- **Time block with buffers**: Add 15–30 minutes to what you think you need
- **Pomodoro technique**: 25 minutes on, 5 minutes off, repeat and take longer breaks
- **Visual timers**: Use timers that show time disappearing (Time Timer, Forest app)
- **Transition cues**: Music, alarms, or rituals to help you move between tasks

#### 🧡 Create Workflows That Work for You

- **Automate repeats**: Templates for emails, recurring calendar events, workflows
- **Standardize processes**: Step-by-step checklists for common tasks
- **Schedule admin time**: Block off time each week for emails, files, and forms
- **Visual boards**: Kanban boards like Trello or sticky notes on a wall

#### 👥 Lean on Support

- **Body double**: Work alongside someone (even virtually) to stay focused
- **Accountability buddy**: Share your top 3 daily goals with a friend or coworker
- **Talk to your supervisor**: Frame needs as productivity boosters
- **Explore accommodations**: Flexible schedules, written instructions, noise-canceling headphones

#### 🌿 Prevent Burnout

- **Take real breaks**: Move your body, eat, step outside, don't just scroll
- **Watch for overcommitment**: Say "Let me check my bandwidth" before agreeing
- **Celebrate what you did do**: Reflect on wins daily, even small ones

### 🧭 Work Tasks ≠ Your Worth

- You're not lazy, broken, or behind, your brain is just wired differently
- Some days are about deep work, others are just about showing up,  both count
- The fact that you're reading this and trying to support yourself makes you successful
- Asking for clarity instead of hiding is a win

> Your accommodations aren't special treatment,  they're leveling the playing field.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> If you started that hard task today, that's a win. If you asked for clarity instead of hiding, that's a win. If you scheduled your focus time, that's a win. You're learning to work with your brain.
