## 🥗 Meal Planning

Meal planning sounds simple, but for ADHD brains, it means predicting **future needs**, **making decisions**, and building a system. That's a lot of **executive load**. You're not failing if it falls apart, your brain just works better with support, not pressure.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Meal planning isn't about being **perfect**, it's about avoiding the **6pm panic**. **ADHD brains** do better with **repeat meals**, **flexible templates**, and **visual options**. **Keep it simple**. You're allowed to eat the **same thing** 3x a week.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're not "**intuitive eating**", you're **avoiding planning** and relying on **impulse**. That's why you're **hangry** and **over budget**. **Sit down**. **Plan three meals**. **Grocery shop**. Stop **winging it**.

### 🧠 Why Meal Planning Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Feel overwhelmed by choices and give up
  - Buy groceries with no idea what you'll make
  - Forget what food you already have and let it go bad
  - Hyperfixate on cooking for one week, then avoid it for three
  - Eat random snacks all day and crash from underfueling

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes planning, sequencing, and follow-through harder  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** causes missed meals and underestimating prep time  
  - 💥 **Decision fatigue** turns "What's for dinner?" into a daily crisis  
  - 🔁 **Perfectionism and shame** can lead to avoidance or all-or-nothing thinking  
  - 🧠 **Working memory issues** make it hard to remember ingredients or steps

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Meal Planning

- **Good enough is good enough**: Repeated meals and shortcuts are valid  
- **Externalize everything**: Use visuals, checklists, and reminders  
- **Less variety = less overwhelm**: Hyperfixation meals are totally fine  
- **Focus on fuel**: Eating something is better than skipping entirely  
- **Your future self deserves support, not punishment**

### 🛠️ Strategies for Meal Planning

#### 🧠 Build Systems That Work With Your Brain

- **Start Simple**: "I'm not lazy, I'm building a system that supports how my brain works"
- **Progress Over Perfection**: A simple meal you eat is better than a perfect plan you abandon
- **Release Expectations**: Stop expecting yourself to meal prep like a TikTok chef if that's not your vibe
- **"Deal-a-Meal" Strategy**: Write your go-to meals on index cards. "Shuffle and deal" 5 for the week = instant plan, no decision fatigue

#### 🧃 Simplify Your System

- **"ADHD Easy Meals"**: Choose meals that are quick, low-mess, low-effort, and repeatable. Framework: "What protein do I have? What can I add to that?"
- **Repeat Meals**: Hyperfixation meals reduce decisions and make shopping easy. If it works, it works, no need to reinvent the wheel every week
- **Stock Smart**: Frozen veggies, microwave grains, canned beans, and rotisserie chicken are your friends. Store perishable food visibly, not buried in the crisper
- **No-Cook Options**: Check out this list of [100 No-Cook Meal Items from Black Girl, Lost Keys](https://blackgirllostkeys.com/adhd/100-no-cook-meal-items/). Great for low-spoon days, sensory overload, or meal planning avoidance
- **Batch What Works**: Roast a tray of protein and veggies and use it 3 ways. Repeat lunch all week if that's easier than variety
- **Meal Kits or Outsourcing**: If budget allows, try meal delivery services to reduce decision-making and prep. Often cheaper than takeout and helps you eat better consistently

#### ⏰ Manage Time & Motivation

- **Schedule Meal Times**: Add breakfast/lunch/dinner blocks to your calendar or planner. Even a snack or smoothie counts, consistency > perfection
- **Use Timers & Alarms**: Set reminders to start cooking. Use a pre-cook timer to help transition from other tasks
- **Track Reality**: Most meals take less time than your brain assumes. Tracking helps reduce procrastination and over-planning
- **Chunk the Process**: Break "meal plan" into steps: 1. Pick 3 meals, 2. Write a grocery list, 3. Order groceries, 4. Put food away
- **Kindness to Future You**: "I don't want to cook now, but Future Me will be glad I chopped this onion." Frame it as care, not a chore
- **Use Rewards**: "After I plan my meals, I get to watch my show." Pair a dopamine reward with a boring but useful task
- **Double It Up**: Do meal planning while chatting with a friend. Meal prep while listening to a podcast. Body doubling works even over Zoom

#### 🍳 Focus on Fuel & Nutrition

- **Prioritize Protein**: Protein fuels your brain and stabilizes energy. Start the day with protein (eggs, yogurt, tofu, cottage cheese, shakes)
- **Stay Hydrated**: Dehydration = brain fog + low mood. Pair hydration with another habit (e.g., drink water every time you open the fridge)
- **Mindful Eating**: Try to sit down and slow down, it helps with digestion and awareness. Sensory-friendly utensils and bowls may help if textures are hard
- **Watch for Food Triggers**: Some ultra-processed foods worsen ADHD symptoms or mood. Pay attention to what foods help you feel sharp, focused, or calm

#### 🧍 Get Support & Accountability

- **Delegate What You Can**: Can someone else plan, shop, or prep a meal or two? Even just sitting with you while you plan can help
- **ADHD Coaching**: Get help developing meal planning systems that actually work for your brain. Focus on routines, not rules
- **Join a Community**: You're not the only one eating cereal for dinner again. ADHD groups (online or IRL) are great for tips, solidarity, and low-shame sharing

### 🧭 Meal Planning ≠ Morality

- Three components = a meal: something to fuel, something to fill, something to hydrate.
- You're allowed to eat the same thing every day. You're allowed to eat in snack format. You're allowed to eat imperfectly and still deserve to feel good.
> Feeding yourself and your family is not a moral test. A frozen pizza is not a parenting failure. A snack plate is not a character flaw. Nourishment can look like takeout, toast, or leftovers. The act of feeding yourself, however it happens, is an act of care, not a performance.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> If you wrote down one meal, that's planning. If you made a grocery list, that's progress. If you ate something today, that's a win. You're not failing, you're feeding yourself in the way that works for your brain.
