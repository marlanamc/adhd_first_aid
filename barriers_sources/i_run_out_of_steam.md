## 📚 Sources: I Run Out of Steam

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological and emotional roots of ADHD-related burnout and energy crashes — and offer strategies for conserving energy, embracing rest, rebuilding momentum, and learning to work _with_ your natural rhythms.

---

### 🧠 Understanding ADHD Fatigue & Burnout

- **Kemp, Jennifer, & Mitchelson, Monique. (2024). _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD_.**  
    Focuses on neurodivergent burnout as the result of demands exceeding coping ability. Offers strategies for slowing down, resting, and replenishing internal resources.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica. (2024). _How to ADHD_.**  
    Frames energy management as essential for ADHD, offering tools like “breadcrumbs” and permission slips to pause, rest, and return without shame.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki, & Wright, Pete D. (2024). _Unapologetically ADHD_.**  
    Encourages curiosity over self-judgment when experiencing energy dips. Reinforces the idea that breaks don’t erase progress.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A. (2008). _The Disorganized Mind_.**  
    Emphasizes recognizing energy patterns and having a “failsafe” plan for low-energy days, including permission to rest and reset.
    

---

### 🪫 Rest as a Right — Not a Reward

- **Davis, KC. (2022). _How to Keep House While Drowning_.**  
    Central quote: “Rest is a right, not a reward.” This book reframes rest as necessary and valid, especially when care tasks are never-ending.
    
- **Lakshmin, Pooja. (2023). _Real Self-Care_.**  
    Reinforces the idea that “real self-care” is gentle, personalized, and includes taking breaks without guilt.
    
- **Solden, Sari, Frank, Michelle, & Littman, Ellen. (2019). _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_.**  
    Promotes honoring internal emotional processes and resting as part of untangling shame and internalized pressure.
    
- **Solden, Sari. (2013). _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_.**  
    Explores the emotional toll of “trying harder” and the protective need for rest. Suggests emotional understanding before action.
    

---

### 🔋 Energy Management & Gentle Productivity

- **Abdaal, Ali. (2023). _Feel-Good Productivity_.**  
    Introduces energy conservation as essential to sustainable output. Quotes like “Do less so you can unlock more” appear in the guide.
    
- **Dee, Lisa. (2025). _Healthy Happy ADHD_.**  
    Advocates for physical care (food, rest, movement) and warns against passion burnout. Encourages tracking signals and honoring energy crashes.
    
- **Donovan, Dani. (2022). _The Anti-Planner_.**  
    Offers specific tools for recharging when burned out. Normalizes low-energy periods and reframes “laziness” as an invalid concept.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure. (2025). _Tiny Experiments_.**  
    Discusses burnout prevention and mindful productivity. Suggests scaling down to maintain consistency and energy.
    
- **Pink, Richard, & Pink, Roxanne. (2024). _Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies_.**  
    Shares lived experiences of abandoning goals or needing to restart, validating energy loss and non-linear paths.
    

---

### 🥦 Physical Fuel & Recovery Tools

- **Miller, Lucinda. (2024). _Brain Brilliance_.**  
    Explains how nutrient deficiencies and inflammation impact energy. Supports guide’s call to “get food” and supplement when needed.
