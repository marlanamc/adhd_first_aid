## 📚 Sources: I Feel Emotionally Blocked

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how emotional paralysis can show up in ADHD — often misread as laziness or apathy — and offer strategies to untangle shame, understand emotional defenses, and gently reconnect with what you're feeling.

---

### 🧠 Understanding Emotional Paralysis

- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ – Introduces the “Wall of Awful” as an emotional barrier built from past pain, and offers tools like feeling wheels and body-based awareness to break through blocks.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ – Reframes stuckness as a self-protective freeze and provides gentle, validating language plus practical micro-strategies to unfreeze.
    
- **Frank, Michelle, Littman, Ellen, & Solden, Sari.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_ – Emphasizes that you must address emotional weight and self-worth before executive function strategies can stick.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_ – Normalizes trauma-linked shutdown and emotional overwhelm, and teaches how to name and feel emotions somatically.
    
- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_ – Describes the toxic shame loop that leads to immobilization and self-blame, and advocates for emotional release.
    
- **van der Kolk, Bessel.** _The Body Keeps the Score_ – Explains how trauma lives in the body and causes emotional numbing, paralysis, or freeze. Movement and co-regulation are essential.
    
- **Wiest, Brianna.** _The Mountain Is You_ – Describes how fear of feeling can drive self-sabotage and emotional suppression, and emphasizes the need to face what you’re avoiding.
    

---

### 🧱 Barriers Beneath the Block

- **Kegan, Robert & Lahey, Lisa.** _Immunity to Change_ – Introduces the concept of hidden fears and commitments that unconsciously resist change, even when we “want” it.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_ – Describes the ADHD shame spiral and emotional dysregulation, especially in the context of RSD.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_ – Offers Emotional Freedom Techniques (EFT) and RSD insights to work with intense internal resistance.
    
- **Carder, Meredith.** _It All Makes Sense Now_ – Encourages nervous system regulation and naming emotional blocks instead of trying to power through them.
    
- **Solden, Sari.** _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_ – Discusses how shame and survival responses create “brick walls” that block progress and visibility.
    

---

### 🧘‍♀️ Self-Compassion and Soft Starts

- **Davis, KC.** _How to Keep House While Drowning_ – Emphasizes self-worth apart from productivity and offers tools for reconnecting when you feel “too stuck to start.”
    
- **Lakshmin, Pooja.** _Real Self-Care_ – Shows how guilt and people-pleasing prevent emotional expression, and reframes rest and boundaries as radical, necessary care.
    
- **Fogg, BJ.** _Tiny Habits_ – Explains why even frozen states can be eased with “laughably small” micro-movements — and how momentum builds from there.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ – Encourages reframing procrastination as information, using affect labeling to reduce emotional overload, and taking tiny emotional risks.
    
- **Miller, William & Rollnick, Stephen.** _Motivational Interviewing_ – Describes “guiding” as a relational strategy to help someone stuck in ambivalence move gently forward.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy.** _The Disorganized Mind_ – Discusses runaway emotions, mental loops, and how to use a log or outside perspective to regain clarity and control.
    
- **Alexander-Grout, Maddy.** _Mad About Money_ – Briefly acknowledges grief as an ADHD-relevant emotional force, helping expand the emotional vocabulary around blocks.
