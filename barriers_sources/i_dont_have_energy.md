## 📚 Sources: I Don’t Have Energy

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of ADHD-related fatigue and offer strategies for managing executive function demands, restoring energy, pacing your efforts, honoring rest, and building sustainable motivation.

---

### 🧠 Understanding ADHD & Energy Drain

- **Dawson & Guare** – _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_  
    Executive function depletes mental fuel quickly; understanding your brain’s “effort pool” can help you pace better.
    
- **Kemp & Mitchelson** – _Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_  
    Burnout happens when demands exceed capacity. Sensory overload, emotional strain, and disorganization drain energy fast.
    
- **Levrini, Abigail** – _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_  
    Breaks down how EF issues like planning, inhibition, and self-regulation wear down energy over time.
    
- **Solden, Frank & Littman** – _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_  
    Describes how everyday cognitive juggling can lead to chronic exhaustion and emotional depletion.
    
- **Kinzer & Wright** – _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Constant task-switching and planning fatigue drain energy; rest and simplicity aren’t luxuries — they’re survival tools.
    
- **Dee, Lisa** – _Healthy Happy ADHD_  
    ADHD makes even basic self-care tasks exhausting. You’re not lazy — your brain burns fuel differently.
    

---

### 💤 Burnout, Rest & Recovery

- **Davis, KC** – _How to Keep House While Drowning_  
    Rest is a right, not a reward. You don’t need to “earn” rest — you need it because your body and brain are working hard.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure** – _Tiny Experiments_  
    Rest isn’t weakness. It’s information. Listen to your fatigue and treat it with curiosity instead of shame.
    
- **Abdaal, Ali** – _Feel-Good Productivity_  
    Recharging isn’t optional. Doing too much too fast drains momentum — tiny actions and joyful breaks restore it.
    
- **Lakshmin, Pooja** – _Real Self-Care_  
    Stillness may feel uncomfortable at first, but learning to rest is a strength — not a flaw.
    
- **Carder, Meredith** – _It All Makes Sense Now_  
    ADHD brains often feel guilty resting. This book reframes rest as an essential ingredient for focus and wellbeing.
    
- **Solden, Frank & Littman** – _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_  
    Sometimes the most productive thing you can do is stop and replenish.
    

---

### 🏃‍♀️ Nutrition, Movement & Sleep

- **Derbyshire, Emma** – _Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia_  
    Details vitamins and hydration tips to reduce fatigue and support brain energy.
    
- **Miller, Lisa** – _Brain Brilliance_  
    Explains how B vitamins and nutrition fuel mitochondrial energy and reduce mental fatigue.
    
- **Hallowell & Ratey** – _ADHD 2.0_  
    Exercise, movement, and proper sleep are foundational for focus and energy regulation.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica** – _How to ADHD_  
    Emphasizes rest on “bad brain days” and acknowledges that movement and sleep are focus tools, not luxuries.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy** – _The Disorganized Mind_  
    Learn your peak energy windows and plan accordingly — don’t waste your best energy on low-priority tasks.
    

---

### 🔁 Motivation, Micro-Steps & Momentum

- **Abdaal, Ali** – _Feel-Good Productivity_  
    Inertia keeps you still — motion gets you back. Focus on the _smallest next step_ to generate energy.
    
- **Kinzer & Wright** – _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Use 5-minute rules, whiteboards, rewards, and “micro-tasks” to start when you feel drained.
    
- **Donovan, Dani** – _The Anti-Planner_  
    Offers tools for overcoming invisible barriers and building energy through play, music, and novelty.
    
- **Carder, Meredith** – _It All Makes Sense Now_  
    Includes a “motivation toolbox” for tracking what actually helps you engage with energy.
    
- **Forte, Tiago** – _The PARA Method_  
    Digital clutter = mental fatigue. Clearing your work environment helps restore mental energy.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure** – _Tiny Experiments_  
    Reframes procrastination as a clue, not a flaw. Use time-blocking and curiosity to work with your natural rhythms.
    

---

### 🤝 Support, Coaching & Asking for Help

- **McCabe, Jessica** – _How to ADHD_  
    Body doubling, ADHD coaches, and accountability buddies all make energy management easier.
    
- **Kinzer & Wright** – _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Asking for help doesn’t mean you’ve failed — it means you’re building smarter systems.
    
- **Kemp & Mitchelson** – _Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_  
    Asking for help is an act of self-compassion. Community reduces shame and boosts capacity.
    
- **Donovan, Dani** – _The Anti-Planner_  
    Body-doubling and gentle nudges can get you moving when your energy feels stuck.
    
- **Solden, Sari** – _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_  
    ADHD-friendly support systems reduce decision fatigue and preserve your emotional energy.
    
- **Kustow, James** – _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_  
    When you're out of fuel, group programs and coaches can help carry the load.
