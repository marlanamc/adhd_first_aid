## 📚 Sources: I Forgot

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of ADHD-related forgetfulness and offer strategies for externalizing memory, building supportive systems, and letting go of shame when things slip your mind.

---

### 🧠 The Science of ADHD & Memory

- **Russell Barkley** – _Taking Charge of ADHD_  
    Frames forgetfulness as an issue of **executive dysfunction**, especially **working memory**. Emphasizes that ADHD is not about knowing what to do — it's about **doing what you know**.
    
- **Edward Hallowell & John Ratey** – _Driven to Distraction_ & _ADHD 2.0_  
    Pioneering ADHD experts who explain how **attention lapses**, **memory overload**, and **emotional dysregulation** reinforce forgetfulness — and why support systems matter.
    
- **Peg Dawson & Richard Guare** – _Smart but Scattered_  
    Breaks down the science of **executive skills**, showing how weak working memory leads to mental fatigue. Recommends **externalization tools** (timers, reminders, checklists).
    
- **Nancy Ratey** – _The Disorganized Mind_  
    Encourages **written structure** and consistent self-monitoring to stay on track. Advocates for personalizing systems instead of forcing yourself to "remember better".

### 🧩 ADHD Strategy & System Design

- **Tiago Forte** – _Building a Second Brain_ / _The PARA Method_  
    Introduces the concept of an **external brain** — organizing information in Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives so nothing gets lost. Promotes trust in **search > sorting**.
    
- **Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright** – _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    States outright: _“You cannot rely on your memory when you have ADHD.”_ Offers a **step-by-step system** for building shame-free, sustainable planning tools.
    
- **Jaclyn Paul** – _Order from Chaos_  
    Advises **assuming you’ll forget** and creating systems around that truth. Uses tools like the “Someday Maybe” list and thought containers to hold ideas safely.
    
- **Anne-Laure Le Cunff** – _Tiny Experiments_  
    Advocates for designing systems based on **your actual behavior patterns**, not idealized ones. Her “digital garden” concept mirrors the idea of an ADHD-friendly knowledge base.

### 🛠 Practical Tools for Remembering

- **Jessica McCabe** – _How to ADHD_  
    Offers friendly, research-backed advice on **"how to remember stuff"**, including encoding strategies, memory cues, and forgiving yourself for forgetting. Encourages working _with_ your brain, not against it.
    
- **Dani Donovan** – _The Anti-Planner_  
    Provides **real-world strategies** for people who struggle with routine. Emphasizes that **forgetfulness isn’t laziness**, and that shame blocks action. Tools include external accountability, micro-starts, and reframing failure.
    
- **Kate Kelly & Peggy Ramundo** – _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!_  
    One of the first books to **destigmatize forgetfulness** in ADHD. Explains how info may never have been encoded or retrieved properly — and why compassion matters more than “trying harder”.
    

---

### 💗 Emotional Impact of Forgetfulness

- **Sari Solden & Michelle Frank** – _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_  
    Highlights how **organizational failures** (like forgetting) lead to **shame and self-doubt**, especially in women. Offers validation, externalization tools, and ways to reconnect with your sense of worth.
    
- **Kate Kelly & Peggy Ramundo** – _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!_  
    (Also listed above) — Speaks directly to the **emotional toll** of forgetfulness and encourages **healing the relationship with yourself** through understanding and forgiveness.
