## 📚 Sources: It’s Not Urgent

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain why ADHD brains often ignore non-urgent tasks until it’s too late — and offer strategies for working with time blindness, activating without pressure, using prompts and structure, and building gentle accountability.

---

### 🧠 Why Urgency Helps ADHD Brains Activate

- **Kinzer, N., & Wright, P. D. (2024). _Unapologetically ADHD_.**  
    Highlights ADHD's need for urgency to kickstart action. Introduces intentional planning tools, visual workflows, and accountability strategies like the “Workbox,” “Weekly Focus,” and “Daily Detour.”
    
- **Ratey, N. A. (2008). _The Disorganized Mind_.**  
    Emphasizes creating **false deadlines** and enlisting an **accountability partner** or **“witness”** to stimulate initiation. Describes how ADHDers may delay because of internal self-deception around time.
    
- **McCabe, J. (2024). _How to ADHD_.**  
    Introduces **time pillars** and rituals to create structure. Encourages embracing ADHD-friendly time supports rather than traditional calendars or alarms alone.
    

---

### 🛠️ Planning Systems, Prompts & Activation Tools

- **Fogg, B. J. (2019). _Tiny Habits_.**  
    Teaches the behavior formula: **Motivation + Ability + Prompt**. Encourages redesigning environments with easy prompts and low-friction behaviors to initiate action.
    
- **Forte, T. (2022). _Building a Second Brain_.**  
    Focuses on organizing digital life around **action**, not just information. His PARA method and Progressive Summarization make tasks and ideas easier to recall and move forward on.
    
- **Paul, J. (2018). _Order from Chaos_.**  
    Recommends the **path of least resistance** and building custom, flexible systems. Emphasizes that failing to plan is not laziness — it’s about finding what truly works for your brain.
    
- **Dawson, P., & Guare, R. (2015). _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_.**  
    Identifies executive skill deficits behind delay. Offers **simple planning**, informal coaching, checklists, and reflection strategies to build follow-through capacity without overcomplicating.
    

---

### ⏰ Deadlines, Time Blindness & Follow-Through

- **Gilkey, C. (2019). _Start Finishing_.**  
    Argues that **“undated items are unsigned checks”** — and underscores the need for deadlines and breaking work into **sequenced chunks**. Encourages building a **“success pack”** for support.
    
- **Abdaal, A. (2023). _Feel-Good Productivity_.**  
    Explains how the amygdala responds to open-ended tasks as threats, making **“next small step”** clarity essential to reduce avoidance. Aims to calm the nervous system through specificity.
    
- **Miller, W. R., & Rollnick, S. (2023). _Motivational Interviewing_.**  
    Emphasizes **evoking internal motivation** before forcing action. Offers the **Ask-Offer-Ask** technique for building collaboration and ownership in planning.
