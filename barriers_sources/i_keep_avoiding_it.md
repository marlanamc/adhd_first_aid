# 📚 Sources: I Keep Avoiding It

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the emotional and neurological roots of ADHD-related avoidance and offer strategies for reducing friction, uncovering hidden fears, building self-compassion, and moving forward — one small step at a time.

---

## 🧠 Understanding Avoidance in ADHD Brains

- **Donovan – _The Anti-Planner_**  
    Defines avoidance as **self-protection**, not laziness. Identifies root causes like fear of failure, intimidation, indecision, and shame. Introduces the **“Wall of Awful”**, a foundational metaphor in your guide.
    
- **McCabe – _How to ADHD_**  
    Frames avoidance as the product of **invisible barriers** and executive dysfunction. Encourages **building a personal “toolbox”**, echoing your guide’s suggestion to **start with small experiments**.
    
- **Kinzer & Wright – _Unapologetically ADHD_**  
    Discuss how **shame spirals**, RSD, and “ADHD absolute thinking” reinforce avoidance. Emphasize planning failures are _not_ personal flaws and call for **systems built with compassion and flexibility**.
    
- **Paul – _Order from Chaos_**  
    Acknowledges that **tools can become painful reminders of failure**, leading to resistance. Offers strategies for **starting small**, managing **backlog overwhelm**, and **accepting inconsistency**.

## 🧘‍♀️ Emotional Roots + Compassionate Reframing

- **Wiest – _The Mountain Is You_**  
    Redefines self-sabotage as **self-protection**. Encourages slowing down and reframing resistance as a **signal**, not an enemy.
    
- **Le Cunff – _Tiny Experiments_**  
    Urges us to treat procrastination as a **“puzzle, not a personal flaw”**. Introduces the **“Triple Check”** (Head, Heart, Hand) method to assess why something feels blocked.
    
- **Solden, Frank & Littman – _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_**  
    Explores how **internalized judgment and fear of rejection** create avoidance patterns, especially in women. Stresses the importance of **unraveling emotional blocks** before strategic tools will stick.
    
- **Kemp & Mitchelson – _Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_**  
    Centers **fear of asking for help**, shame, and RSD as key avoidance triggers. Reframes help-seeking as a **strength** and normalizes **needing others** to move forward.
    
- **Lakshmin – _Real Self-Care_**  
    Encourages self-guided reflection over one-size-fits-all solutions. Reinforces your guide’s call to **ask tough questions** like “What’s really going on under this?”

## 🧩 Cognitive Frameworks to Name the Block

- **Kegan & Lahey – _Immunity to Change_**  
    Provides the framework for **“competing commitments”** — unconscious beliefs or fears that oppose conscious goals. Validates your guide’s advice to “**Name the discomfort**” and explore what you might be protecting yourself from.
    
- **Miller & Rollnick – _Motivational Interviewing_**  
    Argues that **resistance to change** is natural and not something to be bullied through. Encourages **collaborative curiosity** and internal motivation — directly supporting your “gentle reentry” framing.
    

---

## 🔧 Strategy Tools to Make Action Feel Possible

- **Fogg – _Tiny Habits_**  
    Introduces the “**Ability Chain**” (time, money, physical effort, mental effort, routine), explaining that reducing friction increases action. Matches your guide’s message: **fewer decisions = more action**.
    
- **Mahan (via McCabe and Donovan)**  
    Creator of the **“Wall of Awful”** metaphor, which describes avoidance as an emotional obstacle built from repeated failure and shame. This is **central** to your guide and your users’ experience.
