## 📚 Sources: I Feel Shame

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD-related shame stems from misunderstood neurobiology, emotional dysregulation, and internalized societal expectations — and offer strategies to reclaim your voice with self-compassion, community, and context.

---

### 🧠 Understanding ADHD Shame

- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_ – Defines shame as a toxic barrier that paralyzes motivation and stems from perceived inadequacy, not true character flaws.
    
- **Solden, Sari & Frank, Michelle.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_ – Describes how executive dysfunction and years of masking create deep-rooted shame and urges readers to dismantle it with radical acceptance.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ – Explains the “Wall of Awful” created by past failures and how task-based shame can stall progress. Offers tools for reframing and re-engagement.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_ – Identifies shame as a core barrier to growth and encourages replacing self-criticism with accurate, compassionate beliefs.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_ – Defines the ADHD shame spiral and its roots in Rejection Sensitivity, internalized “shoulds,” and social avoidance.
    
- **Davis, KC.** _How to Keep House While Drowning_ – Names the shame attached to everyday care tasks and challenges the morality we place on productivity.
    
- **Lakshmin, Pooja.** _Real Self-Care_ – Distinguishes between guilt and shame, calling for self-compassion and boundary setting as antidotes to chronic shame.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_ – Encourages reframing setbacks as data, not proof of failure, and warns against letting shame stop you from using tools.
    
- **Wiest, Brianna.** _The Mountain Is You_ – Frames shame as a warped extension of embarrassment that leads to self-condemnation instead of self-awareness.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ – Identifies “should” as a shame-based word and encourages replacing it with compassionate self-talk.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_ – Explores how ADHDers internalize blame, offering practical tools to replace shame with agency.
    
- **Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne.** _Small Talk_ – Debunks common ADHD “lies” and normalizes emotional sensitivity, linking shame to misunderstood neurobiology.
    
- **Maté, Gabor.** _Scattered Minds_ – Traces shame back to unmet childhood needs and offers reframes to restore self-worth.
    
- **Brewis, Alexandra & Wutich, Amber.** _Lazy, Crazy, and Disgusting_ – Explores how stigma breeds shame and reinforces self-blame across marginalized identities.
    
- **Hallowell, Edward & Ratey, John.** _ADHD 2.0_ – Emphasizes that shame holds people back more than their symptoms — and calls for reconnection and reframing.
    
- **Orlov, Melissa & Hallowell, Edward.** _The ADHD Effect on Marriage_ – Describes how shame and fear distort communication in relationships, especially when needs are unspoken.

### 🧰 Strategies to Release Shame

- **Lakshmin, Pooja.** _Real Self-Care_ – Reframes compassion and boundary-setting as radical acts that replace people-pleasing and guilt.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_ – Offers grounding practices, self-compassion scripts, and reminders that asking for help is a skill, not a weakness.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ – Encourages pairing difficult tasks with positive experiences and treating resistance as a messenger, not a moral failing.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ – Acknowledges the emotional weight of shame and helps users build momentum through visual tools and micro-steps.
    
- **Fogg, BJ.** _Tiny Habits_ – Builds sustainable behavior change by keeping tasks easy, accessible, and shame-free.
    
- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_ – Validates that executive effort is limited and encourages using community and structure instead of shame.
    
- **van der Kolk, Bessel.** _The Body Keeps the Score_ – Frames shame as a somatic response tied to trauma and emphasizes the importance of letting it move through the body.
    
- **Kegan, Robert & Lahey, Lisa.** _Immunity to Change_ – Explains how unconscious fear and self-protection can masquerade as resistance or shame, blocking change.
