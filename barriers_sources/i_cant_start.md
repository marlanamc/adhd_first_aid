## 📚 Sources: I Can’t Start

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of ADHD-related task initiation challenges and offer strategies for self-compassion, externalization, micro-tasking, body movement, dopamine support, time management, and co-working systems.

---

### 🧠 Why It’s Hard to Start

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ (2022)  
    Tackles shame-based procrastination, promotes micro-starts like “open the tab,” and emphasizes dopamine-based motivation over willpower.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_ (2024)  
    Offers somatic, emotional, and behavioral strategies for initiation — including body doubling, movement, and compassion-based action.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_ (2024)  
    Breaks down initiation into bite-sized first steps, dopamine boosting, body-doubling, intentional planning, and memory externalization.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ (2024)  
    Reframes invisible obstacles and teaches self-created “process documents” for task flow.
    
- **Solden, Sari, Frank, Michelle & Littman, Ellen.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_ (2019)  
    Suggests radical self-compassion and co-working buddies to break through paralysis.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_ (2008)  
    Emphasizes planning, breaking tasks down, setting mini-deadlines, and journaling stuck points.
    

---

### 🪜 Break It Down & Externalize the Start

- **Fogg, BJ.** _Tiny Habits_ (2019)  
    Anchors success in ridiculously small starts — like flossing one tooth or writing one word.
    
- **Gilkey, Charlie.** _Start Finishing_ (2019)  
    Provides frameworks like the “Project Pyramid” to deconstruct large goals into manageable starts.
    
- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_ (2016)  
    Recommends “start with the resource step,” and shows how checklists and alarms scaffold executive function.
    
- **Forte, Tiago.** _Building a Second Brain_ (2022)  
    Encourages offloading mental load using structured systems like PARA, so your brain can focus on action, not storage.
    

---

### 🧠 Get Support: Coaches, Coworkers, & Gentle Voices

- **CHADD / Medical News Today.** _Does ADHD Coaching Work?_  
    Presents evidence on the effectiveness of coaching to support initiation and follow-through.
    
- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_ (2006)  
    Emphasizes the value of ADD coaches and accountability support for getting into action.
    
- **Stanier, Michael Bungay.** _The Coaching Habit_ (2015)  
    Offers a low-pressure approach to self-support through questioning, small wins, and co-regulation.
    
- **Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne.** _Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies…_ (2024)  
    Names body doubling as a go-to strategy — just having someone nearby helps unlock action.
    

---

### ⏱ Time Tools & Dopamine Tricks

- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ (2025)  
    Recommends “pairing” — linking boring tasks with enjoyable stimuli like music or snacks for motivation.
    
- **Wiest, Brianna.** _The Mountain Is You_ (2020)  
    Frames resistance as fear in disguise — and motivation as the gateway to re-entry.
    
- **Davis, KC.** _How to Keep House While Drowning_ (2022)  
    Emphasizes that executive dysfunction, not laziness, is what stops initiation — and meets it with compassion.
    

---

### 🏃‍♂️ Movement, Music, & Novelty

- **Hallowell, Edward & Ratey, John.** _ADHD 2.0_ (2021)  
    Discusses “move to focus” — the brain’s need for physical activation to kickstart attention.
    
- **Dee, Lisa.** _Healthy Happy ADHD_ (2022)  
    Emphasizes exercise as activation for emotional and cognitive regulation.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Adds: “Move your body before your brain,” and “layer novelty” with new music, tools, or environments.
