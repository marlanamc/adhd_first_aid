## 📚 Sources: I Can’t Stay Focused

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of attention struggles in ADHD and offer strategies for managing distractions, externalizing memory, regulating hyperfocus, using environmental tools, and embracing curiosity over shame.

---

### 🧠 Understanding ADHD & Attention
   
- **Hallowell, Edward M. & Ratey, John J.** _Driven to Distraction_ / _ADHD 2.0_  
    Explain how a sluggish frontal cortex affects attention, and why movement and novelty help focus.
    
- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_  
    Validates that ADHD is a central nervous system disorder, not a moral failure.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_  
    Explores how dysregulation in the body impacts attention and offers sensory-friendly interventions.
    
- **Carder, Meredith.** _It All Makes Sense Now_  
    Frames ADHD focus as nonlinear but powerful when aligned with interest and strengths.
    
- **Wright, Nikki Kinzer & Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Explains why ADHD focus is interest-based and offers tools for managing hyperfocus and task-switching fatigue.
    

---

### 🛠 Tools for Managing Distraction & Filtering Noise

- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Shares techniques like “Doing/Not Doing” lists, location resets, and process documents.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    Recommends using “hyperfocus corrals,” visual checklists, and external structures to offload memory.
    
- **Forte, Tiago.** _Building a Second Brain_  
    Teaches how to structure digital and physical environments to reduce mental clutter.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Offers strategies like the “Idea Parking Lot,” app blockers, and body-doubling to stay on track.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_  
    Advocates for whiteboards, notebooks, noise filters, and thought-parking to maintain focus.
    

---

### ⏱ Managing Time, Hyperfocus & Mental Energy

- **Levrini, Abigail.** _Succeeding with Adult ADHD_  
    Suggests short recurring timers to check in and return attention when it drifts.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_  
    Discusses “attentional bottlenecks,” time blindness, and how external timers support focus.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_  
    Emphasizes intentional scheduling and “jolting buzz” timers to exit hyperfocus gently.
    
- **Productivity with ADHD – Complete Guide**  
    Recommends time blocking, buffer zones, and alarms as scaffolding tools for sustained attention.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Recommends Pomodoro timers and “leaving breadcrumbs” to ease transitions out of focus bursts.
    

---

### 🧘‍♀️ Movement, Sensory Support, & Focus Regulation

- **Hallowell & Ratey.** _ADHD 2.0_  
    Movement isn’t optional — it’s essential for regulation and attention.
    
- **Dee, Lisa.** _Healthy Happy ADHD_  
    Encourages physical activity and somatic practices for grounding the ADHD brain.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Shares personal adaptations like under-desk ellipticals and stretching breaks to sustain mental clarity.
    
- **Solden, Sari.** _Women with ADD_  
    Highlights how fidgeting, pacing, and chewing gum can support attentional engagement.
    

---

### 💭 Mindset, Self-Compassion & Focus Reframing

- **Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle & Littman, Ellen.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_  
    Offers compassionate rethinking of what focus “should” look like, and why it’s okay to work with your rhythm.
    
- **Wright, Nikki Kinzer & Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Emphasize radical acceptance, intentional focus management, and curiosity over judgment.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Reminds you: “You’re not lazy,” and offers dopamine-based focus boosts over shame-driven pressure.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    Reframes distraction as feedback, not failure — every lapse is a clue, not a condemnation.
