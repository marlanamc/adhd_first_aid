# 📚 Sources: I Got Distracted

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the ADHD brain’s unique relationship with attention, distraction, and hyperfocus — and offer tools to externalize information, adjust your environment, tame transitions, and gently redirect your focus without shame.

## 🧠 Neurological Understanding of Distraction

- **Hallowell & Ratey – _ADHD 2.0_**  
    Emphasize that distractibility is a **neurological trait**, not a flaw. Their framing supports the guide’s central message: distraction is a _brain thing_.
    
- **Levrini – _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_**  
    Explains that ADHD involves impairments in **motor and cognitive inhibition**, and recommends **writing distractions down** (idea parking lot), using **timers**, and designing your space to support focus.
    
- **Le Cunff – _Evolutionary Mismatch Paper_**  
    Proposes that **ADHD distractibility may stem from high trait curiosity**, an evolutionary mismatch in today’s environment. Supports the guide’s framing of distraction as interest-based, not laziness.
    
- **van der Kolk – _The Body Keeps the Score_**  
    Offers a biological explanation: **the thalamus may be compromised**, impairing the brain’s ability to filter sensory input. Validates the ADHD brain’s struggle with **noise and environmental overload**.

## 🧩 Executive Function + Externalization

- **Dawson & Guare – _Smart but Scattered_**  
    Emphasize **working memory limits** and the need to “**offload**” tasks to visual and audible cues. They recommend **checklists, short intervals**, and **frequent breaks**, matching many of the guide’s strategies.
    
- **McCabe – _How to ADHD_**  
    Provides a comprehensive framework:
    
    - “Focus isn’t a verb” → Redirection is the skill
        
    - Use **Idea Parking Lots, breadcrumbs, physical cues**
        
    - Manage **hyperfocus and transitions**
        
    - Design “**point of performance**” environments
        
- **Forte – _Building a Second Brain_**  
    Champion of **externalizing everything**. Promotes the use of digital tools (PARA method, intermediate packets) to reduce friction and make information **interruptible and accessible**.
    
- **Kinzer & Wright – _Unapologetically ADHD_**  
    “You cannot rely on your memory with ADHD.” Encourage **brain dumps**, visual workflows, and **compassionate planning systems** to reduce chaos and friction.
    
- **Paul – _Order from Chaos_**  
    Advocates for **Someday-Maybe lists**, **hyperfocus corrals**, and assuming “you’ll forget everything.” Reinforces curiosity and experimentation over shame.

## 🧘‍♀️ Self-Compassion + Reframing

- **Davis – _How to Keep House While Drowning_**  
    Encourages **gentle self-talk** and viewing distraction with compassion, not criticism. Promotes the **"compassionate observer"** mindset to disrupt shame spirals.
    
- **Solden, Frank, Littman – _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_**  
    Help reframe internalized failure. Encourage untangling strategies from shame, and stress **visual + auditory prompts** for support.
    
- **Kelly & Ramundo – _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?_**  
    Normalize distractibility as a memory-chain issue. Offer **"memory bypass systems"** like color tags, cheat sheets, and fidget strategies for optimal stimulation.

## 🛠 Practical Strategies to Stay on Track

- **Donovan – _The Anti-Planner_**  
    Offers ADHD-specific tactics to reduce distraction, including **adjusting work environment**, using an **Idea Parking Lot**, and **external accountability**.
    
- **Le Cunff – _Tiny Experiments_**  
    Recommends:
    
    - Avoiding **multitasking**
        
    - Adjusting environment (close tabs, use headphones)
        
    - Pairing dull tasks with fun ones (dopamine boosts)
        
    - Using **body doubling** and **curiosity to investigate patterns**
        
- **Alexander-Grout – _Mad About Money_**  
    Describes real-life ADHD distraction (sounds, smells, people) and promotes **friction-based tools** to slow impulsivity.
    
- **Carder – _It All Makes Sense Now_**  
    Recommends an “**idea parking lot**,” **step-by-step cues**, and **plans for overstimulation**. Highlights that distraction often stems from **under-stimulation**.
    
- **Kustow – _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_**  
    Suggests **placing reminders "at point of action"** and using **visual/audio prompts**. Emphasizes tangible externalization over memory reliance.
    
- **Ratey – _The Disorganized Mind_**  
    Advocates for **“objective awareness”** (track patterns, name obstacles), using **notebooks**, **timers**, and “right space” optimization.

## 🤝 Support + Environment

- **Rochester Medical Center – _73 ADHD-Friendly Tips_**  
    Offers simple interventions like **sticky notes** and "**leave breadcrumbs**" advice to help return to tasks.
    
- **Scholz et al. – _Does ADHD Coaching Work?_**  
    Cites distractibility as a target for coaching and supports the guide’s advice to **seek ADHD-informed support**.
    
- **Heath & Heath – _The Power of Moments_**  
    While not ADHD-specific, supports tailoring support environments and **creating rituals** or **transition anchors** to mark task shifts.
    
- **Stanier – _The Coaching Habit_**  
    Emphasizes **reducing choice overload** and task switching to lower distraction.
    
- **University of Pennsylvania study (via _Do Hard Things_)**  
    Observed that **environmental triggers (like iPads)** dramatically reduced children’s ability to focus, reinforcing the need for **environmental control**.
