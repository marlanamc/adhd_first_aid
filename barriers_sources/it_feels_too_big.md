## 📚 Sources: It Feels Too Big

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain why seemingly simple tasks can feel enormous with ADHD — and offer strategies for reducing friction, breaking tasks into micro-steps, managing emotional overwhelm, and honoring your brain’s unique way of processing effort.

---

### 🧠 Understanding Overwhelm & Executive Dysfunction

- **Kelly, K., & Ramundo, P. (2006). _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_.**  
    Addresses the emotional toll of ADHD and emphasizes breaking tasks into small steps when the brain shuts down.
    
- **Dawson, P., & Guare, R. (2015). _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_.**  
    Explains how quickly executive effort gets depleted and suggests offloading, support systems, and task breakdowns.
    
- **Kemp, J., & Mitchelson, M. (2024). _Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_.**  
    Validates overload from boring tasks, internalized shame, and lack of support. Promotes self-acceptance and asking for help as an act of strength.
    
- **Solden, S. (2013). _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_.**  
    Discusses the mental "flooding" that occurs when ADHDers generate too many ideas or obligations. Emphasizes need for support bridges and external structure.
    

---

### 🔍 Reframing & Breaking Down the Task

- **Abdaal, A. (2023). _Feel-Good Productivity_.**  
    Encourages focusing on the “next tiny step” and lowering activation energy. Reinforces the value of micro-action and friction reduction.
    
- **Fogg, B. J. (2019). _Tiny Habits_.**  
    Introduces behavior design rooted in making things so small they’re effortless to begin. Aligns with the guide’s “make it stupidly small” advice.
    
- **McCabe, J. (2024). _How to ADHD_.**  
    Offers a “menu of tools” and validation for invisible obstacles. Suggests scaffolding and barrier logs to externalize overwhelm and find patterns.
    
- **Forte, T. (2022, 2023). _Building a Second Brain_ & _The PARA Method_.**  
    Encourages organizing around active projects and reducing mental load. Promotes “intermediate packets,” flexibility, and starting fresh if needed.
    
- **University of Rochester Medical Center. (n.d.). _73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now_.**  
    Offers a wide range of concrete ADHD-friendly task breakdown and organization techniques that support externalizing overwhelm.
    

---

### 💬 Emotional Weight, Guilt & Shame

- **Davis, K. C. (2022). _How to Keep House While Drowning_.**  
    Centers compassion and redefines guilt as a signal, not a sentence. Encourages starting from a place of self-acceptance, not pressure.
    
- **Lakshmin, P. (2023). _Real Self-Care_.**  
    Encourages gentle boundaries and acknowledges how guilt and external expectations erode motivation. Supports brain dumps and micro-steps.
    
- **Kinzer, N., & Wright, P. D. (2024). _Unapologetically ADHD_.**  
    Describes the ADHD shame spiral and promotes values-aligned action and planning in small time blocks. Challenges perfectionism with “Good Enough is Fantastic.”
    
- **Solden, S., Frank, M., & Littman, E. (2019). _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_.**  
    Validates emotional burnout and teaches self-worth detachment from productivity. Reinforces that strategies follow self-compassion, not the other way around.
    

---

### 🔧 Strategies, Support & Motivation

- **Donovan, D. (2022). _The Anti-Planner_.**  
    Affirms invisible obstacles and offers flexible, trial-based tools. Recommends social support, self-experimentation, and borrowing motivation when solo efforts fall short.
    
- **Le Cunff, A.-L. (2025). _Tiny Experiments_.**  
    Frames procrastination as information and advocates for pairing tasks, coworking, and adapting through “tiny experiments” instead of white-knuckling.
    
- **Kustow, J. (2024). _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_.**  
    Encourages a menu-style approach to strategy, barrier identification, and creative counter-strategies. Reinforces gradual change and support seeking.
    
- **Carder, M. (2024). _It All Makes Sense Now_.**  
    Focuses on overwhelm as a signal of capacity limits. Advocates letting go of commitments and building motivation around “why,” not just “shoulds.”
    
- **Hallowell, E. M., & Ratey, J. J. (2022). _ADHD 2.0_.**  
    Emphasizes harnessing dopamine by making things meaningful. Reinforces the importance of creating structure and motivation that feels good.
    
- **Kegan, R., & Lahey, L. L. (2009). _Immunity to Change_.**  
    Uncovers emotional resistance and “competing commitments” that prevent action. Aligns with guide’s advice to “get underneath the fog” and find what you’re protecting.
    
- **Miller, W. R., & Rollnick, S. (2023). _Motivational Interviewing_.**  
    Centers hope and confidence rather than pressure. Suggests evoking motivation instead of forcing behavior. Reinforces “why first, how later” thinking.
    
- **Paul, J. (2018). _Order from Chaos_.**  
    Encourages path of least resistance and releasing shame. Reinforces that every failure or delay is a data point — not a verdict.
