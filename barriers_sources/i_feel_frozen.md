## 📚 Sources: I Feel Frozen

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how freezing is often a survival response — not laziness — and offer strategies to gently reconnect, reduce cognitive overload, and take the smallest next step.

---

### 🧠 Why Freezing Happens

- **Solden, Sari & Frank, Michelle.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_ – Describes how executive dysfunction and self-protection lead to withdrawal and shutdown. Promotes self-compassion and acceptance over fixing.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ – Frames freeze mode as a form of nervous system overwhelm and offers ADHD-friendly tools to work with — not against — the brain.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_ – Breaks down “cognitive friction” and explains how disconnected or incomplete systems lead to paralysis and internal shutdown.
    
- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_ – Explains how limited executive resources can lead to burnout, indecision, and freezing under pressure.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy.** _The Disorganized Mind_ – Describes “paralysis of the will” and how externalization and structure help ADHDers move out of frozen states.
    

---

### 🧱 Emotional Root Causes

- **Kegan, Robert & Lahey, Lisa.** _Immunity to Change_ – Explores how hidden commitments to safety and fear of failure unconsciously block action.
    
- **Lakshmin, Pooja.** _Real Self-Care_ – Offers a reframe of rest and stillness as powerful self-care. Validates that guilt and shame are often learned, not earned.
    
- **Wiest, Brianna.** _The Mountain Is You_ – Describes freezing as resistance caused by fear and inner conflict, not procrastination or laziness.
    
- **van der Kolk, Bessel.** _The Body Keeps the Score_ – Shows how trauma can trigger dissociation or numbness. Explains why safety and co-regulation are crucial to unfreezing.
    

---

### 🛠️ Strategies That Help

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ – Names the invisible mental wall behind freezing and provides micro-steps and visual reframing tools to get unstuck.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ – Encourages “working with procrastination” by using curiosity, micro-actions, and play instead of pressure.
    
- **Fogg, BJ.** _Tiny Habits_ – Offers a clear, neuroscience-backed framework for building momentum through tiny, low-friction actions.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_ – Focuses on externalizing task steps to reduce overwhelm and regain control. Checklists and templates are key tools.
