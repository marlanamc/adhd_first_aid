## 📚 Sources: Too Many Decisions

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD brains get overwhelmed by too many choices — and offer tools for simplifying, externalizing, and moving forward without perfectionism or paralysis.

---

### 🧠 Why ADHD Brains Struggle With Choices

- **Kemp, J., & Mitchelson, M. (2024). _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_.**  
    Identifies **decision-making as an executive function** often impaired in ADHD, leading to analysis paralysis and burnout. Recommends simplifying decisions and asking for help as self-compassion.
    
- **Kinzer, N., & Wright, P. D. (2024). _Unapologetically ADHD_.**  
    Explains **FOBO (Fear of Better Options)** and the cycle of research-paralysis. Recommends deadlines for decisions, planning systems, and letting go of perfectionism.
    
- **Le Cunff, A.-L. (2025). _Tiny Experiments_.**  
    Reframes indecision as a product of **emotional interference**, not logic. Suggests **broadening decision frames** and experimenting gently instead of waiting for certainty.
    

---

### 🛠️ Simplify Choices & Reduce Friction

- **Donovan, D. (2022). _The Anti-Planner_.**  
    A core source. Identifies decision fatigue, promotes **ruthless simplification**, externalizing, and using tools like **timers or body doubling**. Encourages “**Good enough is good enough.**”
    
- **Bungay Stanier, M. (2016). _The Coaching Habit_.**  
    Shares research that **fewer options = more action**. Recommends chunking choices into fours to avoid overload.
    
- **Carder, M. (2024). _It All Makes Sense Now_.**  
    Describes **decision overload** as a source of chronic emotional fatigue in ADHD. Advises letting go of excess decisions when overstretched.
    
- **Paul, J. (2018). _Order from Chaos_.**  
    Promotes **autopilot systems** and “decide not to decide” strategies. Encourages building systems that reduce on-the-spot decision-making and shame.
    
- **Fogg, B. J. (2019). _Tiny Habits_.**  
    Supports reducing **decision load** by making the easiest choice the default. Encourages designing environments that nudge action without mental strain.
    

---

### 🧰 Organizational Systems for Mental Clarity

- **Forte, T. (2022 & 2023). _Building a Second Brain_ / _The PARA Method_.**  
    Offers a structure to **organize for action** and eliminate decision clutter. Recommends archiving when overwhelmed and focusing on active projects only.
    
- **Gilkey, C. (2019). _Start Finishing_.**  
    Encourages **chunking and sequencing** to reduce choice overload. Says “**undated items are unsigned checks**,” advocating deadlines for clarity.
    
- **Dawson, P., & Guare, R. (2015). _Smart but Scattered_.**  
    Promotes **external tools** like checklists and simple plans to reduce internal decision stress. Recommends informal coaching and reflection for task follow-through.
    

---

### 💬 Emotional Barriers, Guilt, & Self-Compassion

- **McCabe, J. (2024). _How to ADHD_.**  
    Describes **the “Wall of Awful”** and how fear of choosing “wrong” leads to shut down. Advocates for emotional tools and flexible systems over rigid routines.
    
- **Lakshmin, P. (2023). _Real Self-Care_.**  
    Offers a decision framework rooted in **boundaries and emotional regulation**. Encourages readers to pause, negotiate, and not let guilt override needs.
    
- **Levrini, A. (2012). _Succeeding with Adult ADHD_.**  
    Discusses **emotional self-regulation failures** as core to ADHD decision issues. Recommends minimizing planning complexity and using support systems.
    
- **Ratey, N. A. (2008). _The Disorganized Mind_.**  
    Explains how **overstimulation and stuckness** arise when decisions pile up. Recommends keeping written records and enlisting support to offload mental loops.
    
- **Solden, S., Frank, M., & Littman, E. (2019). _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_.**  
    Addresses internal resistance and **redefining “enough”**. Encourages delegation and rejecting the belief that you must do everything alone.
