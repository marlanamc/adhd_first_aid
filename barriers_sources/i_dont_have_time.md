## 📚 Sources: I Don’t Have Time

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of ADHD-related time blindness and executive dysfunction, and offer strategies for making time visible, reducing overwhelm, building structure, and setting boundaries around energy and attention.

---

### 🧠 Understanding ADHD & Time Blindness

- **Kinzer & Wright – _Unapologetically ADHD_**  
    Introduces the concept of “ADHD Time Zone” and explains how time blindness impacts planning. Emphasizes using external tools without shame and zero-based budgeting for time and energy.
    
- **Dawson & Guare – _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_**  
    Discusses how executive skills (like time management) draw from a limited pool of effort. Suggests improving time estimation through structured practice.
    
- **Levrini – _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_**  
    Breaks down how executive dysfunction (e.g., planning, inhibition) affects follow-through and contributes to perceived time scarcity.
    
- **McCabe – _How to ADHD_**  
    Defines time blindness and connects focus, energy, and task initiation. Encourages strategies to recognize and manage internal clocks.
    
- **Kelly & Ramundo – _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_**  
    Describes how ADHD brains struggle with time perception and recommends doubling task estimates to account for distractions.
    
- **Solden, Frank & Littman – _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_**  
    Explores how time and task management struggles affect self-worth and contribute to chronic overwhelm.
    
- **“One Weird Trick to Quit Stupid, Timewasting Content”**  
    Frames time blindness not as failure, but as information for building smarter workarounds.

### ⏳ Making Time Visible & Managing Tasks

- **Kinzer & Wright – _Unapologetically ADHD_**  
    Offers tools like time blocking, buffer time, alarms, weekly focus reviews, daily detours, and "go micro" steps. Encourages breaking tasks into actions and planning around actual energy levels.
    
- **Donovan – _The Anti-Planner_**  
    Provides low-pressure tools like the 5-minute rule, brain dumps, kanban boards, mini-deadlines, and accountability buddies.
    
- **Gilkey – _Start Finishing_**  
    Teaches project pyramids and weekly block scheduling. Emphasizes setting boundaries, honoring capacity, and reducing “other people’s priorities”.
    
- **Abdaal – _Feel-Good Productivity_**  
    Advocates for "time pillars", reducing distractions, and scheduling intentional breaks.
    
- **University of Rochester – _73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now_**  
    Recommends brain dumps, using “leave time” reminders, and simplifying calendar tools.
    
- **Forte – _Building a Second Brain_**  
    Emphasizes organizing for action, not storage. Offers methods like progressive summarization, stigmergy, and dialing down scope when time is tight.
    
- **Le Cunff – _Tiny Experiments_**  
    Reframes procrastination as a cue, not a flaw. Encourages mindful productivity and reducing multitasking to preserve working memory.
    
- **Paul – _Order from Chaos_**  
    Warns against wishful calendar planning and promotes weekly reviews, time logs, and protecting “sacred time”.
    
- **Fogg – _Tiny Habits_**  
    Teaches how to make behavior easier by reducing the time and energy required.
    
- **Kustow – _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_**  
    Introduces block diaries, 30-minute task bursts, and practical tools to reduce procrastination and make time feel more tangible.
    
- **McCabe – _How to ADHD_**  
    Connects time management to energy management. Offers cue-based planning, barrier logs, and practical examples of adjusting expectations.
    
- **Ratey – _The Disorganized Mind_**  
    Recommends planning around natural energy rhythms, creating written records of obstacles, and preparing for the next workday.
    
- **Kegan & Lahey – _Immunity to Change_**  
    Encourages separating what’s within your control and proactively communicating time needs.
    
- **Miller & Rollnick – _Motivational Interviewing_**  
    Discusses how feeling rushed increases inefficiency and encourages slowing down under pressure.
    
- **Solden – _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_**  
    Suggests eliminating automatic overcommitments and building systems to prevent energy and time drain.

### 🫂 Support & Self-Compassion

- **Kinzer & Wright – _Unapologetically ADHD_**  
    Reframes needing tools as smart, not shameful. Encourages coaching, accountability systems, and time-bound experiments.
    
- **McCabe – _How to ADHD_**  
    Offers flexible "menus" of tools and "permission slips" to stop forcing broken methods. Emphasizes body doubling and help prioritizing.
    
- **Davis – _How to Keep House While Drowning_**  
    Gives permission to not feel guilty about what isn’t done. Focuses on shame, rest, and customizing strategies.
    
- **Kemp & Mitchelson – _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_**  
    Challenges unrealistic time expectations. Encourages asking for help and redefining what counts as “productive”.
    
- **Lakshmin – _Real Self-Care_**  
    Helps identify boundaries and say no without guilt. Emphasizes rest as resistance and self-care as empowerment.
    
- **Kelly & Ramundo – _You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_**  
    Normalizes ADHD coaching, and encourages owning your limits and practicing saying no with confidence.
