## 😞 ADHD First Aid Template: Guilty
 It’s not proof you’re a bad person • It’s not failure • It’s ADHD meeting missed intentions, emotional overwhelm, and a brain that forgets—but cares deeply.

Guilt hits hard when your actions don’t match your values. You meant to text back. You meant to show up. You meant to finish. But ADHD throws roadblocks between intention and follow-through — and when the dust settles, guilt is waiting. It makes you question your worth, your care, your character.

You’re not heartless. You’re human. You’re neurodivergent. You’re trying.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Guilt means you care — and that’s good. But holding on to guilt too long just keeps you stuck. You made a mistake? Apologize, repair if you can, and move forward. You’re still allowed to try again.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You’re sitting in guilt like it’s a punishment that’ll fix everything. It won’t. Take responsibility. Make the repair. Then let it go. You’re not helping anyone by staying stuck in shame.

### 🧠 Why ADHD Creates Guilt Loops:

- ⏳ Time blindness causes you to miss deadlines, appointments, or moments that matter
    
- 🧠 Working memory gaps make it easy to forget tasks and hard to remember _why_ you forgot them
    
- 📉 Emotional dysregulation makes even small slip-ups feel unbearable
    
- 😞 People-pleasing + inconsistency = shame spiral
    
- 🤐 Avoidance fueled by guilt only creates more guilt
---

### 1. 🔍 Recognize Guilt as a Signal, Not a Sentence

Guilt isn’t a moral failure — it’s your body saying “something feels off.” Let’s decode it instead of internalizing it.

**Try this:**

- 🧠 **Name it to tame it**: “This is guilt. It’s uncomfortable, but it’s just a feeling.”
    
- 🩺 **Notice where it lives**: Heavy chest? Tight stomach? Nausea? That’s data, not danger.
    
- 📣 **Ask**: “What am I believing right now — and who told me that?”
    
- 📼 **Trace old tapes**: Societal expectations, family messages, and “shoulds” often shape guilt.
    

> 💡 Tip: You’re not guilty for needing help, doing things differently, or being human.

---

### 2. 🔄 Reframe the Thought Loop

Guilt feeds on shame and perfectionism. Let’s rewrite the mental scripts with more flexibility and self-kindness.

**Try this:**

- 💬 Add “I’m having the thought that…” before your guilty belief — it helps you create distance.
    
- 🧩 Challenge “should” statements: “I should be more organized” → “I’m still learning how to manage this.”
    
- 🧘 Practice a **Good Enough mindset**: Progress matters more than perfection.
    
- 🤔 Ask: “Is this thought helpful? Or just familiar?”
    

> 💡 Tip: Your brain may confuse guilt with responsibility. They’re not the same.

---

### 3. 🛠 Take Gentle Action to Rebuild Trust With Yourself

Guilt shrinks when you respond with clarity and intention — not punishment.

**Try this:**

- 🪜 **Find the “why”**: “What’s really bothering me underneath this guilt?”
    
- ✍️ **Brain dump** to externalize the weight — even if it’s messy
    
- 📦 Break tasks into “micro-steps” you can actually do (imperfect > idealized)
    
- ✅ Celebrate tiny wins — “I emailed one person,” “I drank water,” “I showed up”
    

> 💡 Tip: Guilt often vanishes the moment you take one empowered step forward.

---

### 4. 🧱 Set Boundaries to Stop the Spiral

Much of ADHD guilt comes from overextending. Protecting your peace is a form of prevention.

**Try this:**

- 🧾 Make a list of your current commitments — cross off what isn’t yours to carry
    
- ✋ Practice saying “no” or “not right now” — without a 10-minute apology
    
- 🎯 Reframe rest: It’s not something to “earn,” it’s a right that protects your mental health
    
- 🧠 Remind yourself: _Other people’s disappointment isn’t your failure_
    

> 💡 Tip: Sometimes the kindest thing you can do is disappoint someone _else_ to protect _yourself_.

---

### 5. 🤝 Reach Out & Build Compassion Loops

Guilt shrinks in safe spaces. You don’t have to carry it alone.

**Try this:**

- 🧍‍♀️ Say your guilt out loud to someone safe: “I feel bad because…”
    
- 🙋 Ask for help or co-regulation — you deserve support, not isolation
    
- 🧠 Work with a therapist or ADHD coach to rewire deep-rooted guilt stories
    
- 💬 Speak to yourself as you would to a dear friend: “You didn’t mean harm. You’re learning.”
    

> 💡 Tip: You’re not behind. You’re healing in real time. That counts.
