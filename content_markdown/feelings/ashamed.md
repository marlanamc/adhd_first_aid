## 😔 ADHD First Aid Template: Ashamed
 It’s not a character flaw • It’s not proof you’re broken • It’s ADHD meeting impossible standards, rejection, and years of internalized blame.

Shame hits hard when you feel like you’ve let someone down — especially yourself. For many with ADHD, shame is a chronic background noise. It’s built over years of missed deadlines, forgotten birthdays, broken promises, and feeling "too much" or "not enough" all at once. Even small mistakes can trigger a deep spiral, making it hard to move forward.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Shame says “you are the problem” — but that’s a lie. You’re not broken. You’re human. Everyone struggles. Everyone slips. Talk to yourself the way you would a friend. You deserve compassion, not punishment.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
Shame grows in silence. It’s time to drag it into the light. You made a mistake? Own it. Learn. Fix it if you can. But do not let shame stop your life. You're better than the worst thing you've done.

### 🧠 Why ADHD Fuels Shame:

- ⏰ Chronic lateness, disorganization, and inconsistency can strain relationships and self-trust
    
- 🧠 Working memory struggles make you forget things that matter — and blame yourself after
    
- 😔 Rejection Sensitivity (RSD) makes every misstep feel like you’re a terrible person
    
- 🔁 All-or-nothing thinking turns small slip-ups into proof that you’re a failure
    
- 🪞 Masking for too long can leave you feeling like the real you isn’t acceptable

---

### 1. 🕵️‍♀️ Spot the Shame & Trace Its Roots

Shame often hides in silence. Step one is noticing it — and gently asking where it came from.

**Try this:**

- 🧠 **Ask yourself**: “What story am I telling myself right now?”
    
- 🚩 **Watch for toxic “ADHD lies”**: “I should be able to handle this.” “I’m too much.” “I’m not enough.”
    
- 🗣 **Reflect on past messages**:
    
    - “<font color="#17365d">She Messages</font>” (unrealistic expectations about how you _should_ be)
        
    - “Duh! Messages” (“Just try harder!” “Everyone else can do it.”)
        
    - Covert absorbed messages (seeing yourself through the neurotypical lens)
        
- 💭 **Recall the first time you felt this way**: Is this your shame — or someone else’s voice?
    

> 💡 Tip: Shame thrives in silence. Naming it starts your freedom.

---

### 2. 💗 Talk to Yourself Like Someone You Love

You won’t shame yourself into being “better” — but you _can_ compassion yourself into healing.

**Try this:**

- 🧘 **Practice “good enough”**: You don’t need to be perfect to be worthy
    
- 🔁 **Reframe “shoulds”**: Try “I’m learning how to…” instead of “I should already…”
    
- 💬 **Use defusion**: Say “I’m having the thought that I’m a failure” — not “I am”
    
- 💔 **Forgive yourself**: For the mess, the meltdown, the missed deadline
    
- 🪞 **Ask: Would I say this to someone I care about?** If not — change the tone
    

> 💡 Tip: Self-compassion isn’t weakness. It’s recovery fuel.

---

### 3. 🌬 Release Shame from the Body

Shame isn’t just emotional — it’s physical. Let’s move it through your system.

**Try this:**

- 😭 **Let it move**: Cry, shake, stomp, stretch — discharge what’s stuck
    
- 🖐 **Name what you feel**: “Hot face, heavy chest, tight throat” — then: “That’s shame”
    
- 🤲 **Soothing sensory inputs**: Weighted blanket, warm tea, dim lighting
    
- 🚶 **Gentle movement**: Swaying, walking, humming, or rocking
    
- 🧘‍♀️ **Grounding mantra**: “This feeling is allowed. And it will pass.”
    

> 💡 Tip: You don’t need to “get over it.” You need to move _through_ it.

---

### 4. 🧱 Reclaim Control & Lower the Pressure

Shame often arrives when we feel powerless, behind, or broken. Let’s restore a sense of agency.

**Try this:**

- 🧾 **Brain dump**: Clear the chaos — no filter, no format
    
- 🪜 **Break it down**: One micro-step. That’s it. Then one more.
    
- ✋ **Say “no” to shame-fueled overcommitting**
    
- 🧮 **Do less to unlock more**: Rest is a right, not a reward
    
- 🧠 **Refocus on what you can control**: One action, one conversation, one reset
    

> 💡 Tip: Burnout often leads to shame. Rest and boundaries are the antidotes.

---

### 5. 🤝 Speak It Out Loud & Let Others In

Shame loses power when it’s shared with safe people. You don’t have to do this alone.

**Try this:**

- 📞 Tell someone: “Hey, I’m feeling really ashamed and could use a little grounding”
    
- 🧍 Body double with someone who won’t judge you
    
- 🧠 Talk to a therapist or ADHD coach — especially if shame feels rooted in trauma
    
- 🤝 Join a support group or connect with other neurodivergent people
    
- 🛑 Set boundaries with love: “I can’t take this on right now.” “I need space.” “I’m doing my best.”
    

> 💡 Tip: You are not your worst moment. You are worthy of love, even in your shame.
