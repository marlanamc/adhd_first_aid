## 🧷 ADHD First Aid Template: Tense
It’s not just “in your head” • It’s not overreacting • It’s ADHD meeting chronic stress, unprocessed emotion, and a nervous system stuck in defense mode.

Tension in your body is often the first sign that something’s off — and with ADHD, you might not even notice until you're wound tight. Your shoulders are up, your jaw is clenched, your stomach’s in knots — and you’re still telling yourself to “just focus.” Tension is your brain’s way of bracing for impact, especially when life feels unpredictable.

You’re not broken. You’re carrying more than you realize — and your body is trying to hold it all together.

It’s not a flow state — it’s a flood state.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Your body remembers what your brain tries to power through. Tension is unprocessed stress. You don’t have to fix it — just _feel_ it. Stretch. Move. Breathe into the tight places. You deserve relief.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You’re walking around like a clenched fist. That tension? It’s talking to you — and you’re ignoring it. Stretch. Exhale. Let it go before it locks in. Your body isn’t a stress dump — treat it better.

### 🧠 Why ADHD Leads to Physical and Mental Tension:

- ⚙️ Constant low-level stress from task management and life logistics
    
- 🧠 Poor interoception makes it hard to notice and respond to physical stress signals
    
- 🔁 Emotional suppression and masking build unprocessed pressure
    
- 😰 Anticipatory anxiety and rejection fears keep your nervous system “on”
    
- 🪨 Hypervigilance from past failures or trauma keeps your body in armor mode
---

### 1. 🧠 Notice and Name the Tension

Tension is the body’s way of gripping when it feels unsafe or overstimulated.

**Try this:**

- 👂 Scan for signals: clenching jaw, twisted stomach, tight shoulders, irritability for “no reason”
    
- 🧾 Name it out loud or write it down: “It feels like I’m vibrating on the inside” or “My chest feels like it’s in a vice”
    
- 📝 Externalize: brain dump, voice memo, or tell someone — it helps move the energy out
    

> 💡 Tip: Separate the physical from the emotional. “Tight chest” ≠ “I’m in danger.” It might just mean “I need to breathe.”

---

### 2. 🤸 Move to Discharge the Static

You can’t relax your way out of high energy — you have to move through it.

**Try this:**

- 🚶 Go for a brisk walk
    
- 🧘 Shake out your limbs or stretch long
    
- 🦶 Stomp your feet like a toddler or hug your knees in tight then release
    
- 🌀 Use fidget tools, swivel chairs, or stimming to redirect energy
    

> 💡 Tip: Even 60 seconds of movement can switch your brain’s channel.

---

### 3. 🌿 Soothe Your Senses and Nervous System

Tension builds when your environment overwhelms your inner calm.

**Try this:**

- 🌬️ Breathe: in for 4, out for 8. Humming or chanting helps too
    
- 🪨 Grounding: 5 things you see, 4 feel, 3 hear, 2 smell, 1 taste
    
- 🎧 Noise-canceling headphones or calming music
    
- 🛁 Weighted blanket, warm shower, tight cozy clothes
    

> 💡 Tip: Create a "low-stimulation zone" — dim lighting, minimal noise, soft textures.

---

### 4. 🧩 Calm the Thoughts That Tighten You

Your brain might be running a tight-loop script that’s keeping your body clenched.

**Try this:**

- 💬 Reframe: “I’m feeling pressure” → “I’m getting information from my body”
    
- 🎛️ Turn down the dial: replace “I can’t do this” with “I can take one step”
    
- ✍️ Simplify tasks into the tiniest possible start
    
- 🧭 Ask: What’s _in_ my control right now?
    

> 💡 Tip: Clarity reduces tension. Overwhelm loves vagueness — give it structure.

---

### 5. 🌱 Reach Out, Set Limits, and Let Go

Tension thrives in isolation and over-responsibility. You’re allowed to pause.

**Try this:**

- 🫂 Call or text someone safe and say, “I’m feeling really tense — can you sit with me for a sec?”
    
- 🚫 Say “no” or “not now” to things adding more weight
    
- 🪂 Use your parachutes: pre-planned exits, phrases, or safe spaces
    
- 💤 Rest as a right — not a reward
    

> 💡 Tip: Rest doesn’t have to be productive. It can be stillness, softness, or simply “off.”

---

### 🔁 Starting Point Suggestions

→ Sit in a dim room, put a heavy pillow on your chest, and hum for 30 seconds  
→ Do a wall sit for 10 seconds, then shake your arms and legs  
→ Label the sensation out loud: “This is tension. It’s okay. It will pass.”
