## 📱 ADHD & Social Media

What starts as checking one thing becomes hours lost in a scroll. ADHD brains are wired for **novelty**, and social platforms deliver it in endless waves,  pulling you into **dopamine loops**, **comparison spirals**, or complete **dissociation**. It’s not a lack of willpower,  it’s a system designed to hijack attention.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Social media gives you stimulation, connection, and escape,  especially when you’re lonely or overwhelmed. That’s real. But too much of it turns inward,  and hurts more than it helps. Start by limiting when and how you use it. Your brain deserves a cleaner input.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
Scroll. Spiral. Shame. Repeat. That’s the loop you’re stuck in,  and it’s stealing your time, your self-worth, and your focus. This isn’t just a bad habit. It’s a system designed to keep you addicted and empty. **Delete the app.** All of them, if you can. Your brain deserves peace, not performance. Reclaim your life.


### 🧠 Why Social Media Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
	- Scroll for hours and forget why you picked up your phone
	- Feel drained or overstimulated after opening your apps
	- Compare yourself to others and spiral into self-doubt
	- Overshare or feel regret after posting impulsively
	- Lose track of time and delay tasks or bedtime
- Here’s what’s really going on:
	- 🎯 **Dopamine-seeking:** ADHD brains crave stimulation, social media delivers it fast and endlessly
	- ⚡ **Impulsivity** makes it hard to pause before clicking, posting, or reacting
	- ⏳ **Time blindness** distorts how long you’ve been scrolling
	- 🧠 **Working memory lapses** cause you to forget why you opened the app
	- 🌪️ **Emotional dysregulation** turns comparisons into spirals
	- 🔊 **Sensory overload** from nonstop input tires out your nervous system


### ✅ ADHD-Friendly Strategies for Navigating Social Media

### 1. **Create Boundaries That Work With Your Brain**

- ⛔ **Delete or Limit Apps**: Remove apps from your phone or log in only on a browser
- 📵 **Set Screen-Free Zones**: No phones in bed or during meals
- ⏱️ **Use Timers or App Limits**: Try one Pomodoro of focused work before a scroll break
- 🔕 **Turn Off Notifications**: Keep your focus without constant interruption

### 2. **Curate Your Feed With Intention**

- 💖 **Follow What Feeds You**: Uplifting, validating, ADHD-positive content
- 🧹 **Unfollow What Drains You**: Anything that triggers shame, comparison, or doomscrolling
- 🎯 **Ask: “Why am I opening this?”** If it’s boredom or escape, redirect with a feel-good alternative (music, walk, fidget)

### 3. **Interrupt the Spiral**

- 📝 **Brain Dump Before Scroll**: If you’re overwhelmed, jot it down before reaching for your phone
- 🐢 **Slow the Scroll**: Scroll slowly and deliberately instead of chasing the next dopamine hit
- 🤔 **Practice Pause**: Before you post, comment, or buy, give yourself 10 seconds to check in with your intention

### 4. **Support Emotional Regulation**

- 🌿 **Start Mornings Without Your Feed**: Give your brain space before exposing it to the outside world
- 💬 **Process Comparison Thoughts**: “Their success doesn’t take away from mine”
- 🙏 **Try Grounding Practices**: Breathwork, stretching, or sensory resets can re-center you after overstimulation

### 5. **Build In Accountability and Support**

- 🧍 **Body Double While Browsing**: Be on FaceTime or co-working apps when managing social or tech tasks
- 📚 **Use a Social Media Planner**: If you post for work or fun, batch your content ahead to reduce impulsive posting
- 🤝 **Connect With ADHD Communities**: You’re not alone, others are navigating the same dopamine loops


### ✨ Encouragement to Take With You
- If you closed the app on purpose, win.
- If you scrolled for 10 minutes instead of 3 hours, win.
> You’re not lazy, you have a brain wired for stimulation and connection. The fact that you’re thinking critically about how you engage with tech already puts you on a path toward healthier habits.
