## 📆 Double-Booking Yourself

You didn’t mean to,  you just **forgot to check**, **misread the calendar**, or **said yes too fast**. ADHD makes time slippery and working memory unreliable. Double-booking isn’t carelessness,  it’s a mismatch between **internal time perception** and external systems.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not flaky, you're time blind. Your brain isn't naturally built for tracking what's on the calendar. **Externalize it**. **Sync everything**. You're not a bad person for forgetting, just overloaded.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
**Get a calendar system and stick to it**. You don't get to keep saying "oops." **Every double-book is a crack in your reputation**. **Respect your time**. **Respect other people's**.


### 🧠 Why ADHD Makes This Worse

- You might:
  - Say "yes" before checking your calendar
  - Think you'll remember… and forget immediately
  - Miss overlap because your brain sees time as blocks, not a flowing map
  - Take on too much to avoid letting people down
  - Feel ashamed and spiral when you realize your mistake

- Here's what's really going on:
  - ⏳ **Time blindness** makes it hard to track past, present, and future commitments  
  - 🧠 **Working memory** drops info fast, what you scheduled 10 minutes ago might be gone  
  - ⚡ **Impulsivity** leads to quick yeses without checking the calendar  
  - 😬 **RSD and people-pleasing** make it hard to say no or push back  
  - 📅 **Executive dysfunction** makes managing complex, overlapping tasks overwhelming  
  - 📈 **Overwhelm** and avoidance mean you forget to check the system that's supposed to help you


### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Scheduling

- **One central calendar**: All commitments in one place, synced everywhere  
- **Check before you commit**: Pause phrases buy you time to verify availability  
- **Include buffer time**: Account for prep, travel, and transition time  
- **External systems beat memory**: Your brain can't reliably track overlapping commitments

### 🛠️ Strategies for Double-Booking Prevention

#### 📲 Build a Calendar System You’ll Actually Use

- **Pick ONE place** to track everything,  digital calendar, paper planner, or combo (but synced)
    
- **Make it visible**: Use widgets, open tabs, or keep it on your desk
    
- **Set a calendar check ritual**,  morning, after lunch, and night
    
- **Add events immediately**,  if it’s not in the calendar, it doesn’t exist
    
- **Include travel and buffer time** so your calendar reflects real capacity
    

#### 🧩 Externalize Your Brain

- **Use visual reminders**: sticky notes, whiteboards, post-it “flags” on your laptop
    
- **Set smart alarms**: reminders a day before and 1 hour before can reduce surprises
    
- **Color code types of events**: personal, work, fun,  your brain needs structure at a glance
    
- **Do brain dumps often**: get everything floating around out of your head and onto paper or a doc
    

#### 🛑 Prevent Impulsive Yeses

- **Use pause phrases** like “Let me check my calendar and get back to you”
    
- **Create a decision delay** rule: No immediate yeses unless you’re 100% sure
    
- **Evaluate the cost** of every new commitment,  time, energy, stress
    
- **Say no gently but firmly**: “I’d love to, but I’m at capacity right now” is enough
    

#### 🔄 Reduce Friction and Simplify

- **Overestimate time**: if you think it’ll take 30 minutes, block 45
    
- **Stack commitments with location or energy in mind**,  don’t book a Zoom back-to-back with in-person events
    
- **Automate** recurring tasks, bills, or reminders to free up brain space
    
- **Declutter your schedule**,  having “too many balls in the air” leads to dropping some
    

#### 🧠 Use Tech Strategically

- **Shared calendars** (with partners, roommates, bosses) reduce communication breakdowns
    
- **AI tools** like ChatGPT can help create weekly plans or conflict-check appointments
    
- **Try apps** like Notion, Google Calendar, Sunsama, or Fantastical if you need structure + beauty
    
- **Use scheduling links** to avoid back-and-forths,  Calendly or SavvyCal can save you time and prevent overlap
    

#### 🤝 Build in Support

- **Ask for accountability**,  have a friend or partner do weekly schedule reviews with you
    
- **Work with an ADHD coach** to structure your system and recover from shame cycles
    
- **Use body doubling** when entering commitments into your calendar,  it’s easier with someone else present
    
- **Join ADHD communities** to hear how others manage calendar chaos and gain new ideas
    


### 💥 What To Do _When_ It Happens

Even with systems, it’ll happen. Your job isn’t to be perfect,  it’s to respond well.

- **Tell the truth quickly**,  don’t ghost or lie. A short, honest message with an apology and new options is enough
    
- **Forgive yourself**,  this is part of your brain wiring, not a moral failure
    
- **Treat it like a learning moment**,  ask: what could’ve helped you catch the conflict sooner?
    
- **Update your system**,  add new reminders or calendar colors if something keeps slipping through
    


### 🧭 Double-Booking ≠ Carelessness

- Double-booking isn't about carelessness, it's about capacity. ADHD brains need external systems.  
- You're not irresponsible. You just need a map, and maybe a reminder that it's okay to have limits.  
- Your worth isn't measured by perfect scheduling.
> You're building time management skills that work with your brain, not against it.

### ✨ Encouragement to Take With You
- If you checked your calendar before saying yes, that's a win.  
- If you added buffer time to your schedule, that's a win.  
- If you communicated quickly when a conflict arose, that's a win.  
- Progress over perfection, every system improvement counts.
> You're not behind. You're learning to manage time differently. That's real progress.
