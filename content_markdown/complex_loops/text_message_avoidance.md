## 💬 Replying to Texts

That message has been sitting there for days, not because you don't care, but because **responding feels weirdly hard**. With ADHD, texts can trigger **overthinking**, **forgetfulness**, or even **shame spirals**. You're not being a bad friend, you're stuck in a silent cycle of pressure and paralysis.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Your brain might see **"reply" as a multi-step social task**, not just a tap. That's **real**. **RSD, working memory, and anxiety** all play a role. Try keeping replies **simple and low-pressure**. **A late response is better than none**. You still **deserve connection**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
**Enough rereading**. **Enough guilt**. They texted you. **Reply**. You don't need a **paragraph**. You don't need **perfection**. **Ghosting people you care about** isn't harmless, it's **avoidance dressed as politeness**.

### 🧠 Why Replying to Texts Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - See a message, mentally draft a reply, then forget to send it
  - Feel frozen when someone sends a long or emotional message
  - Mean to respond later and then forget entirely
  - Feel guilt or shame about how long it's been since you replied
  - Want to say something thoughtful but can't find the words

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 📝 **Working memory issues** make replies vanish from your mental to-do list  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** causes you to lose track of how long it's been  
  - 💡 **Task initiation struggles** make it hard to start a reply even if you know what to say  
  - 📵 **Low dopamine** makes texting feel like a heavy lift  
  - 🤯 **Message backlog** or intense emotions trigger shutdown or avoidance  
  - 😔 **Shame and perfectionism** delay or derail your response further

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Texting

- **Connection over perfection**: A short reply or emoji is still communication  
- **Radical acceptance**: You're not careless, your brain works differently  
- **External systems**: Don't rely on memory alone for text responses  
- **Progress over perfection**: Late responses are better than no responses

### 🛠️ Strategies for Text Replies

#### 🧺 Externalize & Simplify

- **Touch It Once**: If you can reply in under 2 minutes, do it right then
- **Quick Capture**: Can't reply now? Write a sticky note, voice memo, or create a task in your to-do app
- **Micro-Templates**: Save drafts like "Hey! Just saw this, sending love. Will reply properly later" or "Thinking of you! Catch up soon?"
- **Use Emojis/Reactions**: A quick ❤️ or 😂 still shows you care

#### ⏰ Make Time for Texts

- **Schedule "Reply Time"**: Choose a daily or weekly moment to catch up (after work, while eating, on the bus)
- **Habit Stack**: Add it to another routine: "After breakfast, I check my inbox and texts"
- **Timers**: Set a 15-minute timer and reply to as many as you can. Then stop

#### 📱 Tools & Tech

- **Use Reminders**: "Text Sam back at 8 PM"
- **Delay Send Apps**: Schedule messages to go out when you think of them
- **Task Managers**: Add social check-ins to your to-do list (e.g., "Reply to Kim")
- **Organize Messages**: Archive unimportant chats so your inbox feels less overwhelming

#### 🧠 Energy & Motivation

- **Reward Yourself**: After a reply session, give yourself a small treat
- **Gamify It**: How many texts can you send in 10 minutes?
- **Honor Energy Levels**: Don't try to reply when drained, come back when you've recharged
- **Minimum Viable Reply**: A one-line check-in is better than ghosting
- **Track Your Patterns**: Notice what times/days you're best at replying and use that info

#### 🧍 Boundaries & Communication

- **Be Honest**: Tell friends, "I sometimes take a while to reply, but I really care about you"
- **Set Expectations**: "I check messages once a day" or "I don't text much during the workday"
- **Use "I" Statements**: "I get overwhelmed when I have too many convos at once"
- **Say No Kindly**: "I'm not up for hanging out this weekend, but I'd love to catch up next week"
- **Ask for Written Communication**: If verbal chats are hard, request texts or emails

### 🧭 Texting ≠ Friendship

- You're not a bad friend for delayed responses
- Communication struggles are neurological, not personal failures

> Every emoji, delayed reply, or short message counts. You are doing enough.

✨ Encouragement to Take With You
- Send one simple reply today, even if it's just an emoji
- Set one reminder to check texts at a specific time
- Remember that people who care about you understand
- Your worth isn't measured by response speed
