## 📧 Writing Emails

Emails demand **clarity**, **tone-checking**, and **remembering to hit send**,  all things that ADHD brains can **overthink**, **delay**, or **avoid entirely**. You might start one and never finish. Or reread it 12 times and still feel unsure. It's not just writing,  it's a **high-friction task** disguised as a quick one.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Emails feel like **high-pressure social puzzles**. What do I say? How do I say it? That **freeze** is real. Start with a **template**. Keep it **short**. You're allowed to send **imperfect messages**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
It's just an **email**,  not a **thesis**. Stop **overthinking**. **Open it**. **Type it**. **Send it**. You're not being **thoughtful** by stalling,  you're just **avoiding**.

### 🧠 Why Writing Emails Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Open an email, panic, and close it again
  - Write a response,  and forget to send it
  - Feel totally unsure how to start or what tone to use
  - Procrastinate because the task feels too "small to justify how hard it is"
  - Rewrite the same message over and over

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes it hard to initiate, organize, and complete tasks
  - ⏰ **Email isn't one task**,  it's reading, thinking, writing, editing, and clicking send
  - 💥 **Perfectionism and rejection sensitivity** turn messages into emotional landmines
  - 🔁 **Communication anxiety** makes tone and structure feel impossibly high-stakes
  - 🧠 **Working memory challenges** cause you to lose track of what you were trying to say

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Emailing

- **Progress over polish**: A clear, kind email beats a perfect one that's never sent
- **Templates are tools, not cheats**: You don't have to write from scratch
- **Short is okay**: Most people prefer clear over clever
- **Externalize the steps**: Don't trust memory,  jot down what you need to say
- **Fast send beats guilt**: Avoidance adds pressure

### 🛠️ Strategies for Writing Emails

#### 🛠 Break It Down

- **Chunk the task**: Open inbox → Identify what needs replying → Write rough response → Reread once → Hit send
- **2-Minute Rule**: If it'll take under 2 minutes, just reply now
- **Write first, format later**: Start with a brain dump, worry about punctuation after

#### 🧠 Externalize Your Thoughts

- **Use templates**: Save drafts for common replies like "Thanks for your patience…"
- **Sticky note or checklist**: Jot down what you need to say before opening the draft window
- **Talk it out first**: Verbally rehearse to clarify what you want to say

#### ⏰ Time & Energy Hacks

- **Batch email time**: Set "Admin Blocks" during low-energy windows for multiple replies
- **Use timers**: 10-15 minute timer to clear inbox without spiraling
- **Reward yourself**: "After I send this email, I get a snack/scroll break"

#### 💡 Communication Tips

- **Lead with the ask**: "Can you send me the file by Friday?" up top
- **Keep it short**: 3 sentences is enough, say "Let's chat live" if more needed
- **Add clarity, not fluff**: Kind, simple, and clear beats clever
- **End with a nudge**: "No rush, but a reply by [day] helps me plan"

#### ⚙️ Tech That Helps

- **Gmail/Outlook templates**: Prewrite responses you can reuse and edit quickly
- **Email schedulers**: Write when you're in the zone, send when appropriate
- **AI assist**: Tools like Grammarly or ChatGPT can help rewrite or tone-check
- **Delay send**: Schedule emails to go out in 5 mins for unsend window

#### 👯 Support Systems

- **Body doubling**: Reply to emails with a friend nearby or on video
- **Accountability check-ins**: "Hey, I'll send that email in 10 mins,  ask me if I did"
- **Use scripts when stuck**: Save "I'm following up…" or "I'm running behind but wanted to reply" blurbs

### 🧭 Emails ≠ Essays

- You're not being graded,  you're communicating
- Most people skim, not scrutinize
- An imperfect message sent on time beats a perfect one in Drafts forever
- Your words don't have to be perfect,  just kind, clear, and sent

> If you opened your inbox, that's a win.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> If you typed a draft, that's progress. If you sent something that felt scary,  that's success. One email at a time. One reply at a time. You've got this.
