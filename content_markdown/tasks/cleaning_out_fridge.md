## 🧊 Cleaning Out the Fridge

Cleaning out the fridge with ADHD isn’t just a chore,  it’s **overwhelming**, **visually chaotic**, and full of **executive function traps**. You’re hit with **decisions**, **time pressure**, and sometimes **shame** over waste. It’s not laziness,  it’s your brain freezing when too many sensory and emotional triggers collide at once.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not **gross**,  you're **overwhelmed**. **ADHD** makes **time** and **tasks** slippery, and it's easy to **forget** what's hiding behind the **takeout container**. But hear this: **you deserve a clean, functional, and sanitary space.** That **expired yogurt** isn't a **moral failure**. **Toss it**. **Wipe one shelf**. You don't have to **do it all**,  just start **claiming your space back**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
Your **fridge** is not a **storage unit** for **guilt** and **rot**. **Old food** isn't a **"maybe someday"**,  it's **bacteria**, **mold**, and **wasted energy**. **Trash the science experiments**. Don't **mourn** the **slimy veggies**. **A clean fridge is not a luxury,  it's your right.**

### 🧠 Why This Task Is So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Keep avoiding the fridge altogether because it’s “too far gone”  
  - Feel ashamed of food waste, and avoid facing it  
  - Open the door, shut it immediately, and walk away  
  - Forget what’s in there until the smell forces you to act  

- What’s really going on:

  - ⏰ **Time blindness** means “that expired yogurt” is now 3 months old  
  - � � **Sensory overload** kicks in fast: sights, smells, textures, and guilt  
  - � � **Shame** creates avoidance, which creates more mess  
  - � � **Decision fatigue** hits hard when sorting, organizing, and tossing  

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Fridge Cleaning

- **Progress > perfection**: You don’t need a sparkling fridge,  just a usable one  
- **Externalize the process**: Don’t keep it all in your head,  use timers, checklists, and visuals  
- **One shelf counts**: You don’t have to clean it all today  
- **Use movement, music, and novelty**: Dopamine fuels momentum  
- **Make it easier to reset**: Don’t organize like a Pinterest kitchen,  organize for function  

### 🛠️ Strategies for Cleaning the Fridge

#### 🧽 Set Up Your Reset

- **Gather supplies first**: Trash bags, paper towels, natural cleaner, gloves if needed 
- **Put on something fun**: Music, podcast, or timer,  whatever keeps you from zoning out  
- **Before photo (optional)**: This gives your brain a clear dopamine hit when you're done  
- **Start with 15 minutes**: That’s it. One shelf is a win  

#### 🗑️ Toss First, Don’t Think

- **Throw away expired or sketchy items first**,  no judgment  
- **Gross containers? Toss the whole thing**,  you can replace the Tupperware  
- **Take out trash immediately** to remove odor and shame triggers  
- **Don’t sort yet,  just remove the obvious trash**  
> Say it out loud: “It’s okay to throw this away.”

#### 🧼 Clean One Section at a Time

- **One shelf = one cycle**: Remove → wipe → return what stays  
- **Use all-purpose spray and a rag or paper towel**  
- **Good enough is enough**,  this is not a lab inspection  
- **Skip drawers for now if needed**,  they can wait  

#### 📦 Put Things Back With Future You in Mind

- **Visibility wins**: Don’t bury produce in drawers you forget to open  
- **Group items by type**: Condiments together, leftovers together, snacks in one spot  
- **Use clear containers** if you can,  “out of sight” = out of mind  
- **Label if needed**: “Eat Me First” bins or masking tape dates help  

#### 🔁 Maintain With Micro-Resets

- **Use “microwave minutes”**: Wipe one shelf while reheating food  
- **Toss 1–2 items weekly**: Garbage day = mini fridge cleanse  
- **Tie it to another task**: Clean fridge while podcasting or waiting for laundry  
- **Make it visual**: Dry-erase fridge inventory, magnet checklist, or sticky notes  

#### 🤝 Support Systems & Accountability

- **Body double it**: Clean while chatting with a friend or watching a YouTube reset video  
- **Ask for help**: Having someone nearby (even virtually) helps hold attention  
- **Text a win**: “Just tossed 3 expired things and wiped the top shelf” = dopamine hit 
- **Outsource if needed**: It’s okay to ask a roommate, family member, or support worker to help reset it
