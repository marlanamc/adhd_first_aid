## 🍱 Meal Prepping

Meal prepping with ADHD can feel like a puzzle of **timing**, **energy**, and **logistics**. You might get stuck before starting, burn out halfway through, or forget what you made. The goal isn't perfection,  it's finding ways to support your **future self** without overwhelming your **present one**.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Meal prep doesn't have to mean **ten containers** of chicken and broccoli. It can be **washing fruit**, **chopping veggies**, or **just making one thing easier**. You're not **failing** if it's **imperfect**,  you're **winning** every time you do something that helps **future-you eat**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You keep saying you're **too tired to cook**,  but you're also **refusing to prep**. That's not a **time issue**,  that's a **planning issue**. Spend **30 minutes today** so you're not **spiraling tomorrow**. **Cut the damn veggies**.

### 🧠 Why Meal Prepping Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Overthink recipes and never pick one
  - Get lost in grocery aisles without a list
  - Start prepping but stop halfway through
  - Forget you even made food
  - Burn out after cooking once

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes planning, sequencing, and follow-through harder
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** makes it hard to predict how long things will take
  - 💥 **Low dopamine** makes boring or repetitive tasks hard to start
  - 🔁 **Overwhelm and decision fatigue** cause "analysis paralysis"
  - 🧠 **Working memory issues** make multi-step cooking feel like a blur

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Meal Prep

- **Pick protein first**: Build meals around it
- **Embrace food fixations**: It's fine to eat the same thing for 3 days
- **Limit decision-making**: 2–3 go-to meals is enough
- **Frozen and pre-cut foods are lifesavers**: Use them without shame
- **Stock up on "emergency food"**: Frozen rice, canned beans, rotisserie chicken

### 🛠️ Strategies for Meal Prepping

#### 🍳 Make Cooking Doable

- **Batch one base item**: Like chicken, beef, or roasted veg
- **One-pot, one-pan, sheet-pan**: Less cleanup = more doable
- **"Do the work once"**: Prep ingredients you can reuse
- **Set a timer and music**: Give the task a beginning and end
- **Gamify it**: See how much you can prep before your playlist ends
- **Use a checklist**: Yes, even for "obvious" steps

#### 🧊 Keep It Visible + Usable

- **Use clear containers**: Label or stack meals by day
- **Keep food in sight**: Don't bury it in the crisper drawer
- **Dry-erase board on fridge**: List what's ready to eat
- **Take a fridge photo**: Reference it instead of re-opening the door
- **Portion out snacks**: Future-you will thank you

#### 🧠 Rethink Hunger

- **ADHD can mess with hunger cues**: If you wait until you feel it, you might already be crashing
- **Schedule "practical eating"**: Meals or snacks before energy dips
- **Even if it's not perfect or pretty**: Eating something is a win

#### 👯‍♀️ Support & Motivation

- **Body double meal prep**: Even over video chat
- **Meal plan with a friend**: Share ideas or swap dishes
- **Reward yourself**: Small treat after you prep
- **Don't go it alone**: Coaching, support groups, or ADHD communities can help

### 🧭 Meal Prep ≠ Perfection

- You don't need a Pinterest kitchen or gourmet plan
- You need food you'll actually eat and systems you'll actually use
- Meal prepping isn't about control,  it's about care
- Every step you take is a small act of self-respect

> Grace for when it doesn't happen is part of the plan.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> You're winning every time you do something that helps future-you eat. Even washing fruit counts. Even having emergency snacks counts. You're taking care of yourself.
