## 🧰 Minor Repairs

Fixing small things around the house sounds easy, until ADHD shows up with **task initiation blocks**, **decision paralysis**, and **time blindness**. That broken drawer or loose handle becomes background noise until it's urgent. You're not neglectful, your brain just deprioritizes what doesn't scream for attention.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Those "tiny" tasks aren't **tiny** to an **overwhelmed brain**. **Out of sight = out of mind**. And broken things can **drain you** without you realizing it. Pick **one repair**. Set a **timer**. Your space deserves to feel **cared for**, just like you do.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You've walked past that **broken bulb** or **squeaky hinge** for weeks. It's not a **project**, it's a **5-minute fix**. Stop letting **little things pile up** and eat your peace. **Handle it now** and be done with it.

### 🧠 Why Minor Repairs Are Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Walk past a broken drawer or burnt-out bulb for weeks
  - Keep meaning to fix something but never start
  - Forget where your tools are or what you need
  - Feel paralyzed by tiny decisions (what kind of screwdriver?)
  - Avoid repairs until they become urgent or unsafe

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes planning, sequencing, and follow-through harder
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** causes you to underestimate how long "quick fixes" will take
  - 💥 **Low dopamine** makes boring or repetitive tasks hard to start
  - 🔁 **Overwhelm** kicks in when a repair involves clutter or unclear steps
  - 🧠 **Working memory issues** mean you forget steps mid-task

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Repairs

- **Externalize everything**: Keep tools visible and broken items in sight
- **Break it way down**: Ridiculously small steps beat overwhelm
- **Good enough is the goal**: Function over perfection
- **Use timers**: Most fixes take less time than you think
- **Get support**: Body doubling and accountability help

### 🛠️ Strategies for Minor Repairs

#### 🔄 Externalize Everything

- **Create a dedicated toolbox**: Most-used items like screwdrivers, extra bulbs, super glue, batteries
- **Keep broken items in sight**: Put the dead bulb on your dresser, broken knob on your keyboard
- **Make visual task lists**: Sticky notes near the issue work better than memory
- **Use a "maintenance dump list"**: One place to log every "I need to fix that" thought

#### 🔧 Break It Way Down

- **Ridiculously small steps**: "Find wrench," "Watch 2 minutes of how-to video," "Touch the loose hinge"
- **Use checklists for recurring stuff**: "Change smoke detector battery" or "Unclog sink"
- **One step at a time**: Don't plan the whole repair, just the next action

#### ⏱️ Time It

- **Set a 5-minute timer**: Just start, most tasks take less time than you think
- **Use a Pomodoro**: 25/5 for slightly longer fixes
- **Track actual time**: Correct warped time perception for future reference

#### 👯 Add Support

- **Body double**: Call a friend or have someone sit nearby
- **Accountability buddy**: Tell someone what you're fixing, send a pic when done
- **Outsource when needed**: Not a failure to hire help or trade tasks

### 🧭 Minor Repairs ≠ Major Projects

- "Good enough" is the goal, you need it to work, not be perfect
- You're not lazy, you're up against a brain wired for urgency, not upkeep
- Fixed one lightbulb? That's a dopamine hit and a step forward
- A working drawer means less stress in the morning

> These little wins matter more than you think.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> Your space deserves to feel cared for, just like you do. One small fix at a time builds momentum for the next.
