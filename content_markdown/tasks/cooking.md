## 🍳 Cooking

Cooking with ADHD requires juggling **timing**, **sequencing**, and **attention**, all of which are vulnerable to dysregulation. It’s not that you don’t want to cook,  it’s that even one misstep or distraction can throw the whole process into chaos.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You don't have to **love cooking** to **feed yourself well**. **Executive dysfunction**, **sensory aversions**, and **perfectionism** can all get in the way. Start with **low-effort wins**: **one-pan meals**, **frozen backups**, **repeat recipes**. You're allowed to **keep it simple**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You don't need to cook like a **chef**,  you just need to **fuel your body**. Living on **snacks** and **takeout** will **wear you down**. Start simple: **protein**, **veggie**, **pan**. You don't have to **love it**,  just build the **habit** of **showing up for yourself**.

### 🧠 Why Cooking Feels So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Get stuck staring at the fridge with “nothing to eat” despite a full kitchen  
  - Plan meals you never end up cooking  
  - Burn food because you forgot it was on  
  - Feel paralyzed by mess, smell, or clutter before even starting  
  - Lose steam halfway through a recipe  

- Here’s what’s really going on:
  - � � **Executive dysfunction** impacts sequencing, time awareness, and attention  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** makes meal prep feel either eternal or invisible  
  - � � **Task initiation** issues make it hard to start (even if you’re hungry)  
  - � � **Working memory** struggles = forgetting what step you’re on mid-recipe  
  - � � Sensory sensitivities can make textures, smells, or dish sounds hard to tolerate  
  - � � **Perfectionism** or fear of “doing it wrong” leads to avoiding it altogether  

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Cooking

- **You don’t have to love cooking to make it manageable**  
- **The goal is nourishment, not a five-star experience**  
- **Start where you are and simplify from there**  
- **Cooking counts even if it’s reheating, assembling, or microwaving**  
- **You’re not behind,  you’re experimenting your way forward**  

### 🛠️ Cooking Strategies That Actually Work

#### 🧠 Self-Compassion & Mindset

- **“Try differently, not harder”**,  standard methods often don’t work for ADHD brains  
- **“Good enough” meals = successful meals**  
- **Progress looks like getting food on a plate, not making it look good**  
- **Every time you cook, you're learning what works for _you_**  
- **Cooking is a skill, not a moral test.** You’re allowed to build it your way  

#### 🔥 Activate with Simplicity

- 🥗 **Lean on ADHD Easy Meals**  
  - Assemble meals instead of cooking from scratch  
  - Combine protein + carb + something crunchy or colorful  
  - Use snack plates (cheese, crackers, fruit) or wraps if full meals feel like too much  

- 🧂 **Cook in batches**  
  - Make a big tray of chicken, rice, or veggies  
  - Use it across multiple meals to reduce daily prep  
  - Freeze leftovers in single portions if you can  

- 📋 **Create a short list of go-to meals**  
  - Tape them to your fridge or put them in your phone  
  - Think: 10-minute meals, one-pan meals, no-cook options  
  - This helps when your brain is too tired to “decide” anything  

- 🧊 **Stock fallback food**  
  - Frozen dumplings, soups, instant rice, or high-protein snacks  
  - Build a shelf of “brain-off” backups for when everything feels too hard  

- 🍱 **Microwave = valid**  
  - Reheating frozen meals, leftovers, or steam bags counts as cooking  
  - Not every meal needs to happen on a stovetop  

#### 🍳 Set Up Your Space

- 🔪 **Pre-cut, pre-washed ingredients**  
  - Pre-chopped veggies, pre-cooked proteins = fewer barriers  
  - The goal is low-prep, not guilt-free  

- 📦 **Visible storage**  
  - Store perishables where you can _see_ them  
  - Use clear bins or glass containers so nothing goes to waste unseen  

- 🧴 **Dawn Powerwash** (or DIY)  
  - Makes quick scrubbing feel less daunting  
  - **DIY Powerwash Spray**:  
    - 1/3 cup dish soap  
    - 1/3 cup rubbing alcohol  
    - Fill rest with water  
    - Optional: a few drops of lemon or tea tree oil  

- 🧼 **Clean while you wait (if it helps)**  
  - Do 1–2 dishes during moments like boiling or preheating. Or don’t. You choose what serves your energy  

#### ⏰ Manage Time & Overwhelm

- 🕰️ **Set a pre-cook transition timer**  
  - Give yourself 5–10 minutes of “prep runway”  
  - This helps your brain _shift gears_ from task-switching to cooking mode  

- ⏳ **Use a visual timer**  
  - Great for remembering you’re cooking something  
  - Place it in your line of sight, or use smart speaker reminders  

- 🔁 **Break the task into mini steps**  
  - “Get out the pan” → “Take out the veggies” → “Turn on burner”  
  - You don’t have to do it all at once. Just do the next thing  
  - ADHD momentum builds _after_ action, not before. Don’t wait to “feel ready”  

- 🚪 **“Move toward it” strategy**  
  - Go to the kitchen. Stand near the stove. Open a cabinet  
  - Momentum often starts with a single movement  

- 🎁 **Build in a reward**  
  - “When I cook dinner, I get to binge my favorite show guilt-free”  
  - Rewards give your brain something to look forward to  

- 👯 **Use body doubling**  
  - Call a friend while you cook  
  - Or play a YouTube video of someone else cooking,  mimic the vibe  

### 💡 Support Fuel & Focus

- 💪 **Start with protein**  
  - Eggs, rotisserie chicken, tofu, Greek yogurt, lentils  
  - Protein improves focus and energy regulation  

- 💦 **Stay hydrated while you cook**  
  - Keep a water bottle on the counter  
  - Cooking can be dehydrating and impact energy  

- 🧠 **Eat mindfully (when you can)**  
  - Sit down when possible  
  - Use sensory-friendly utensils if textures or sounds are irritating  
  - Your relationship with food matters more than what’s on the plate  

### 🧍 Support & Delegation

- 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 **Delegate when possible**  
  - If you live with someone, share the job:  
    - One shops, the other cooks  
    - One cooks, the other cleans  
  - If you’re solo, split tasks across days: plan Monday, shop Tuesday, prep Wednesday  

- 🧠 **ADHD coaching or support groups**  
  - Get support for shame-free systems, habit-stacking, and dopamine-aware routines  
  - You’re not alone,  cooking is _very_ ADHD-unfriendly by default  

- 💬 **Ask for help**  
  - “Can you help me figure out what to make this week?”  
  - “Can you sit on Zoom while I cook dinner?”  

### 💡 Cooking Isn’t a Moral Test

- Feeding yourself is care,  not performance
- You’re allowed to cook the same thing on repeat
- You’re allowed to microwave a frozen burrito and call it dinner
- You’re allowed to eat imperfectly and still be taking care of yourself
- Progress over perfection,  every effort counts

> You’re not behind. You’re building a rhythm that works for _your_ brain

- If you opened the fridge and _thought_ about dinner, that’s a step
