## 📅 Scheduling Appointments

Scheduling appointments takes more than picking a time,  it requires **executive planning**, **time prediction**, and often **phone or email follow-up**. With ADHD, even the thought of it can lead to shutdown. You're not avoiding responsibility,  you're navigating invisible cognitive load.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Making appointments can feel like **opening a door** to more **obligations**. You're not **bad at adulting**,  you're managing **fear** and **executive dysfunction**. Start with **one call**. You don't need to do them **all**,  just the **next one**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're not avoiding **appointments**,  you're avoiding **responsibility**. Whether it's your **health**, your **money**, or your **legal stuff**, this won't **solve itself**. **Make the call**. **Book the visit**. **Handle your life**.

### 🧠 Why Scheduling Appointments Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Stare at your phone for 20 minutes, unable to dial
  - Forget who you were supposed to call or why
  - Say yes to something you didn't want, just to get off the phone
  - Avoid it for so long that it becomes a bigger problem
  - Book it… and then forget to show up

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes even simple steps feel impossible to initiate
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** leads to poor time prediction and scheduling pileups
  - 💥 **Phone anxiety** adds friction to an already draining task
  - 🔁 **Shame or fear of judgment** can trigger total avoidance
  - 🧠 **Working memory struggles** make it hard to juggle times, questions, and logistics

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Scheduling

- **Break it way down**: "Book appointment" becomes 5 micro-steps
- **Externalize and automate**: Multiple calendar alarms and visual reminders
- **Add support**: Body doubling, accountability buddies, or ask someone to book for you
- **Adjust to fit your brain**: Schedule calls during peak focus windows
- **Progress over perfection**: Booking one appointment is a win

### 🛠️ Strategies for Scheduling Appointments

#### 🧠 Break It Down First

- **Micro-steps**: Find phone number → Check 3 time slots → Write down questions → Call and book → Add to calendar + alarm
- **Brain dump first**: List everything circling your mind about the task
- **Pre-script**: What you want to say so you're not caught off guard

#### 📆 Externalize & Automate

- **Add to calendar immediately**: Don't trust your memory
- **Multiple alarms**: Day before, hour before, time to leave
- **Visual wall calendar**: Help with time awareness
- **Pre-appointment checklist**: Confirm details, gather forms, plan transit
- **Notes in calendar**: "Follow-up bloodwork," "renew Rx"

#### 👯 Add Support

- **Body double**: Have someone nearby while you make the call
- **Accountability buddy**: Text "I'm calling at 3pm" and follow up
- **Ask someone to book**: If that's an option when overwhelmed
- **Talk through decisions**: If stuck choosing time or provider

#### 🔧 Adjust to Fit Your Brain

- **Peak focus window**: Schedule hard tasks during your best time
- **Time-blocking**: "Admin hour" once a week for scheduling
- **Name the emotion**: Fear, shame, overwhelm,  give it compassion
- **Practice boundaries**: "Let me check and call you back"

### 🧭 Scheduling ≠ Perfection

- Progress over perfection,  booking one appointment is a win
- "Good enough" is great,  you don't need the best time or most efficient setup
- You're not bad at this,  your brain is protecting itself from overload
- Use rewards,  pair scheduling with something nice

> Your future self will thank you for showing up.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> Making the appointment is a win, not a chore. You don't need to feel totally ready or have it all figured out,  just take the first step.
