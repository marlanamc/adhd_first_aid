## 🚪 Getting Out the Door

Leaving the house with ADHD can be a mini-battle of **time blindness**, **transition trouble**, and **task stacking overwhelm**. You may feel rushed, flustered, or paralyzed by all the tiny steps involved. It's not about being disorganized, it's that your brain struggles to sequence and shift gears smoothly.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
**Transitions are hard**. **ADHD brains** struggle to **shift** from "here" to "there," especially under **pressure**. You're not **broken** for needing **more prep time**. Build in **buffers**, **prep bags** the night before, and celebrate _**just getting out**_. That's a **win**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're not getting anywhere **on time** without a **system**. Stop **underestimating** how long things take. Get your stuff **ready the night before**. Set alarms _**earlier**_ than you think. Being **late** is optional, if you **plan** like it isn't.

### 🧠 Why Leaving the House Is So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Underestimate how long getting ready actually takes
  - Forget things like your wallet or keys even after "checking"
  - Panic at the last minute, overwhelmed by all the micro-tasks
  - Get stuck switching from one mode (resting, working) to another
  - Arrive late and feel guilty, again

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧠 **Working memory gaps** make you forget small steps  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** means you don't _feel_ the clock ticking  
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes sequencing and planning difficult  
  - 💥 **Low dopamine** makes boring tasks like getting dressed feel impossible  
  - 🌀 **ADHD paralysis** turns urgency into shutdown  
  - ⚠️ **Shame spirals** kick in if you've been late one too many times

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Departures

- **Prep is kindness to future-you**: Getting things ready the night before removes decision fatigue  
- **The last 10 minutes are always chaos**: Expect it and plan around it  
- **Visual reminders beat mental checklists**: Externalize everything you can  
- **Done is better than perfect**: You don't need to look magazine-ready to leave  
- **Being late is rarely about laziness**: It's about friction and executive function struggles

### 🛠️ Strategies to Get Out the Door

#### 🧠 Externalize Everything

- **Use a launch pad**: Always put keys, wallet, headphones, and essentials in the same visible place
- **Sticky note your exit**: Put a note by the door with last-minute reminders: "Lunch? Water? ID?"
- **Pack your bag the night before**: Even if it feels silly, future-you will thank you
- **Set alarms in layers**: One to wrap up, one to get ready, one to walk out
- **Try a checklist**: Either on your phone or taped near the door

#### ⏰ Tame Time Blindness

- **Start earlier than you think**: If you _feel_ like you have time, you probably don't
- **Use visual timers**: Time Timer or Pomodoro to countdown "leave by" prep
- **Time track your routine once**: Learn how long things actually take, not what you _think_ they take
- **Name your exit window**: "If I'm not brushing my teeth by 8:15, I'll be late"

#### 📦 Break It Into Micro-Steps

- **"Get ready" = 10+ steps**: Write them out or make them visual:
  - 🛁 Shower
  - 🧴 Skincare / deodorant
  - 👕 Get dressed
  - 🍳 Eat or pack food
  - 🎒 Pack bag
  - 🔑 Grab essentials
  - 🚪 Leave
- **Do one thing at a time**: That's enough.

#### 🎮 Gamify & Motivate

- **Play a favorite playlist**: Use it for your "get ready" routine
- **Set a reward**: Coffee when you're out the door on time
- **Time yourself**: Try to beat your own record
- **Use "dopamine bait"**: Pair morning prep with something enjoyable (music, podcast, iced coffee)

#### 🧍 Support & Environment

- **Ask for a check-in text**: "Hey, leaving by 9?"
- **Body double mornings**: Hop on a quick call while you pack your bag
- **Leave helpful chaos**: Put your keys on top of your shoes so you can't miss them
- **Keep backups**: A spare charger, hair tie, snack bar already in your bag

### 🧭 This Isn't Just a Routine, It's a System

- Getting out the door isn't "just get dressed and go." It's a sequence of high-friction transitions.  
- When your brain's not wired for smooth handoffs, build in supports.
> Every step you externalize is one less thing to forget.  
> Every alarm is a guide rope.  
> Every late day is a chance to tweak the system, not proof you're failing.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> If you made it out in mismatched socks, that counts. If you packed your bag the night before, that's self-trust. If you were 5 minutes early, you just rewrote a pattern. The goal isn't perfection, it's making leaving less painful, one exit at a time.
