## 🔁 Following Up

Following up requires **working memory**, **prioritization**, and often **social risk**, all tough for ADHD brains. You may forget to respond, overthink your reply, or lose track entirely. It's not rudeness, it's an invisible wall made of cognitive load and emotional resistance.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Following up is hard when your brain **forgets the thread** or **fears rejection**. You're not **irresponsible**, you just need **external reminders** and **simple scripts**. Following up doesn't make you **annoying**, it shows you **care**. You deserve to be **heard** and **remembered**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're **ghosting** your own **goals**. That **message**, **job app**, or **invoice** isn't going to **chase itself**. People **forget**, not because they don't care, but because they're **busy**. Following up isn't **desperate**. It's **strategic**. Be the person who **circles back**.

### 🧠 Why Following Up Feels So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Forget what you're even supposed to follow up on
  - Freeze at the thought of sounding "pushy"
  - Avoid checking your email because of anxiety
  - Lose the thread after a day (or an hour)
  - Feel like the moment "passed" and now it's too late

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧠 **Working memory struggles** mean you forget what you meant to do  
  - ⏳ **Time blindness** distorts your sense of urgency and timing  
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes planning and follow-through harder  
  - 📉 **Low dopamine** makes boring tasks feel physically impossible to begin  
  - 💬 **Rejection sensitivity** turns one polite nudge into a social minefield

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Follow-Up

- **It's not personal, it's neurological**: Your brain isn't built for linear tracking  
- **Reminders are tools, not weaknesses**: Use them unapologetically  
- **Done is better than perfect**: A short, late follow-up is still a win  
- **Following up shows professionalism, not desperation**  
- **Templates and scripts save energy for the real work**

### 🛠️ Strategies for Following Up

#### 🧠 Externalize Everything

- **Never rely on memory**: Your brain's RAM is already full
- **Set calendar events**: "Follow up with X, Monday 10am"
- **Use email flags, stars, or folders** like "Waiting On"
- **Add reminders when you send the first message**: future-you will forget
- **Put sticky notes at your desk, bathroom mirror, fridge**: wherever you pause

#### 🔨 Break It Down

- **Split it into micro-steps**:
  - Find the thread
  - Draft a 1–2 sentence message
  - Re-read if needed
  - Click send
- **Touch it once**: If it'll take less than 2 minutes, do it _now_, don't start a guilt pile

#### 💬 Keep It Simple

- **Use templates**:
  - "Hi! Just checking in on this, any updates?"
  - "Hey, following up to see if you saw this!"
  - "Just bringing this back to the top of your inbox :)"
- **Be direct and kind**: You don't need an excuse or apology. Clarity is kindness.
- **No pressure phrasing**: "If this is still relevant, I'm happy to help!" can ease anxiety

#### ⏰ Time Blindness Solutions

- **Automate reminders**: Use Google Calendar, FollowUpThen, or task apps
- **Schedule follow-up time blocks**: 10–15 mins daily or weekly to check loose ends
- **Name the task specifically**: "Email Jen re: invoice," not "follow up stuff"

#### 🧍 Support & Motivation

- **Body doubling**: Follow up while someone sits nearby or co-works on video
- **Accountability buddies**: "Ask me on Friday if I messaged them!"
- **Gamify it**: Turn it into a 10-minute sprint with a reward (snack, scroll, break)
- **Use dopamine bait**: Pair it with music, coffee, or something cozy

### 🧭 You're Not a Nuisance

- Your reminder might be the nudge they needed. Most people aren't ignoring you, they're juggling too much, too.
> Following up is not annoying.  
> It's not desperate.  
> It's how people stay visible in a noisy world.  
> You deserve responses. You deserve clarity. You're not too much for circling back.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> If you re-opened the thread, that's progress.  
> If you copied a template and hit send, that's success.  
> If it's late but you followed up anyway, that's resilience.  
> You're not behind, you're re-engaging. That's powerful.
