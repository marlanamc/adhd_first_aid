## 📱 Phone Scrolling

You pick up your phone for a break, and suddenly it's been hours. ADHD brains seek **novelty**, **dopamine**, and **escape**, all of which scrolling provides. The guilt after doesn't help. This isn't a failure of discipline, it's a sign your brain is overstimulated, under-supported, or both.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Your phone is trying to meet a real need: **stimulation**, **escape**, **regulation**. And that's okay, ADHD brains crave those things. But over time, the scroll doesn't soothe, it **numbs**. According to NYU researcher Dr. Adam Alter, the average person may spend **up to 20 years** of their life scrolling. That's **not** rest. That's **loss**. You deserve **better** than algorithms pretending to care.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
**You're wasting your life.** Literally. Dr. Adam Alter of NYU found that the average person will spend **up to 20 years** of their life scrolling. Twenty. Years. That's how much time you're **handing over** to apps that **don't** love you back. Put the phone down. Close the app. Don't let your **legacy** be likes and loops.

### 🧠 Why Phone Use Feels So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Pick up your phone to check the weather and find yourself on Instagram 20 minutes later
  - Intend to text someone back, but forget what you were doing mid-scroll
  - Feel pulled to check your phone constantly, even during other tasks
  - Struggle to stop scrolling at night, even when you're tired
  - Feel overstimulated but can't look away

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧠 **Executive dysfunction** makes it hard to resist impulse use and disengage  
  - 📝 **Working memory issues** cause you to forget why you picked up your phone  
  - 🎰 **Dopamine-seeking** leads you to apps that feel rewarding or novel  
  - 📵 **Task avoidance** drives you to reach for your phone instead of starting something hard  
  - 📱 **Hyperfocus** on your phone means losing track of time entirely  
  - 💬 **Constant notifications** create distraction and decision fatigue

### ⚓ Core Principles for Managing Phone Use

- **Your phone isn't evil**: It just needs structure  
- **Interrupt the autopilot**: You don't need to eliminate screen time, just make it intentional  
- **Use your novelty-seeking**: Channel that ADHD brain trait toward healthier outlets  
- **Mindful control over perfect discipline**: The goal is awareness, not perfection

### 🛠️ Strategies for Phone Use

#### 📵 Reduce Access & Disrupt the Autopilot

- **Designated "homes" for your phone** (e.g., basket by the door or charging dock away from the bed)
- **Phone-free zones**: Make your bedroom or bathroom phone-free to support better sleep and mental clarity
- **Leave it in another room** during focus time or meals
- **Lock it up**: Use a physical lockbox or a smart timer-safe for periods of deep work
- **Charging station out of sight**: Bonus points if it's in another room

#### 🔧 Automate Boundaries with Tech

- **Use Focus Mode or Do Not Disturb**: Schedule these during deep work blocks or meals
- **Set App Limits**: Built-in iOS/Android tools let you cap how much time you spend on apps
- **Try Brick or similar tools**: Brick is a dedicated tool that blocks apps and creates digital boundaries when willpower fails
- **App folders with reminders**: Rename folders "Is this what I really want?" to prompt mindfulness
- **Move distracting apps off your home screen** or into nested folders

#### 🧘 Mindful & Intentional Use

- **Intentional Unlock Ritual**: Before unlocking your phone, ask yourself, "What do I want to do right now?"
- **Speak your action out loud**: "I'm checking the weather" can help break the unconscious scroll cycle
- **Touch It Once**: If you open your phone to do something, do it, then put the phone down
- **Visual timers or widgets**: Use visual time tools on your home screen to stay grounded in the moment
- **Keep a mini phone-use log**: Track when, why, and how you use your phone, patterns often reveal themselves quickly

#### 🧠 Design for Distraction Resistance

- **Designate phone-free hours** (e.g., first hour after waking)
- **Use physical boundaries**: Headphones with music to reduce sensory input, sunglasses to help in overstimulating stores
- **Try a Paper Alternative**: Carry a paper notebook for brain dumps, reminders, and planning so you don't need your phone
- **Use sticky notes or visuals on your desk** that remind you of your focus intention

#### 🤝 Boundaries, Accountability & Community

- **Communicate your needs**: Let others know you're experimenting with limiting notifications or social media
- **Ask others to put info in writing**: Avoid having to recall instructions or appointments by requesting text or email confirmations
- **Body Doubling**: Have a friend virtually co-work with you to reduce the likelihood of scrolling
- **Work with a coach or therapist** if phone use is tied to deeper emotional regulation issues
- **Join a support community**: You're not the only one struggling with tech addiction, connection reduces shame

### 🧭 Phone Use ≠ Self-Control

- You don't have to "quit your phone" or "be more disciplined" to feel better
- You just need tools that match your brain's rhythm, not the tech industry's agenda

> Start with one small win, like moving one app or setting one boundary, and build from there.
