## 🤝 People-Pleasing Burnout

Saying yes to everything can feel like survival. With ADHD, **rejection sensitivity**, **low self-worth**, and a need to feel useful can push you to overextend,  until you crash. People-pleasing isn’t kindness gone too far,  it’s often a trauma-informed strategy for staying safe and accepted.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You learned to **meet needs** to **stay safe**. You're not **selfish** for wanting **rest now**. **Boundaries** aren't **betrayal**, they're how we stop **bleeding out in service** of others. You don't have to **earn rest** by being **needed**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
**People-pleasing is a disguise** for **self-neglect**. Every **"yes"** that should be a **"no"** is **energy theft** from your **future self**. If you don't start **setting limits**, your **body will set them** for you.

### 🧠 Why People-Pleasing Burnout Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Say yes to things you don't have time or energy for
  - Feel panic or guilt at the thought of disappointing someone
  - Overwork yourself to "make up" for your ADHD
  - Crash hard after doing too much for others
  - Feel resentment but say nothing, then spiral

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 💔 **Rejection Sensitivity (RSD)** makes you terrified of letting people down  
  - ⚡ **Impulsivity** leads to fast yeses before checking your bandwidth  
  - 🫥 **Masking and overcompensation** push you to prove your worth  
  - 🧠 **Executive dysfunction** makes it hard to see how full your plate already is  
  - 🔁 **Low self-worth** drives the belief you need to earn approval through service  
  - 🌪️ **Chronic overwhelm** builds when people-pleasing replaces true rest

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Boundaries

- **Pause before committing**: Your first impulse to say yes might not reflect your true capacity  
- **Boundaries protect relationships**: Saying no preserves your ability to show up authentically  
- **Energy is finite**: Managing your energy is as important as managing your time  
- **Self-worth isn't earned**: You don't have to be useful to be valuable  
- **Rest is responsibility**: Taking care of yourself helps you take care of others

### 🛠️ Strategies for People-Pleasing Burnout

#### ✋ Build Boundaries That Protect You

- **Pause before answering**: Say "Let me get back to you" instead of defaulting to yes
- **Write out your non-negotiables**: Energy limits, sleep, family time, etc.
- **Create go-to no phrases**: "I'd love to, but I'm at capacity right now."
- **Ask: "If I say yes to this, what am I saying no to?"** before committing
- **Use a "decision delay" rule** to stop impulsive yeses

#### 🧠 Rewire the People-Pleasing Loop

- **Notice where guilt shows up**: Guilt isn't always a sign you did something wrong
- **Reframe rest as responsible**: Protecting your energy helps you show up better
- **Journal your patterns**: What situations trigger your overgiving?
- **Celebrate when you say no**: Even small ones build strength
- **Acknowledge the fear**: People-pleasing often stems from trauma, not weakness


#### 📆 Manage Your Energy, Not Just Your Time

- **Track how long tasks really take** to build scheduling accuracy
- **Time-block with margin**: Leave space for transitions, rest, and unexpected needs
- **Use visual planners or digital calendars** to see your true capacity
- **Stack high-drain events with recovery time**: e.g., don't book back-to-back favors
- **Color-code commitments** by type or energy level to stay balanced


#### 🔄 Externalize and Simplify

- **Keep a "No List"**: Things you've said yes to in the past that drained you
- **Do regular brain dumps** to avoid overcommitting from mental clutter
- **Set reminder alarms to review your week** and spot patterns
- **Automate or delegate** tasks that don't actually need you
- **Reduce decisions**: Fewer daily inputs = more clarity on what you want to do


#### 🤝 Build a Safety Net

- **Body double or debrief with a friend** when you're about to overcommit
- **Join ADHD support groups**: Normalize your needs, celebrate your no's
- **Consider an ADHD coach** to help untangle self-worth from productivity
- **Ask trusted people for feedback** on when you're doing too much
- **Let safe people love the real you**, not just your usefulness


#### 💥 What To Do When You've Overcommitted

- **Don't ghost, communicate** as soon as you know you need to back out
- **Offer a clear boundary**: "I said yes too fast, I can't do this right now."
- **Forgive yourself**: Overgiving was once a survival skill
- **Revisit your systems**: Did you ignore your calendar? Skip a review?
- **Cancel something else**: Protect your recovery, not your perfect record

### 🧭 People-Pleasing Burnout ≠ Morality

- You're not selfish. You're not broken. You learned to survive by being useful.  
- People-pleasing is often rooted in fear of rejection, abandonment, failure. That's trauma, not character.
> You don't owe anyone your exhaustion. Boundaries are how you protect your future self.  
> Rest isn't earned. It's necessary.

### ✨ Encouragement to Take With You
- If you paused before saying yes, that's a win.  
- If you said no to something you didn't have energy for, that's a win.  
- If you protected your recovery time, that's a win.  
- Progress over perfection, every boundary counts.
> You're learning that your worth isn't tied to your usefulness.
> You don't have to earn love by being exhausted. You're valuable exactly as you are.
