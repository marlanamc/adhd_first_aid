# 📚 Sources: Feeling Restless with ADHD

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how restlessness in ADHD can stem from emotional overload, nervous system dysregulation, hopelessness, or the urge to _do something_ — even when you're exhausted. They offer tools to help you slow down, reconnect with your body, and move forward one small step at a time.

---

## 🔄 Emotional Turbulence, Hopelessness & Disconnection

- **The Anti-Planner** – _Dani Donovan_  
    Includes direct tools for managing “hopeless” spirals, including **Draw Your Feels**, **emotional first aid**, and self-compassion pep talks. Validates the paralysis that comes with restlessness.
    
- **How to Thrive with Adult ADHD** – _James Kustow_  
    Speaks to feeling “fed up and increasingly hopeless” and provides direction for building sustainable action plans that begin with self-awareness and small steps.
    
- **Do Hard Things** – _Steve Magness_  
    Frames emotions as **messengers, not dictators**, and urges turning discomfort into clarity. Cites Viktor Frankl’s reframing of suffering as a pathway to meaning and momentum.
    
- **Real Self-Care** – _Pooja Lakshmin, M.D._  
    Reminds readers that **hope is a skill**, not a feeling, and that acknowledging emotional depletion is a step toward healing. Discusses restlessness as a side effect of burnout masked by “faux self-care.”
    
- **Unapologetically ADHD** – _Kinzer & Wright_  
    Highlights the emotional fallout when ADHD systems fail. Encourages **resilience over perfection**, and supports using **brain dumps** and reset rituals to restore calm.
    
- **The Body Keeps the Score** – _Bessel van der Kolk, M.D._  
    Discusses **learned helplessness**, nervous system shutdown, and the role of **movement, breath, and interoception** in regaining internal regulation.
    
- **Mad About Money** – _Maddy Alexander-Grout_  
    Explores how grief and overwhelm intersect with neurodivergence, offering emotional support for feelings of hopelessness and emotional urgency.
    
- **How to ADHD** – _Jessica McCabe_  
    Validates big emotions and supports the use of personalized tools to interrupt spirals and regain a sense of control.

## ✍️ Externalization: Free-Writing, Body Mapping & Naming Emotions

- **The Anti-Planner** – _Dani Donovan_  
    Features **“Draw Your Feels”** and techniques to work through judgment, insecurity, and emotional blocks — ideal for processing restlessness.
    
- **The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook** – _Kemp & Mitchelson_  
    Teaches **interoception** (body awareness) and provides exercises like **“Track Your Emotions and Physical Sensations”** and **“Map Your Body”** to understand urges and emotional agitation.
    
- **Tiny Experiments** – _Anne-Laure Le Cunff_  
    Advocates for **affect labeling** (naming emotions) and using metaphor or imagery to understand internal states. Notes that **uncomfortable emotions aren't inherently bad** — it's how we respond to them.
    
- **Do Hard Things** – _Steve Magness_  
    Emphasizes that labeling and examining emotions builds **emotional clarity**, allowing you to choose responses that support — not sabotage — your long-term values.
    
- **The Body Keeps the Score** – _Bessel van der Kolk, M.D._  
    Deep dive into the **body’s role in storing emotion**, highlighting physical practices (like drawing or movement) that help integrate and process difficult sensations.

## 🧠 Brain Dumping & External Processing

- **Building a Second Brain** – _Tiago Forte_  
    Describes **brain dumping** as a cognitive unloading tool, allowing thoughts to be held **outside your brain** to relieve overwhelm and free up working memory.
    
- **Unapologetically ADHD** – _Kinzer & Wright_  
    Frames **brain dumps** as a core ADHD-friendly strategy. Includes printable worksheets for offloading thoughts without judgment or obligation.
    
- **Order from Chaos** – _Jaclyn Paul_  
    Offers structures like a **“Someday Maybe” list** and inbox processing strategies to reduce anxiety and settle mental restlessness.
    

---

## ⚖️ Reclaiming Control: Doing Less, Resting More

- **Feel-Good Productivity** – _Ali Abdaal_  
    Challenges hustle culture by advocating **intentional rest** and “doing nothing” as a valid and productive choice when the mind is overwhelmed or restless.
    
- **How to Keep House While Drowning** – _KC Davis_  
    Declares that **rest is a right, not a reward**. Encourages guilt-free downtime, even if tasks are unfinished — especially helpful when restlessness masks exhaustion.
    

---

## 🪜 Micro-Steps, Baby Steps & Self-Pacing

- **Tiny Habits** – _BJ Fogg_  
    Focuses on **tiny actions** as a way to build momentum and break paralysis. If it feels too hard, **make it smaller**.
    
- **You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!** – _Kelly & Ramundo_  
    Advises starting with a **“baby step”** to reduce resistance. This is especially helpful when restless energy feels urgent but unfocused.
    
- **The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success** – _Dawson & Guare_  
    Recommends **scaling back interventions** when overwhelm hits, and **building ramps instead of leaps** to encourage action without burnout.
