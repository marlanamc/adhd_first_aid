# 📚 Sources: Feeling Scattered with ADHD

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of scattered thinking — from executive function breakdowns to emotional overload — and offer strategies for organization, externalization, pacing, and self-compassion when your mind feels like ten tabs open and no focus.

---

## 🧠 Executive Dysfunction, Attention, and the Scattered Mind

- **A New Understanding of ADHD** – _Thomas E. Brown_  
    Defines the six executive function clusters disrupted by ADHD, including **working memory**, **focus**, and **emotional regulation** — all core to the scattered feeling.
    
- **The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success** – _Dawson & Guare_  
    Designed for people who feel “scattered and not accomplishing goals.” Covers **time management**, **organization**, and **mind-wandering**, with step-by-step tools.
    
- **A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD** – _Frank, Solden, & Littman_  
    Breaks down executive function issues like **task coordination**, **routine building**, and **memory management** — and how they impact identity and self-esteem.
    
- **It All Makes Sense Now** – _Meredith Carder_  
    Affirms the non-linear way ADHD minds process the world: “like a spiderweb, not a straight line.” Reduces shame about feeling disorganized or behind.
    
- **Unapologetically ADHD** – _Kinzer & Wright_  
    Describes how **motor and cognitive inhibition issues** lead to impulsivity, distraction, and disorganization. Offers planning systems that allow for **buffer time** and unexpected chaos.
    
- **Scattered Minds** – _Gabor Maté_  
    A foundational text that not only shares its name with this experience but also illustrates what it’s like to follow one idea into another, and another... until you forget where you started.
    
- **How to ADHD** – _Jessica McCabe_  
    Created _because_ she couldn’t remember where she stored her ADHD resources. Uses white space, short sections, and checklists to model scattered-brain-friendly design.
    
- **The Disorganized Mind** – _Nancy Ratey_  
    Covers **distractibility**, **emotional response**, and **planning failures**, with coaching strategies to build external structure.
    
- **Succeeding with Adult ADHD** – _Abigail Levrini_  
    Teaches **metacognition**, **planning**, and **impulse control**, and directly addresses the overwhelm of trying to transform chaos into clarity.
    
- **Order from Chaos** – _Jaclyn Paul_  
    Literally built to help ADHDers manage scattered energy and turn mental clutter into systems. Introduces idea safekeeping tools like a **“treasure chest”** and **brain dumps**.
    
- **Women with Attention Deficit Disorder** – _Sari Solden_  
    Explores how emotional layers — especially shame and low self-worth — make disorganization and scattered thinking even harder to manage.
    
- **How to Thrive with Adult ADHD** – _James Kustow_  
    Offers a 7-pillar plan to reduce **attentional chaos** and slowly build sustainable systems of clarity.
    
- **ADHD 2.0** – _Hallowell & Ratey_  
    Provides updated science and relatable analogies to help make sense of how attention fractures and executive systems short-circuit.
    
- **Tiny Experiments** – _Anne-Laure Le Cunff_  
    Tackles **analysis paralysis**, **choice overwhelm**, and scattered thought patterns with playful, forgiving micro-interventions.

## 📝 Brain Dumps & Externalizing Chaos

- **Building a Second Brain** – _Tiago Forte_  
    Teaches you how to build a **“storage system for your mind”** so you don’t have to juggle everything internally. Emphasizes **detachment gain** and dynamic organization.
    
- **The Anti-Planner** – _Dani Donovan_  
    Includes **brain dump lists** and permission to externalize messy thoughts without judgment.
    
- **Order from Chaos** – _Jaclyn Paul_  
    Adds practical systems like **“Someday Maybe” lists** and inbox processing to capture scattered thoughts. Encourages voice memos or delegation when needed.
    
- **Unapologetically ADHD** – _Kinzer & Wright_  
    Offers worksheets for brain dumping and intentional processing, helping you reassemble scattered mental threads into something actionable.

## 🧍‍♀️ Reconnecting with the Body & Reframing Emotion

- **The Body Keeps the Score** – _Bessel van der Kolk, M.D._  
    Explores how disorganization and trauma live in the body. Introduces the **“felt sense”** — bodily awareness as a navigation tool for scattered minds.
    
- **Tiny Experiments** – _Anne-Laure Le Cunff_  
    Suggests visual and metaphor-based reframing of emotions, connecting scattered feelings to internal landscapes.
    
- **Do Hard Things** – _Steve Magness_  
    Frames emotions as **“messengers, not dictators”**, supporting emotional awareness and physical grounding to regulate scattered overwhelm.
    
- **The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook** – _Kemp & Mitchelson_  
    Teaches **interoception** and helps you **map your internal state**, recognize urges, and pause before reacting.
    
- **How to ADHD** – _Jessica McCabe_  
    Includes emotion regulation worksheets and encourages identifying needs behind scattered panic and overwhelm.
    
- **The Anti-Planner** – _Dani Donovan_  
    Invites readers to **Draw Your Feels**, and confront the inner chaos with play and humor.

## 🧃 Scaling Back & Finding Calm

- **Feel-Good Productivity** – _Ali Abdaal_  
    Says “Doing nothing” is sometimes the most productive thing when your mind is spinning.
    
- **How to Keep House While Drowning** – _KC Davis_  
    Reminds you that **rest is a right, not a reward** — especially when ADHD burnout becomes internal clutter.
    
- **Real Self-Care** – _Pooja Lakshmin, M.D._  
    Advocates for **boundaries** and choosing what matters, rather than trying to manage everything.

## 🪜 Tiny Steps When You’re All Over the Place

- **Tiny Habits** – _BJ Fogg_  
    Focuses on **small, low-bar wins** that make scattered minds feel progress without pressure.
    
- **You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!** – _Kelly & Ramundo_  
    Encourages **baby steps** to reduce overwhelm and help attention stick to one thing at a time.
    
- **Unapologetically ADHD** – _Kinzer & Wright_  
    Offers planning tools and **intentional time blocks** to bring structure without rigidity.
    
- **Succeeding with Adult ADHD** – _Levrini_  
    Shows how small action-oriented changes and reframing mental roadblocks can rewire attention and motivation.
    
- **The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success** – _Dawson & Guare_  
    Advises starting **tiny and gradual**, especially when attention fractures or task-switching is impaired.
