# 📚 Sources: Feeling Tense with ADHD

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of tension and offer strategies for nervous system regulation, emotional awareness, somatic grounding, boundary-setting, and calming an overactivated ADHD brain.

---

## 🧠 Understanding Tension in ADHD

- **Carder, Meredith.** _It All Makes Sense Now: Embrace Your ADHD Brain to Live a_ (2024).  
    Explains ADHD as a neurobiological difference, not a moral failure. Highlights how tension arises from a brain that develops and functions differently.
    
- **Hallowell, Edward M., & Ratey, John J.** _ADHD 2.0: New Science and Essential Strategies for Thriving_ (2020).  
    Compares the ADHD brain to a Ferrari with bicycle brakes—powerful but hard to control, leading to chronic internal tension.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD_ (2024).  
    Explores how neurodivergent brains experience stress and trauma from living in a non-accommodating world, contributing to chronic nervous system activation.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_ (2024).  
    Describes ADHD as a dysregulation disorder and affirms that differences in processing and regulation often lead to chronic stress states.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_ (2024).  
    Frames ADHD as a disorder of regulation. Notes that low-grade, persistent stress can erode your ability to relax or focus.
    
- **Levrini, Abigail L.** _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_ (2025).  
    Discusses how ADHD continues into adulthood, impacting emotional regulation and creating underlying tension in everyday life.
    
- **Maté, Gabor.** _Scattered Minds_ (2019).  
    Describes tension and anxiety as arising from “too much arousal,” and shows how helplessness in ADHD contributes to hyperactive energy.
    
- **Orlov, Melissa & Hallowell, Edward M.** _The ADHD Effect on Marriage_ (2010).  
    Describes the ADHD brain as “super-charged all the time,” prone to emotional overwhelm when time feels compressed.
    
- **Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne.** _Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them_ (2024).  
    Explains how ADHD often keeps the brain in fight-or-flight mode, contributing to chronic tension.
    
- **van der Kolk, Bessel.** _The Body Keeps the Score_ (2015).  
    Details how trauma and stress create lasting changes in the nervous system. Emphasizes how unprocessed emotions manifest physically.

## 🔍 Noticing & Naming Tension

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ (2022).  
    Recommends taking an “inventory of body sensations” to shift out of emotional reactivity. Helps translate tension into something tangible and manageable.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_ (2024).  
    Provides prompts for recognizing and labeling physical tension and the emotions behind it.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ (2025).  
    Introduces “affective labeling” as a way to acknowledge feelings and reduce their grip.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ (2024).  
    Emphasizes emotion labeling and using body awareness tools (e.g., feeling wheels, intensity scales) to process tension.
    
- **van der Kolk, Bessel.** _The Body Keeps the Score_ (2015).  
    States that “simply noticing” physical sensations like tension or anxiety can shift emotional state and improve regulation.

## 🌀 Regulating the Nervous System

- **Dee, Lisa.** _Healthy Happy ADHD_ (2025).  
    Describes how ADHD can keep the nervous system stuck in sympathetic overdrive. Recommends daily movement, sensory input, and body-based tools to discharge stress.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ (2022).  
    Suggests deep pressure, like swaddling or weighted blankets, to help deactivate high-alert states.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_ (2024).  
    Offers grounding and sensory strategies to soothe tension and re-engage the parasympathetic system.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_ (2024).  
    Recommends diaphragmatic breathing, yoga, and meditation to support a calm baseline.
    
- **Magness, Steve.** _Do Hard Things_ (2022).  
    Introduces “physical zooming” and reframing body signals as helpful cues rather than threats. Advocates deep breathing to calm negative sensations.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ (2024).  
    Shares strategies like box breathing, guided meditation, and grounding to manage hyperarousal.
    
- **Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne.** _Small Talk_ (2024).  
    Mentions “regulation cuddles” and other physical support tools to help ADHDers feel safe during moments of panic or tension.
    
- **van der Kolk, Bessel.** _The Body Keeps the Score_ (2015).  
    Recommends bottom-up strategies—breath, movement, touch—to shift the body out of stress response.

## 💭 Calming Mental Overload

- **Carder, Meredith.** _It All Makes Sense Now_ (2024).  
    Recommends reducing decision fatigue and simplifying environments to calm mental noise and relieve internal pressure.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ (2022).  
    Suggests clearing mental clutter with tools like brain dumps and starting fresh when digital overwhelm hits.
    
- **Forte, Tiago.** _Building a Second Brain_ (2022).  
    Encourages externalizing thoughts to reduce working memory strain and help the mind rest.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_ (2024).  
    Discusses chunking tasks and reframing negative thoughts to make the mind feel safer.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ (2025).  
    Offers reframing exercises and curiosity-driven prompts to break out of spiraling thought loops.
    
- **Levrini, Abigail L.** _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_ (2025).  
    Describes how “thinking errors” can intensify tension, and suggests thought-challenging tools.
    
- **Magness, Steve.** _Do Hard Things_ (2022).  
    Advocates reappraising stress signals and turning fear into clarity.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_ (2018).  
    Warns against multi-step to-dos and encourages single-action task lists to minimize overload.

## 🤝 Support & Boundaries

- **Dee, Lisa.** _Healthy Happy ADHD_ (2025).  
    Emphasizes the role of community connection in nervous system regulation. Recommends building regular co-regulation into your routine.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_ (2024).  
    Encourages letting go of tasks that don’t serve you and emphasizes the power of starting fresh when stuck.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_ (2024).  
    Notes that external support can anchor ADHDers, bringing clarity and emotional calm.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ (2024).  
    Introduces “micro-vulnerabilities” as a way to let people in without shame. Offers “Permission Slip” tools for rest and boundary-setting.
    
- **Orlov, Melissa & Hallowell, Edward M.** _The ADHD Effect on Marriage_ (2010).  
    Encourages seeking ADHD-informed community and professionals, and learning from others’ experiences.
    
- **Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne.** _Small Talk_ (2024).  
    Emphasizes the importance of safe spaces for ADHDers to unmask, regulate, and ask for help.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_ (2008).  
    Notes that ADHDers need structure, support, and self-compassion to function without collapsing into shame or burnout.
    
- **Solden, Sari, Frank, Michelle & Littman, Ellen.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_ (2019).  
    Focuses on removing shame and replacing it with acceptance and support. Encourages community as a buffer against internal tension.
