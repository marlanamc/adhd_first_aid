# 📚 Sources: Feeling Wired with ADHD

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of feeling “wired” and offer strategies for calming overstimulation, regulating adrenaline spikes, reframing urgency, and restoring a sense of internal safety and control.

---

## 🧠 Understanding the Wired State in ADHD

- **Barkley, Russell A.** _Taking Charge of Adult ADHD_ (2010).  
    A foundational text outlining how ADHD disrupts self-regulation, planning, and emotional control — core elements of the wired state.
    
- **Brown, Thomas E.** Executive function research (multiple works).  
    Describes the ADHD brain’s struggle to initiate, shift, and sustain attention — reinforcing how the “go-mode” can lack internal brakes.
    
- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_ (2015).  
    Offers insights into executive dysfunction — especially difficulty transitioning between tasks and managing internal urgency.
    
- **Hallowell, Edward M. & Ratey, John J.** _ADHD 2.0_ (2020).  
    Compares ADHD brains to “Ferraris with bicycle brakes,” describing the nervous and impulsive pace many ADHDers feel stuck in.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_ (2024).  
    Frames ADHD as a dysregulation disorder. Discusses how internal restlessness and overdrive often come from poor nervous system modulation.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_ (2024).  
    Focuses on proactive systems for planning and boundaries to help ADHDers feel safer and more grounded.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_ (2008).  
    Discusses how emotional storms, adrenaline loops, and chaotic thinking patterns can push ADHDers into wired cycles.
    
- **Solden, Sari & Frank, Michelle.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_ (2019).  
    Addresses the emotional toll of trying to “keep up” by staying wired — especially for women who mask or overfunction.

## 🔬 Neurological & Emotional Contributors to Feeling Wired

- **Bellocchi, Chiara, et al.** "The Interplay Between Autonomic Nervous System and Inflammation…" (2022).  
    Highlights how inflammation and nervous system dysregulation can intensify overstimulation.
    
- **Blum, Dani.** “The Truth About the Internet’s Favorite Stress Hormone.” _NYT_ (2023).  
    Explores cortisol’s role in stress and why ADHDers may stay stuck in fight-or-flight loops.
    
- **Clapp, Megan, et al.** "Gut Microbiota’s Effect on Mental Health…" (2017).  
    Explores how internal health can influence overstimulation, attention, and mood regulation.
    
- **David, Susan.** _Emotional Agility_ (2016).  
    Offers strategies for navigating reactive emotional states without getting swept up in them.
    
- **Ferranti, Anthony S., et al.** “GABAergic Dysfunction in ADHD.” _Neuropharmacology_ (2024).  
    Highlights GABA’s role in calming the brain — and how its deficiency can cause hyperarousal.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_ (2024).  
    Offers emotional mapping and regulation strategies for moments of internal chaos or shutdown.
    
- **Kempton, Matthew, et al.** “Psychosocial Stress and Dopaminergic Function.” _eLife_ (2019).  
    Explains how ADHDers often over-rely on adrenaline and dopamine spikes to feel motivated.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_ + related research (2024).  
    Describes curiosity as a way to reframe stimulation, and critiques hustle culture as an ADHD trap.
    
- **Lieberman, Matthew, et al.** "Putting Feelings into Words…” (2007).  
    Shows how naming emotions helps calm the amygdala and regulate reactivity.
    
- **Maté, Gabor.** _The Myth of Normal_ (2023).  
    Introduces the idea of “hyperfunctioning on top of hidden distress” — a perfect descriptor of being wired.
    
- **Neff, Megan Anna.** “The Autistic and ADHD Nervous System.” _Neurodivergent Insights_.  
    Discusses low vagal tone and how nervous systems stay stuck in survival mode.
    
- **Porges, Stephen.** _The Polyvagal Theory_ (2011).  
    Offers core theory on how the nervous system assesses safety vs. danger, impacting calm and reactivity.
    
- **Raichle, Marcus.** “The Brain’s Default Mode Network” (2015).  
    Explains why unstructured time or “stillness” can trigger thought spirals and mental noise in ADHD brains.
    
- **Strack dos Santos Gonçalves, J., et al.** "When Anxiety Boosts Motivation…” (2017).  
    Useful for understanding how anxiety and urgency can sometimes enhance — but often burn out — performance.
    
- **Westbrook, A., et al.** “Dopamine and Cognitive Effort.” _Science_ (2020).  
    Shows how dopamine spikes drive ADHDers to chase stimulation and “overdo” things, reinforcing the wired cycle.
    
- **Willroth, Emily C., et al.** “Judging Emotions as Good or Bad…” _Emotion_ (2023).  
    Reminds us that rejecting certain emotions (like tension or anxiety) can intensify them.

## 🛠️ Strategies to Unwind the Wired Feeling

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_ (2022).  
    Offers tools like “Grounding Countdown,” “Draw Your Feels,” and “Self-Compassion Pep Talks” to help ADHDers downshift from overwhelm.
    
- **Frankl, Viktor E.** _Man’s Search for Meaning_ (2006).  
    Encourages reframing suffering and finding purpose — helpful when wired states feel empty or pointless.
    
- **Kegan, Robert & Lahey, Lisa.** _Immunity to Change_ (2009).  
    Helps uncover hidden beliefs that keep ADHDers stuck in constant over-functioning.
    
- **Lakshmin, Pooja.** _Real Self-Care_ (2023).  
    Teaches boundary-setting, guilt reduction, and redefining success — all crucial for unhooking from hustle mode.
    
- **Magness, Steve.** _Do Hard Things_ (2022).  
    Offers exercises for calming the mind and decoding emotional signals instead of bulldozing through discomfort.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_ (2024).  
    Packed with emotion-regulation tools, planning strategies, and personal stories to help ADHDers feel less alone and more in control.
    
- **Miller, William R. & Rollnick, Stephen.** _Motivational Interviewing_ (2023).  
    Offers tools like “decisional balance grids” and gentle reflection to guide behavior change from within.
    
- **Ratey, John J. & Hagerman, Eric.** _Spark_ (2013).  
    Describes the neurological benefits of exercise — a natural way to release adrenaline and regulate stimulation.
    
- **Speranza, Luisa, et al.** “Music and Brain Connectivity.” _Reviews in the Neurosciences_ (2022).  
    Supports the use of music as a sensory anchor for regulation and mood shifts.
    
- **van der Kolk, Bessel.** _The Body Keeps the Score_ (2015).  
    Offers bottom-up regulation strategies like yoga, breathwork, and movement to restore nervous system balance.
