### **The Brain’s Management Tools**

> These are your brain’s behind-the-scenes tools for managing tasks, emotions, attention, and decisions—the systems that help you move from being stuck to taking action.
> 

| **Term** | **What It Means** |
| --- | --- |
| **Executive Function**  | Mental processes that help with planning, initiating, focusing, remembering, and regulating |
| **Task Initiation** | The ability to *start* a task without getting stuck in overwhelm or avoidance |
| **Working Memory** | Holding info in your brain while using it (e.g., following steps without forgetting mid-process)  |
| **Cognitive Flexibility** | Switching between tasks or ideas, like adapting when plans change |
| **Emotional Regulation** | Managing emotional responses without spiraling or freezing |
| **Inhibition** | Resisting distractions or impulses (e.g., “don’t scroll!”) |
</aside>

### Inattentive-Specific Challenges

> These patterns show up when attention drifts, mental energy dips, or activation is hard to spark without novelty or urgency.
> 

| **Term** | **What It Means** |
| --- | --- |
| **Mental Fog** | Feeling like your brain is “buffering,” slow to engage, or hard to direct |
| **Time Blindness** | Losing track of time or not feeling the urgency until it’s too late |
| **Drift** | Zoning out mid-task or forgetting what you were just doing |
| **Task Abandonment** | Starting things, then wandering off or losing steam without closure |
| **Activation Deficit** | Knowing what you need to do—but physically can’t start without external input |
| **Dopamine Seeking** | Needing novelty, urgency, or interest to feel “turned on” mentally |
</aside>

### **Hyperactive-Specific Challenges**

> These patterns reflect when energy, movement, or impulsivity runs high, leading to cycles of overdrive and burnout.
> 

| **Term** | **What It Means** |
| --- | --- |
| Motor Restlessness | Feeling physically agitated; needing to move, fidget, or engage the body to stay regulated |
| Verbal Impulsivity | Speaking quickly or out of turn; interrupting or blurting without intending to |
| Sensory Seeking | Craving stimulation through noise, touch, movement, or novelty to stay engaged |
| Task Hopping | Jumping rapidly between tasks or activities without finishing |
| Emotional Reactivity | Strong, fast emotional responses that feel disproportionate or hard to rein back in |
| Burnout Cycles | Surging into projects with intense energy, then crashing into exhaustion or shutdown |
| Overcommitting | Saying “yes” to too many things, driven by excitement or a desire to please, then feeling overwhelmed later |
</aside>

### Emotional & Social Challenges

> These internal patterns shape how we handle criticism, relationships, shame, and the emotional labor of appearing “together.”
> 

| **Term** | **What It Means** |
| --- | --- |
| Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD) | Extreme emotional pain or panic in response to perceived rejection, criticism, or failure—even if imagined |
| Masking | Hiding symptoms, needs, or true feelings to appear “functional,” “normal,” or more socially acceptable |
| Shame Spiral | Intense self-blame or hopelessness after small mistakes, inconsistency, or perceived failures |
| Perfectionism Loop | Getting stuck in avoidance or over-editing because nothing feels “good enough” to finish |
| Emotional Flooding | Feeling overwhelmed by intense emotions that derail focus or motivation |
| Social Exhaustion | Fatigue after long periods of masking or people-pleasing, leading to shutdown or isolation |
</aside>

### Cognitive & Sensory Experiences

> These describe how the ADHD brain interacts with thoughts, decisions, and the sensory world—sometimes amplifying or freezing under input.
> 

| **Term** | **What It Means** |
| --- | --- |
| Sensory Overload | Feeling overwhelmed or agitated by too much noise, light, movement, or sensory input |
| Hyperfocus | Becoming intensely absorbed in one task or topic, sometimes losing awareness of time or surroundings |
| Analysis Paralysis | Overthinking decisions or steps so much that you become stuck, unable to act or choose |
| Thought Ping-Pong | Rapid bouncing between ideas, making it hard to hold onto one train of thought |
| Low Frustration Tolerance | Quick to feel irritated or upset when tasks are challenging or progress is blocked |
</aside>

### **Support Tools & Techniques**

> These are strategies and systems that help externalize tasks, reduce overwhelm, and create movement when internal resources feel low.
> 

| **Term** | **What It Means** |
| --- | --- |
| **Body Doubling** | Doing a task alongside someone (even silently) to help initiate/focus |
| **Externalizing Systems** | Using visuals, calendars, alarms to “offload” memory and mental steps |
| **Scaffolding** | Adding simple supports (e.g., a checklist or step-by-step plan) to build success |
| **Anchoring** | Linking a habit to something already in your routine (e.g., “after coffee…”) |
| **Low-Activation Task** | A task that requires almost no setup, pressure, or planning—just motion |
| **Nonlinear Progress** | Emphasizing that success may look like spirals, not straight lines |
</aside>
