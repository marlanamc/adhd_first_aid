# Life Vest: Resource Guide

### What Is the Life Vest?

### “Can I offer myself grace before I try to ‘fixʼ anything?ˮ

```
Your Life Vest is your internal safety net. When youʼre overwhelmed, ashamed,
or stuck, this is your first move. Itʼs how you soften the story in your head so
you can move forward without spiraling.
```
### Why it Matters

```
ADHD brains are wired for perfectionism, RSD, and shame spirals.
Shame doesnʼt motivate — it paralyzes.
Skipping self-compassion = burnout, avoidance, shutdown.
When you lead with care, you unlock actual momentum.
```
### Why ADHD Brains Need This

```
ADHD brains often carry years of messaging like “Youʼre lazyˮ or “You just
need to try harder.ˮ This creates a shame loop — where every struggle feels
like a character flaw instead of a pattern.
Hereʼs whatʼs really going on:
ADHD brains are sensitive to rejection and failure , so we avoid starting to
avoid messing up
We often have emotional intensity that makes little things feel huge
```
Life Vest: Resource Guide 1


```
We feel guilt about being “behindˮ and assume kindness = giving up
But self-compassion isnʼt permission to quit. Itʼs the reset button that actually
lets you start.
When you feel stuck or ashamed, your brain doesnʼt need pressure. It needs
safety. Thatʼs what the Life Vest gives you — a mental and emotional
grounding before anything else.
```
### When to Use this Level

```
At the start of every session or solo check-in
Before big transitions (starting work, leaving the house, switching tasks)
After a shame spiral (scrolling, missing a deadline, snapping at someone)
During stuck moments (when you canʼt start, canʼt focus, or feel gross)
```
### Core Practice: Create Your Own Mantra

```
Choose one that feels honest — not cheesy. This is a voice you can believe
when youʼre underwater.
Examples I offer to you:
“My mess isnʼt a moral failure.ˮ
“Being human is allowed here.ˮ
“Iʼm allowed to start from this moment.ˮ
“Remembering you are enough in this moment, just as you are, simply by
being.ˮ
```
### Make it Visible

```
Where can you place your mantra so it actually helps?
Sticky note on your mirror
Phone lock screen
Whiteboard near your desk
Inside your logbook
```
Life Vest: Resource Guide 2


```
Seeing it is what makes it interrupt the spiral.
```
### How it Connects to Other Levels

```
You canʼt reach your Destination if your nervous system is underwater.
You canʼt use your Compass when youʼre lost in shame fog.

# Ration: Resource Guide

### What Is the Ration?

### “What does my brain and body need before I ask them to

### perform?ˮ

```
Ration is the pause before the push. ADHD often shows up as dysregulation,
not laziness. This level helps you check your fuel tank and tend to your
nervous system before jumping into tasks. You donʼt need to push harder —
you might just need to pause smarter.
```
### Why It Matters

```
ADHD brains struggle with regulation and awareness of internal needs (like
hunger, thirst, or tension).
Dysregulation = shutdown, avoidance, or spirals.
You canʼt access motivation or focus when your nervous system is in
distress.
Caring for your body helps unlock the mental energy youʼve been waiting
for.
```
### Why ADHD Brains Need This

```
ADHD brains often struggle with interoception , which means itʼs easy to miss
basic needs, like hunger, thirst, tension, or fatigue, until they hit hard.
Hereʼs whatʼs happening under the surface:
```
Ration: Resource Guide 1


```
Youʼre not avoiding the task, youʼre running on empty
The brain needs regulation before it can access focus, motivation, or logic
We often try to “push throughˮ discomfort without noticing itʼs a fuel issue ,
not a willpower issue
ADHD also means we often disconnect from our bodyʼs signals, especially if
weʼre in fight, flight, freeze, or fawn. Ration brings you back into your body and
helps you meet your needs before trying to force productivity.
You donʼt need to earn regulation. You need it to begin.
```
### When to Use This Level

```
Before you start work, errands, or transitions
When you feel off and donʼt know why
When you're stuck, shut down, or avoiding tasks
When youʼre overstimulated, drained, or scattered
After something emotional, stressful, or chaotic
```
### Quick Regulation Check-In

```
Ask yourself:
Have I eaten recently?
Have I had any water?
Are my meds active or kicking in soon?
Is my body tense, sore, or jittery?
Would a stretch, walk, or shake help right now?
Whatʼs the sensory vibe — too loud, too quiet, too bright?
Do I need music, silence, a comfort show, or some nature?
```
### Brain Dumping as Regulation

```
Sometimes the thing dysregulating you is internal noise. Thatʼs where brain
dumping helps.
```

```
A brain dump is a fast, unfiltered list of everything on your mind: to-dos,
worries, reminders, ideas, rants. Youʼre not organizing yet. Youʼre offloading
pressure.
Why it works:
Clears mental clutter
Reduces overwhelm
Helps name the things causing shutdown
Creates space to move forward
You can do it on paper, Notion, or voice memo — whateverʼs easiest.
```
### Try this:

```
“Whatʼs swirling in my brain right now?ˮ
Brain dump for 2 minutes. No judgment. No fixing.
```
### Regulation Toolkit Ideas

```
Create your own list of mini resets. Here are a few to try:
Warm drink or cold water
Lie down and breathe slowly for 60 seconds
Step outside or open a window
Listen to a grounding song
Pet an animal or hug a pillow
Stretch, shake, or walk around the room
Put on cozy clothes or remove an annoying layer
Rewatch a favorite scene or calming video
```
### How It Connects to Other Levels

```
You canʼt follow the Compass if your nervous system is in panic mode.
