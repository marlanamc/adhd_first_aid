## 💔 ADHD First Aid Template: Rejected
It’s not overreacting • It’s not being needy • It’s ADHD meeting Rejection Sensitivity Dysphoria, emotional intensity, and a nervous system wired for threat.

When you feel rejected, it doesn’t just sting — it _burns_. A tone shift, a delayed reply, a perceived criticism — it can spiral into shame, panic, or withdrawal. ADHD often comes with RSD: Rejection Sensitivity Dysphoria, where even small slights feel like deep wounds. And once it hits, it’s hard to come back to center.

This isn’t just emotional — it’s neurological. And you’re not weak for feeling it.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Rejection hits harder for you — that’s real. ADHD often comes with RSD, and it’s not your fault. Let yourself feel it, but don’t let it rewrite your worth. You’re still lovable. Still capable. Still needed.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You’re not broken — you’re bruised. Rejection is painful, but it’s not a death sentence. Feel it, then move. Don’t let one “no” silence everything else you could be saying yes to.

### 🧠 Why ADHD Amplifies Rejection:

- 💥 Rejection Sensitivity Dysphoria (RSD) makes perceived slights feel unbearable
    
- 🔁 Emotional dysregulation can turn a small moment into a multi-day spiral
    
- 🧠 Overthinking and working memory loops replay rejection on a loop
    
- 😶 Masking and people-pleasing lead to identity confusion and fear of being “too much”
    
- 🧱 Past experiences of being misunderstood or punished for being different build emotional armor — or walls

---

### 1. 🧠 Recognize the Pain Without Letting It Take Over

Rejection — real or imagined — can feel physically painful. That’s not an exaggeration. Your brain interprets it as a threat, and for people with ADHD and RSD, it can hit _hard_.

**Try this:**

- 💬 Name what’s happening: “My RSD just flared up” or “This feels like I’m being erased.”
    
- 🫀 Notice the physical sensations: hollow chest, clenched jaw, nausea, heavy limbs?
    
- 📝 Dump the spiral: Write or speak out the swirl of thoughts before they spiral further. Get it _out_ of your head.
    

> 💡Tip: Feeling rejected doesn’t always mean you _were_ rejected. Your brain is protecting you from pain — even if it misreads the threat.

---

### 2. 🔍 Challenge the Story in Your Head

Rejection often leads to a nasty internal narrative. Let’s rewrite it.

**Try this:**

- ⚖️ “Take it to court”: Is this _really_ true? Can you prove it? Can you prove the opposite?
    
- 🔁 Flip the script: What if this isn’t rejection, but miscommunication? Or someone else’s bad mood?
    
- 🧠 Interrupt harsh thoughts: When your brain says, “They hate you,” try: “I’m having the thought that they hate me.”
    
- 📣 Say something kind about yourself _out loud_, even if it feels cheesy.
    

> 💡Tip: Your brain may be doing _defensive storytelling_. Just because it _feels_ real doesn’t mean it _is_ real.

---

### 3. 🗣 Communicate with Clarity (and Courage)

Misunderstandings often feed rejection cycles. Speak up — even if your voice shakes.

**Try this:**

- 🧍 “I” statements: “When you didn’t text back, I felt unsure if you were upset.”
    
- 🧩 Explain your needs + offer a solution: “I process things slowly. Can we talk tomorrow instead?”
    
- 🧭 Ask direct questions if you’re ruminating: “Hey, just checking — are we good?”
    
- 👂 Practice deep listening too: You want to be understood? Show them how it’s done.
    

> 💡Tip: You don’t need to explain your whole life story. Just enough context to create clarity, not confusion.

---

### 4. 🚫 Hold Boundaries + Learn to Tolerate Discomfort

You are not required to earn belonging by shape-shifting into what others prefer.

**Try this:**

- 🔒 Say no without apology. That’s a full sentence.
    
- 🧱 Assert your needs: “I need quiet time after work,” “I prefer texts over calls.”
    
- 😬 Practice letting someone be disappointed in you — and still being okay.
    
- 💬 When asking for help, name what kind of support you want: venting, advice, validation?
    

> 💡Tip: You don’t need everyone to like you. You need a few safe people who get you — and that starts with you respecting your own limits.

---

### 5. 💗 Rebuild Trust in Yourself + Find Real Connection

Rejection breeds isolation. Let’s rewrite that story too.

**Try this:**

- 🧸 Be kind to yourself — _especially_ when you feel most unlovable.
    
- 🌱 Find your people: Look for spaces (online or in-person) where being ADHD, sensitive, or quirky is _normal_.
    
- 🔐 Share small truths: Try a “micro-vulnerability” — a feeling, a hope, a fear. Let people see the real you, one layer at a time.
    
- 🔄 Let others co-regulate with you: Even a hug, shared playlist, or walk together can help reset your nervous system.
    

> 💡Tip: You are allowed to ask, “Can I be real with you?” and choose people who say, “Yes, please.”

---

### 🔁 Starting Point Suggestions

→ Text someone: “Hey, I’m feeling kind of off and just wanted to say hi.”  
→ Join a community where people _already understand_ neurodivergence.  
→ Write a letter to your younger self — the version who thought they had to be perfect to be loved.
