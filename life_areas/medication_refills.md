## 💊 Medication Refills

Refilling meds seems simple, but ADHD turns it into a maze of **timing**, **logistics**, and **executive function strain**. You might forget the date, lose the bottle, or avoid the task entirely. This isn’t about being irresponsible,  it’s your brain hitting friction at every step of the process.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not **irresponsible**,  you're managing a **brain** that **forgets** the thing keeping it **functioning**. Set **refill alarms**. Use a **tracker**. Let **someone help** if needed. You deserve **consistent care** without **shame**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You cannot keep **playing chicken** with your **prescriptions**. **ADHD** makes you **forget**,  that's **real**. But it doesn't **excuse** running out **every month**. Set **recurring reminders**. **Call the pharmacy**. **Take control** of your **treatment**.

### 🧠 Why Medication Refills Feel So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Forget to call the pharmacy until the day you run out
  - Lose the pill bottle or can't remember the name of your meds
  - Procrastinate calling because it feels awkward or confusing
  - Assume you'll remember later (but you don't)
  - Get overwhelmed by all the steps and avoid the task entirely

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes planning, initiating, and follow-through harder  
  - 📦 **Working memory issues** mean key steps slip your mind  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** makes deadlines feel invisible until it's too late  
  - 💥 **Low dopamine** makes boring, high-friction tasks hard to start  
  - 🌪️ **Overwhelm and decision fatigue** shut your brain down mid-process  
  - 📞 **Phone call avoidance** adds another layer of resistance  
  - 📉 **Shame or fear of judgment** (especially around stimulant meds) can block action  
  - 🧱 **Pharmacy systems** often create barriers that are harder for ADHD brains to navigate

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Refills

- **You are not a burden for needing medication**: Many ADHDers report feeling judged or dismissed when refilling stimulant prescriptions. You deserve consistent care, and you are worth advocating for
- **Externalize everything**: Your brain is not a pharmacy calendar
- **Build in buffer time**: Don't wait until the last pill
- **Simplify the process**: Remove as many steps as possible

### 🛠️ Strategies for Medication Refills

#### 📱 Externalize Reminders

- **Set recurring alarms**: In your phone for 5-7 days before you run out
- **Use a medication tracking app**: Medisafe, MyTherapy, or similar

- **Flip your pill bottle upside down**: Or rubber band it when it's time to refill
- **Ask your pharmacy to text or call**: When a refill is due
- **Write the refill date**: On a calendar or sticky note where you actually look

#### 🔨 Break It Down

Don't just write "refill meds" on your to-do list. Break it into micro-steps:
- Check how many pills are left
- Find the bottle or prescription number
- Call or message pharmacy
- Contact prescriber if no refills
- Confirm pickup or delivery time
- Set an alarm to go get it

Each step = one small action your brain can handle.

#### 👥 Use Support Systems

- **Body doubling**: Have someone sit with you while you call
- **Accountability buddy**: Ask someone to remind or check in with you
- **Pharmacy support**: Many can set standing reminders or sync all your meds
- **Scripts**: Use pre-written notes or saved templates to reduce overwhelm

#### ⚡ Reduce Friction

- **Keep meds in a visible place**: Don't hide them in cabinets
- **Store contact info**: Doctor/pharmacy numbers in your notes app
- **Use a pill organizer**: With labeled days to see when you're running low

- **When possible, ask about 90-day supplies**: To reduce refill frequency

#### 💭 Mindset Shifts

- **"Done is better than perfect"**: It doesn't have to be smooth, just done
- **Refilling meds is part of care**: Not a chore to resent
- **You are not failing**: Because this part is hard. You're learning to work with your brain

#### 💊 A Note on Shame and Stimulants

- **Many ADHDers report feeling judged**: When picking up stimulant meds
- **Pharmacies may ask extra questions**: Or treat you differently
- **You are not doing anything wrong**: You shouldn't have to feel ashamed for managing your health
- **You are worth fighting for**: Keep advocating, even when it's frustrating
- **You deserve medication, support, and respect**

### 🧭 Medication Refills ≠ Character Test

- Refills aren't a moral issue,  they're a logistics challenge your brain needs support with
- You deserve consistent care without shame or judgment
- Automate what you can, ask for help when you need to

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> Every refill you complete is an act of self-care. You're not failing at medication management,  you're learning to work with your brain, not against it.
