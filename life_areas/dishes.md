## 🍽️ Dishes

Dishes pile up because they're never truly "done." For ADHD brains, that endless loop,  plus the **gross factor**, **boring repetition**, and **lack of closure**,  makes it incredibly hard to start. You're not broken,  you're reacting to a task that never ends and rarely rewards.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Dishes pile up fast, especially when **ADHD** makes task transitions feel enormous. You're not lazy. Start with just the silverware or soak everything. Use gloves, music, whatever helps. A few clean dishes are better than none.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
This isn't optional. Dishes aren't just annoying, they can become a **health issue**. Mold, flies, and bacteria don't wait until you're motivated. Your kitchen deserves basic care, and so do you.

### 🧠 Why Dishes Feel So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Feel dread just walking into the kitchen
  - Avoid dishes until the pile becomes overwhelming
  - Get stuck in shame loops that make starting even harder
  - Start loading and suddenly start organizing the spice rack instead
  - Feel grossed out or overstimulated by soggy food, strong smells, or water on your wrists

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes planning, sequencing, and follow-through harder  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** makes it feel like "it'll take forever"  
  - 💥 **Low dopamine** makes the task feel boring and unimportant  
  - 🔁 **Perfectionism and shame** lead to procrastination or shutdown  
  - 🧠 **Working memory issues** make you forget what step you're on mid-task  
  - 🎧 **Sensory sensitivity** can make it physically uncomfortable,  textures, smells, noise, or splashy water on your skin

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Dishes

- **Design your system around your brain, not around shame**
- **Small steps count**: Even rinsing one plate is progress
- **Function over perfection**: A clean sink is enough
- **Reduce the total dish load**: Fewer dishes = less overwhelm
- **Good enough is good enough**: It doesn't need to look Instagram-worthy

### 🛠️ Strategies for Dishes

#### 🧠 Self-Compassion & Reframing

- **You're not lazy**: Dishes are an executive function task
- **Talk back to shame**: "This is hard for me, and that's okay."
- **Rhythm, not routine**: If you skip a beat, you can still get back in the groove
- **This is a loop, not a flaw**: Your brain isn't broken for struggling with "simple" tasks

#### 🔨 Micro-Stepping & Momentum

- **Set the stage first**: Put on a hype song, light a candle, or eat something sweet
- **Deconstruct the task**: Stack dishes by category → Clear sink space → Wash one group at a time
- **Move toward it**: Can't start? Just go stand near the sink. That counts.

#### 🍽️ Simplify the System

- **Reduce dish load**: Use fewer cups per day, try "one dish per person", paper plates are survival tools during tough times
- **Purge dish clutter**: Get rid of duplicate mugs or anything that creates extra work
- **Limit sink access**: Use a designated dish tub to separate dirty dishes from your sink

#### 🎧 Sensory Hacks

- **Wear dish gloves** to block wet textures
- **Use long-handled scrub brushes** so you don't have to touch dishes directly
- **Soak crusty dishes first** to avoid scrubbing trauma
- **Keep a towel handy** if water on your arms drives you up the wall
- **Play music or white noise** to reduce overstimulating clatter and splash sounds

#### 🏡 Environmental Design

- **Create visual stations**: "Dirty Dish Station" (dish rack, bin, or tub), extra silverware holder for overflow
- **Store cleaning tools where you use them**: Sponge, soap, gloves under each sink
- **Unload dishwasher first thing in the morning**: So it's ready for dirty dishes during the day
- **Open shelving can help** if you're a visual thinker who forgets what's hidden
- **Dawn Powerwash Spray** can be a game-changer,  just spray and rinse. No scrubbing, no buildup of dread

#### 🎮 Motivation & Accountability

- **Gamify it**: Set a 5- or 10-minute timer and race yourself, narrate like a cleaning vlogger, keep a "dish streak" tally
- **Prep a reset playlist**: 10–15 minutes of motivating audio you only use while cleaning
- **Leave a visible dish-free zone** (like one side of the sink) to build a sense of closure
- **Use body doubling**: Have someone sit with you (in person or via video) while you do it
- **Do it for Future You**: Clean dishes = easier meals tomorrow = less stress later

#### 🧍 Support & Boundaries

- **Ask for co-regulation**: "Can you sit with me while I load the dishwasher?"
- **Set expectations clearly**: "I'm building a new system that works for me"
- **Use scripts when needed**: "Thanks for your concern, but I'm not taking feedback on this issue right now"
- **Outsource if possible**: Hiring dish help or swapping chores is a valid accommodation

### 🧭 Dishes ≠ Morality

- The number of dishes in your sink is not a reflection of your worth, your capability, or your maturity
- Some people use doing dishes to unwind. Others find it deeply stressful. Both are valid
- You deserve systems that support your brain,  even if they look unconventional
> A clean-ish plate to eat off of and a clear-ish sink to start from tomorrow is enough

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> If you rinsed one spoon, that's momentum. If you organized the pile and walked away, that's a win. If you just stood near the sink today, that's a start. You don't have to feel good about the mess to start,  every effort counts, and you are making progress, one step at a time.
