## 🚗 Car Maintenance

Car tasks pile up not because you’re careless, but because ADHD brains struggle with **future-oriented tasks**, **logistics**, and **time estimation**. Oil changes, inspections, and renewals all require initiating action without urgency, which is exactly where ADHD hits hardest.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not **irresponsible**, your brain just doesn't **track time** or **preventative tasks** well. But your car doesn't run on **good intentions**. **ADHD** is linked to **higher accident rates** and **missed maintenance** only adds **risk**. Set **recurring reminders**. Ask for **help** with **scheduling** if you need it. **Staying safe** isn't a luxury, it's a **need**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
This is about **safety**, not just **inconvenience**. **ADHD** is linked to **higher rates** of **crashes** and **critical driving errors**. **Ignoring** your car's needs puts you and others at **risk**. **Missed maintenance** can turn **deadly**. **Check your brakes**. **Change the oil**. You don't get to **gamble with lives** because you **forgot again**.

### ⚓ Core Principles for Car Ownership with ADHD

- **Prevent panic with planning**: Systems reduce chaos  
- **Externalize everything**: Don’t rely on memory for important details  
- **Don’t aim for perfection**: Aim for “safe and functional”  
- **Break it down**: “Car maintenance” is not one task, it’s many  
- **Support is allowed**: Outsourcing and body doubling count

### 🛠️ Strategies for Car Maintenance

#### 🗂️ Set Up a System You Can See

- **Dedicated Car Folder**: Keep all docs, registration, insurance, repairs, in one labeled folder in your glovebox or home  
- **Receipt Baggy**: Stash a small ziplock for car-related receipts in your glove box. Review monthly  
- **Visible Stickers or Whiteboard**: Write due dates for oil changes, inspections, or tags on a sticky note in your car or planner  
- **Car Note in Your Phone**: Keep a digital list of what’s been done and what’s due next (apps like Notion, Apple Notes, or even your calendar)  

#### ⏱️ Time, Triggers & Scheduling

- **Monthly Car Ritual**: Choose one day per month (e.g., first Sunday) to check tire pressure, mileage, and dashboard lights  
- **Set Recurring Reminders**: Use Google Calendar, TickTick, or Apple Reminders with custom alerts like “Book inspection – 2 weeks before due”  
- **Link to Paychecks**: Tie certain tasks (like gas fill-ups or washes) to payday routines  
- **Chunk Large Tasks**: Break down “get car serviced” into 3–5 steps: find mechanic → call → clean car → schedule drop-off  

#### 🔋 Avoid Shutdown & Overwhelm

- **Overestimate Time**: Assume everything will take 30 minutes longer than expected  
- **Pre-Decide the Where & Who**: Have a go-to mechanic, tire place, and car wash saved to reduce decision fatigue  
- **Create an Essentials Kit**: Emergency bag in your trunk with jumper cables, flashlight, wipes, phone charger, and tire gauge  

#### 🧠 Emotional Regulation & Avoidance

- **Use a Reset Ritual**: When facing an overdue task, play music, move your body, or text a friend to reset shame spirals  
- **Practice Self-Compassion**: It’s better to face it late than keep hiding from it  
- **Visual Cueing**: Put a sticky note on your debit card: “Car overdue?” or “Check oil this week?”  

#### 🤝 Support Systems & Delegation

- **Body Double for Scheduling**: Ask a friend to sit with you while you book appointments  
- **Outsource Where You Can**: Use mobile mechanics, automatic car washes, or professional detailing  
- **Let Someone Else Be the Car Brain**: If you have a trusted partner or family member, let them hold the timeline and just tell you what to do and when  
- **Find Accountability Buddies**: “Car Check Saturdays”, remind each other monthly with a quick check-in text
