## 🧺 Laundry

Laundry with ADHD isn't just one chore, it's a loop of **incomplete steps**, and folding is often where everything stalls. Your brain might **forget a load**, **freeze at folding**, or **delay putting things away** for days. It's not laziness, it's the toll of **executive dysfunction**, **boredom paralysis**, and **decision fatigue** stacking up.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Laundry isn't one task, it's five. Sorting, washing, drying, folding, putting away. No wonder it's **overwhelming**. **Break it into steps**. Celebrate each stage. Even one load done is a **win**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
Stop cycling the same load five times and calling it **progress**. Wash it. Dry it. Fold it. Put it away. This isn't mysterious. You don't need motivation, you need **momentum**.

### 🧠 Why Laundry Feels So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Want clean clothes but never feel like you can "start"
  - Forget a load in the washer until it smells
  - Feel shame about your space or your system
  - Keep clean clothes in a basket for weeks because folding feels impossible

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes starting, switching, and completing tasks harder  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** makes it easy to forget loads or underestimate how long things take  
  - 🌀 **Working memory issues** = "I forgot I even started laundry"  
  - 🔁 **Perfectionism, shame, and overwhelm** make finishing harder than starting  
  - 💡 You've likely internalized **unrealistic or judgmental ideas** of what laundry "should" look like

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Laundry

- **You're not lazy**: You're navigating a system mismatch, not a moral failure  
- **Progress counts**: Getting the clothes washed = success, even if they stay in a basket  
- **Simplify the process**: Make it easy to start, easier to finish  
- **Design around your energy, not a "perfect system"**  
- **Flexible rhythms > rigid routines**: If you skip a beat, you can still get back in the groove

### 🛠️ Strategies for Laundry

#### 🧠 Self-Compassion & Mindset

- **"No one ever shamed themselves into better mental health"**: Laundry isn't a reflection of your worth
- **Good enough = functional enough**: Your goal is clean clothes when you need them. That's it.
- **Progress counts**: Getting the clothes washed = success, even if they stay in a basket
- **Flexible rhythms > rigid routines**: If you skip a beat, you can still get back in the groove

#### 🧼 Simplify the System

- **Reduce the volume**: Fewer clothes = less laundry. Donate or store items you don't wear regularly
- **Optimize the space**: Keep hampers where clothes actually come off. Store supplies in reach (detergent, dryer sheets, stain pens)
- **Question old rules**: Folding is optional. If folding leads to procrastination, skip it. Toss clothes into labeled bins, use shower curtain rings to hang items, or create "grab & go" outfit bundles
- **Use no-fold systems**: Assign a basket per person or per clothing category. "Done" = clean, dry, and tossed where you'll find it later
- **Wash by category**: One load per person or per item type. No post-wash sorting = less mental effort
- **Make one better choice**: Missed the dryer window? Restart the wash and try again. Can't fold? At least move it from the machine to the basket

#### 🔨 Break It Down

- **Chunk the steps**: Break it into micro-tasks: Put clothes in hamper → Bring hamper to laundry room → Start wash → Switch to dryer → Dump in basket → Put away top 3 items
- **Find the smallest point of entry**: Just walk to the washer. Just start the water. Just put one thing in
- **Create checklists**: A visual step-by-step keeps working memory from getting overloaded
- **Use body doubling**: Invite someone to sit with you, or hop on a video call. Laundry chat sessions can help make it less isolating

#### ⏰ Manage Time & Attention

- **Set timers**: Use alarms to remind you to switch loads. Set a final alarm to get stuff out before bedtime
- **Track how long it actually takes**: Laundry feels like it takes forever, but you might be surprised. Knowledge combats overwhelm and avoidance
- **Try laundry rhythms**: Example: one load every other day, or "towels Tuesdays". Keep it flexible, if you miss a day, just pick it up again
- **Pair with something enjoyable**: Laundry = time to watch a show, listen to a podcast, or scroll guilt-free. Make it a ritual you look forward to
- **Use rewards**: "After I start the wash, I'll have coffee" or "When the basket's empty, I can buy that new candle"
- **Be kind to Future You**: "It would be so nice if I had clean clothes for tomorrow." Reframing laundry as care instead of punishment boosts motivation

#### 🧍 Support & Delegation

- **Delegate or share the load**: Assign each person their own day or their own basket. Partner with roommates or family and rotate tasks
- **Outsource if needed**: Wash-and-fold services exist for a reason. If it saves you spoons, it's a valid use of your money
- **Ask for specific help**: "Can you sit with me while I fold?" or "Can you carry the basket while I sort?"
- **Name your needs clearly**: "I'm trying to keep up, but this part is really hard for me." You don't need to apologize for how your brain works
- **ADHD coaching**: Get help building customized systems that feel natural, not forced. Coaches can help you problem-solve and practice self-kindness

### 🧭 Laundry ≠ Morality

- You don't have to fold everything. You don't have to get it all done today
- You are allowed to leave it unfolded. You are allowed to try again tomorrow
- Your worth is not measured by how long your socks stay paired
- You're not lazy, you're navigating a system mismatch, not a moral failure
> Laundry can be "good enough" at every stage

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> If you ran the washer, that's momentum. If you moved the pile, that's progress. If you just stood in the laundry room and sighed, that's a start. You are not a failure, you're building a system that works with your brain.
