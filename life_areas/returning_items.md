## 📦 Returning Items

Returning items often involves more steps than expected,  tracking receipts, packaging, deadlines, and transportation. With ADHD, these kinds of **multi-step tasks** can trigger **avoidance**, **forgetfulness**, and **decision fatigue**. It's not carelessness,  it's your brain protecting itself from tasks that feel disproportionately draining.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not **wasteful**,  your brain just doesn't **track deadlines** well. **Returns** involve **steps**, **effort**, and **social energy**. Make it **easier**: keep **receipts visible**, put **items by the door**, and set **calendar reminders**. You can do this **one return** at a time.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're **losing money**. That **item** sitting in the **box** is **costing you**,  in **cash** and **clutter**. **Print the label**. **Bag it up**. **Drop it off**. Stop letting **$40 go to waste** over a **short task**.

### 🧠 Why Returning Items Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Toss the receipt and forget the return window
  - Leave the item in the box for weeks
  - Feel shame or anxiety about returning it
  - Miss the deadline, then spiral about the lost money
  - Feel overwhelmed by all the little steps

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes multi-step tasks feel paralyzing  
  - 🔁 **Working memory issues** mean you forget where the item or receipt even is  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** causes you to underestimate how long you have or how long it’ll take  
  - 😰 **Shame and fear** from past mistakes or imagined conflict at the store  
  - ⚡ **Low dopamine** makes even easy returns feel unbearably boring  
  - 📦 **Perfectionism** stalls you if you can’t do it “all at once” or “the right way”

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Returns 

- **Stop the inflow**: Before buying, ask "Where will this live?"
- **Build a return station**: Bag by the door with supplies ready
- **Break it way down**: "Return item" becomes 6 tiny steps
- **Time it, don't dread it**: 5-minute timers and calendar reminders
- **Progress counts**: Moving item to the door is still progress

### 🛠️ Strategies for Returning Items

#### 📥 Stop the Inflow

- **Before buying ask**: "Where will this live?" to reduce future regrets
- **"Pause Rule"**: Wait 24 hours before buying non-essentials
- **Shop mindfully**: Avoid the ADHD tax or delete apps entirely

#### 👜 Build a "Return Station"

- **Designate a bag or box by your door**: Just for returns
- **As soon as you decide to return**: Put item, receipt, and return label in station
- **Write deadline on sticky note**: Attach it to the item
- **Keep return supplies handy**: Tape, printer paper, scissors

#### 🧠 Break It Way Down

- **Tiny actions**: Find receipt → Put item in bag → Print return label → Tape up box → Set alarm → Go to store
- **Write it down**: Like instructions to a robot with no memory
- **One step at a time**: Don't try to do it all at once

#### ⏱️ Time It, Don't Dread It

- **5-minute timer**: Just do one step
- **Calendar event + alarm**: For the return deadline
- **Stack with errands**: You're already doing
- **Running "Returns" checklist**: In your notes app

#### 👯 Add Support

- **Body double**: Phone a friend while prepping the return
- **Accountability buddy**: "Ask me if I dropped it off by Friday"
- **Ask for help**: With printing, re-packaging, or going to the store
- **Trade tasks**: If your partner is good at this stuff

### 🧭 Mindset Shift

- Missing a return window is a data point, not a character flaw
- You're not bad with money,  your brain navigates friction others don't see
- Returning something isn't admitting defeat,  it's a win
- Progress counts,  even if you only move the item to the door today

> Every small step is reclaiming money, space, and peace of mind.

### 💬 Encouragement to Take With You

> Unreturned items are silent stress signals. You don't have to do every return perfectly, but you're allowed to need help. You're allowed to go slow. And you're absolutely capable of getting it done.
