## 🧼 Cleaning

Cleaning isn’t just about effort, it’s about **task initiation**, **sustained attention**, and **overcoming boredom paralysis**. ADHD turns “just clean it” into a maze of distractions, emotional resistance, and unclear steps. You’re not messy by choice, your brain is overwhelmed by how to begin.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not messy because you don't care, you're navigating **overwhelm**, overstimulation, and perfectionism. **ADHD** makes it hard to start and even harder to stop once you do. One surface. One corner. That's a **win**. Pick one small area. One surface. One reset. You deserve a clean space that feels safe and manageable.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You don't need to love cleaning, but you do need to take responsibility for your space. Crumbs, mold, and clutter aren't just frustrating, they can become real health and safety issues. This isn't about being tidy, it's about creating a home that protects your body and mind. You deserve that. And it starts with **action**.

### 🧠 Why Cleaning Feels So Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Want a clean space, but feel paralyzed by where to start  
  - Get overwhelmed by clutter and shut down  
  - Feel shame about your home and avoid letting others in  
  - Do a cleaning marathon, crash, and not clean again for weeks  

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes organizing, prioritizing, and follow-through harder  
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** makes estimating cleaning time feel impossible  
  - 💥 **Boredom and low dopamine** kill motivation  
  - 🔁 **Perfectionism and shame** fuel avoidance and shutdown  
  - 🔍 **Visual clutter** increases overwhelm and decision fatigue  

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Cleaning

- **Good enough is good enough**: Forget perfection. Progress counts  
- **Externalize everything**: Don’t keep plans in your head. Write them down. Make them visual  
- **Small steps move mountains**: One micro-action at a time  
- **Dopamine fuels momentum**: Make it fun, novel, or urgent  
- **Environment matters**: Set up your space to make cleaning easier, not harder  

### 🧽 KC Davis's Gentle Cleaning Hierarchy

> Created by therapist KC Davis, this approach is perfect for ADHDers and people experiencing burnout. It focuses on restoring *function*, not chasing perfection.
- Start in this order, and stop at *any* point when things feel "functional enough":
  - 1. 🗑️ **Trash** – Take out any visible garbage  
  - 2. 🍽️ **Dishes** – Do just enough to eat again  
  - 3. 👕 **Laundry** – Start a load, or create a clean-clothes pile  
  - 4. 🧴 **Surfaces** – Wipe counters, tables, bathroom sink  
  - 5. 🧹 **Floors** – Sweep or vacuum main areas  
  - 6. 📦 **The Rest** – Tidy or organize if you have the energy  

> “Care tasks are morally neutral.” – KC Davis  
> 🧠 Learn more: *Struggle Care* podcast or KC Davis’s book _How to Keep House While Drowning_

### 🛠️ Cleaning Strategies That Actually Help

#### 📂 Environmental Design

- **Declutter first**: Less stuff = less to manage. Start with floors, then surfaces  
- **Keep cleaning supplies where you use them**: Wipes under the sink, broom by the door  
- **Use launchpads**: Designate a spot for keys, wallet, etc. to prevent pile-ups  
- **Label everything**: Clear homes = less brain clutter  
- **Use open storage**: If you forget what you can’t see, make it visible  

#### 📊 Task Breakdown & Motivation

- **Micro-task everything**: "Clean kitchen" becomes "Unload dishwasher", "Wipe counters", etc  
- **Focus only on the *next step***: This keeps momentum going  
- **Gamify**: Set a timer, race yourself, play music, or narrate your actions like a cleaning show  
- **Add urgency**: Invite a friend over or plan a reward  
- **Use body doubling**: Clean with a buddy or on a video call  

#### 🧠 Energy & Emotional Regulation

- **Create a pre-cleaning ritual**: Light a candle, stretch, or put on a hype song  
- **Take breaks**: 10 minutes counts. Come back when you can  
- **Notice shame spirals**: Pause, reframe, and reset. You’re not a bad person  
- **Rest is strategic**: Exhaustion leads to avoidance. Recharge intentionally  
- **Avoid all-or-nothing thinking**: You don’t need to clean the *whole* house. One corner is enough  

#### 🚪 Accountability & Support

- **Ask for help**: Invite a friend, partner, or coach to help break the seal  
- **Hire support if possible**: A cleaning service or ADHD-informed organizer can jumpstart progress  
- **Talk to your household**: Set expectations and explain your system. Ask for visual-friendly organization  
- **Use a timer-based co-working group**: Sites like Focusmate or Flown offer body doubling sessions, including "domestic admin" sessions  
- **Be direct with boundaries**: "I'm not taking feedback on this right now" is a full sentence  

### 🧭 Cleaning ≠ Morality

- Your cleaning habits are not a reflection of your worth 
- You are not lazy. You are navigating life with a brain that has different needs  
- You deserve a home that supports you, and it doesn't have to look like Pinterest

> Shame keeps you stuck. Self-compassion keeps you moving  

### ✨ Encouragement to Take With You

- If you did one small thing today, that's momentum  
- If you're overwhelmed, start with trash. Or dishes. Or nothing. Breathe  
- Progress over perfection, every effort counts
> Your home doesn't need to be perfect to be safe, cozy, or good enough  
> You are not bad, you're building a rhythm that works for *your* brain
