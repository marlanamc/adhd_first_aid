## Sources for Work Tasks

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD impacts productivity, organization, initiation, and time management at work — and offer strategies like external systems, body doubling, emotional reframing, and prioritization to help ADHD brains navigate professional tasks more effectively.

---

### 🧠 Executive Function & Work Performance

**73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now**  
ADDitude Experts (2012)  
➤ Recommends **sorting incoming paperwork immediately** (e.g., “Action Required” vs. “Trash”) to keep desks clear and reduce decision fatigue. Promotes maintaining a **master to-do list** to externalize tasks and ease cognitive load.

**The Disorganized Mind: Coaching Your ADHD Brain to Take Control of Your Time, Tasks, and Talents**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Offers systems for **initiating and completing projects**, managing **hyperfocus**, and collaborating through clear expectations and delegation. Stresses **customized strategies**, not one-size-fits-all.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2023)  
➤ Addresses work-related struggles like **procrastination**, **job-hopping**, and **organizational perfectionism**. Advocates for **realistic goals**, **process-based planning**, and **self-imposed deadlines**.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Tackles **multi-step task paralysis** and **perfectionism**. Suggests automating repetitive work (e.g., password storage) to prevent overthinking and preserve energy for meaningful tasks.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Promotes an **organized digital and physical environment** as key to productivity. Encourages leveraging ADHD strengths like **hyperfocus and innovation**, and stresses **self-care** to avoid burnout.

### 🛠️ Tools & Systems That Work

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Introduces the **Workbox system** and **ADHD Priority Matrix** to manage and prioritize work tasks. Encourages combining **digital tools** with **paper planners**, and using **body doubling** for large projects.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018)  
➤ Suggests “**containment before control**” and emphasizes digitizing paperwork. Defines a “**project**” as any task with more than one step. Teaches the discipline of **processing inboxes** and changing broken systems instead of blaming yourself.

**15 Good Work Habits for Adults with ADHD**  
ADDA (n.d.)  
➤ Focuses on simple, consistent **routines** and **work habits** that reduce distraction and increase follow-through. Covers scheduling, organization, communication, and deadline tracking.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Offers systems for managing **information overload**, **email**, and **impulsivity**. Introduces **body doubling** and **action-based cues** like Post-its or phone reminders to prompt redirection during tasks.

### 🧩 Mindset & Emotional Barriers

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Debunks internalized beliefs like “I’m lazy” or “I can’t work right.” Redefines **“hard work”** through the ADHD lens and promotes open dialogue about work style and needs.

**You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ Addresses unrealistic goal setting and **poor time estimation** at work. Highlights how **stimulant medication** can aid focus and strategy implementation.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Explains how **executive dysfunction** and **burnout** interfere with work. Promotes **value-aligned work**, medication as support, and **task clarity** to improve follow-through.

**6 Essential Mindfulness Practices To Help With 6 Common ADHD Symptoms**  
The ADHD Centre (2022)  
➤ Dismantles the myth of multitasking and emphasizes **mindful task-switching** to improve focus and reduce overwhelm in work-related responsibilities.

**The Neurodiversity Edge**  
Dunne, Maureen (2024)  
➤ Highlights **neurodivergent strengths** like unconventional problem-solving and creative ideation as valuable assets in the workplace.
