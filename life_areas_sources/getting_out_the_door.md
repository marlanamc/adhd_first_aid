## 🚪 Sources for Getting Out the Door

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack why getting out the door is so difficult with ADHD — from time blindness and dopamine crashes to transition trouble, overwhelm, and working memory overload — and offer strategies like visual cues, transition rituals, body doubling, chunking, and external time tracking to support smoother exits.

---

### 🧠 Understanding the ADHD Departure Struggle

**It All Makes Sense Now: Embrace Your ADHD Brain to Live a**  
_Carder, Meredith. (2024)_  
➤ Introduces **“time agnosia”** and how it leads to underestimating prep time and constant lateness. Recommends **externalizing time** by talking plans out loud and using **time tracking** to build awareness. Suggests **brain dumps, alarms, and transition rituals** to anchor the departure process. Emphasizes that low dopamine makes it hard to start just because you “should.”

**How to ADHD**  
_McCabe, Jessica. (2024)_  
➤ Explains how **time blindness** and poor **prospective memory** make it hard to plan or remember timed actions like leaving. Suggests using **event-based cues** and visual timers. Identifies **task paralysis** and **perseveration** as reasons people get stuck before leaving. Recommends building urgency and identifying all micro-steps ahead of time.

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018)_  
➤ Warns that “you won’t just do it.” Says working memory deficits prevent holding steps in mind. Suggests **external containers** like launchpads and visual cues to support departure. Introduces a **“hyperfocus rescue”** strategy: visualize an unrelated mundane task to snap out of focus traps and get moving.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
_Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy. (2006)_  
➤ Explains the ADHD **activation problem**: the brain doesn't reliably respond to internal cues to start tasks. Notes that **memory storage issues and spatial disorganization** make it hard to find essentials before leaving. Encourages rejecting “try harder” narratives and working with ADHD wiring instead.

### ⏰ Systems & Transitions

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024)_  
➤ Says **transition support** is essential. Recommends **Daily Detours** and **grace periods** to build transition time into schedules. Validates external tools like alarms as essential—not crutches. Emphasizes how back-to-back commitments can sabotage readiness. Suggests micro-rituals (walk, stretch, breathing) to shift gears.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_Kustow, James. (2024)_  
➤ Addresses the **“getting started” block** as a form of overwhelm and avoidance. Suggests **chunking** departure prep into bite-sized steps. Notes that even tools like to-do lists often need **external backup nudges** (e.g., a text from a partner). If using meds, recommends **anchoring dosage reminders** to prevent inconsistent mornings.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Levrini, Abigail L. (2025)_  
➤ Talks about **positive illusory bias** — the ADHD tendency to underestimate time. Suggests **overestimating how long tasks take** and collecting actual task durations to improve realism. Stresses that **external, immediate motivation** (not internal pressure) is more effective for starting “boring” routines like getting ready.

### 🧩 Practical Strategies & Self-Compassion

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)_  
➤ Stresses that even “simple” tasks like remembering chores or prepping to leave can feel monumental. Encourages **creating personalized routines** and using **visual cues** like a launchpad for essentials. Even recommends **sleeping in gym clothes** to bypass the morning motivation hurdle.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
_Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne. (2024)_  
➤ Describes **“panic leaving”** — when ADHDers realize they’re missing something critical right before leaving. Suggests using **body doubling** to help activate action and **scripts for the first steps** of departure to unlock forward momentum.
