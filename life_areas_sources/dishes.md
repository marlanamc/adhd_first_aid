## 📚 Sources for Dishes

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain why doing the dishes can feel like an unscalable mountain when you have ADHD — and how to reduce shame, use environmental design, and create compassionate micro-systems to make it doable again.

---

### 🧼 Dishwashing-Specific Strategies

- **Davis, KC.** _How to Keep House While Drowning_  
    The foundational text for ADHD-friendly dishwashing strategies. Introduces **function over perfection** as a core principle. Recommends:
    
    - **“Dirty dish station”** to keep the sink usable.
        
    - **Category-based unloading/stacking** (e.g., forks first).
        
    - **Ritualized prep** (music, snacks, external motivation).
        
    - **Micro-steps** to reduce overwhelm.
        
    - Radical options like **paper plates** or **throwing away moldy dishes** when reclaiming space is more important than shame.  
        Davis reframes dishes as a **care task**, not a moral obligation — a pivotal shift for ADHDers battling task inertia.
        

---

### 🥣 Cooking & Cleanup Connection

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Recommends **“low-mess recipes”** as a preventive strategy to reduce post-meal cleanup. Reinforces the idea that cooking and cleanup should be planned as a **connected loop**, not separate burdens.
    
- **Miller, Lucinda.** _Brain Brilliance_  
    While focused on nutrition, Miller highlights how **meal planning, kitchen simplicity**, and stocked staples reduce chaos around food — indirectly supporting an easier cleanup experience by **lowering the overall friction** in the kitchen.
