## 🧼 Sources for Hygiene

Explore the books and research that shaped this page. These resources highlight how hygiene challenges with ADHD can stem from executive dysfunction, burnout, sensory sensitivity, and internalized shame — and offer strategies like care kits, environmental design, self-compassion, and “tiny habits” to support both physical and emotional well-being.

---

### 🧠 Reframing Hygiene as Function, Not Morality

**How to Keep House While Drowning: A Gentle Approach to Cleaning and Organizing**  
_Davis, KC. (2022). Simon Element._  
➤ Frames hygiene tasks — bathing, brushing teeth, grooming — as **care tasks**, not moral obligations. Recommends **hygiene kits** (e.g., dry shampoo, wipes), **half-done hygiene** (mouthwash instead of brushing), and treating providers as allies, not judges. Breaks care tasks into three layers: **health/safety**, **comfort**, and **joy**.

**Lazy, Crazy, and Disgusting: Stigma and the Undoing of Global Health**  
_Brewis, Alexandra & Wutich, Amber. (2019). Johns Hopkins University Press._  
➤ Analyzes how **hygiene stigma** functions socially — to separate the “clean and worthy” from the “dirty and shamed.” Critiques interventions like **Community-Led Total Sanitation**, which weaponize disgust to change behavior. Argues for **stigma-free public health models** and acknowledges how hygiene norms often **reinforce class and disability stigmas**.

**Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything**  
_Fogg, BJ. (2019). Houghton Mifflin Harcourt._  
➤ Recommends anchoring hygiene routines to existing habits (e.g., **flossing one tooth** after brushing). Emphasizes **environmental tweaks** like keeping floss out on the counter instead of hidden. Shows how **small, consistent actions** lower the barrier to care routines.

### 🧩 ADHD, Burnout, and the Barriers to Self-Care

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique. (2024). New Harbinger Publications._  
➤ Identifies how **burnout can shut down basic hygiene** like teeth brushing or showering. Emphasizes that people are **“worthy of care”** even during struggle. Offers scripts and strategies for asking for help or **co-regulating care tasks** without shame.

**Unapologetically ADHD: A Step-by-Step Framework for Everyday**  
_Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024). John Wiley & Sons, Inc._  
➤ Discusses physical organization as part of functional support — suggesting that **systems built around real energy levels and routines** reduce friction around care tasks, including hygiene.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD: The 7-Pillar Plan for Focus, Productivity, and Inner Peace**  
_Kustow, James. (2024). Ebury Publishing._  
➤ Highlights **cleaning and personal care products** as potential sources of sensory or chemical overwhelm. Recommends **reducing “toxic load”**, including filtering water and choosing low-irritant hygiene products to support sustainable routines.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
_Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy. (2006). Scribner._  
➤ Introduces the idea of **“mental hygiene”** — clearing out shame-based self-talk that makes care tasks feel like proof of personal failure. Emphasizes **mess management as ergonomic** — the goal is usability, not perfection.

### 🛠️ Environmental Design & Functional Tools

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn. (2018). ADHD Homestead Press._  
➤ Suggests placing **cleaning tools where they’re used** (e.g., shower spray in the shower). Encourages making tasks “**laughably easy**” to increase follow-through. Warns that if you have to move items to clean, **the surface won’t get cleaned**.

**The Anti-Planner: How to Get Sh*t Done When You Don’t Feel Like It**  
_Donovan, Dani. (2023). Penguin Life._  
➤ Recommends **visible storage strategies** (e.g., not hiding vegetables in drawers). Offers **gamified cleaning strategies** like scavenger hunts and visual checklists to help activate action without shame.

**It All Makes Sense Now: Embrace Your ADHD Brain to Live a Better Life**  
_Carder, Meredith. (2024). Hay House, Inc._  
➤ Reframes friction reduction as a win, not laziness. Endorses **shortcuts like pre-chopped food** or visible food storage to **maintain hygiene indirectly** without creating more overwhelm.

### 💧 Hygiene, Nutrition, and Whole-Body Health

**Brain Brilliance: 60 Nourishing Recipes and a Nutritional Plan for Kids and Teens with ADHD and Autism**  
_Miller, Lucinda. (2024). Quadrille Publishing._  
➤ Connects **clean water, filtered fluids**, and toxin avoidance to better mental and physical health — reinforcing hygiene as a foundation for wellness.

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia: A Practical Guide to Food, Diet and Supplementation**  
_Derbyshire, Emma. (2025). Jessica Kingsley Publishers._  
➤ Highlights the role of **hydration, sleep, and nutrition** in supporting consistent self-care. Though focused on food, it reinforces **interconnected hygiene behaviors** that support executive function.

**Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change and Grow**  
_Miller, William R. & Rollnick, Stephen. (2023). The Guilford Press._  
➤ Shares examples of **hygiene-focused interventions** that use curiosity and agency — not shame — to support behavior change. Offers **low-stigma approaches** to handwashing, diet, and medication adherence.

**Real Self-Care: A Transformative Program for Redefining Wellness**  
_Lakshmin, Pooja. (2023). Penguin Publishing Group._  
➤ Redefines self-care as **an internal process**, not external appearances. Encourages **values-based care decisions** rather than perfectionism — affirming that **flexible hygiene routines are still valid**.
