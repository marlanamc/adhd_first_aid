## Sources for To-Do Lists

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD impacts planning, memory, and task initiation — and offer strategies for building lists that are externalized, prioritized, and realistic for ADHD brains.

---

### 🧠 Executive Function & Memory Support

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Nancy A. Ratey (2008)  
➤ Highlights how ADHD makes it hard to plan ahead and follow through, even on simple tasks. Recommends reviewing your to-do list daily to assess progress, prep for the next day, and create accountability.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Peg Dawson & Richard Guare (2016)  
➤ Checklists are one of the best supports for working memory issues. Paper or digital — whatever helps you externalize and remember.

**Order from Chaos**  
Jaclyn Paul (2018)  
➤ Emphasizes that your brain is for processing, not storage. Recommends externalizing tasks onto paper or digital lists and using checklists to reduce overwhelm.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Abigail L. Levrini (2025)  
➤ Recommends writing down and prioritizing tasks, then tracking how long they take. This helps with planning, estimating, and minimizing procrastination.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kate Kelly & Peggy Ramundo (2006)  
➤ ADHD brains often overestimate what can be done. Encourages separating “should-do’s” from “must-do’s” to keep lists realistic and emotionally manageable.

### ✅ To-Do List Structures & Systems

**73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now**  
ADDitude Experts (2012)  
➤ Try two-tiered lists: one short “Action List” for today, and a “Parking Lot” for later. Or stick to a 5-item index card. Clear systems reduce overwhelm.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)  
➤ Break tasks into tiny pieces and categorize them using the “ADHD Priority Matrix” — Red (urgent), Green (important), Blue (can wait). Add a “brain dump” list to capture everything before sorting.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
James Kustow (2024)  
➤ Create a “dump-all” task list for capturing everything, then rewrite a daily/weekly shortlist on a visible whiteboard to stay focused.

**Getting Things Done**  
David Allen (2015)  
➤ Not ADHD-specific but deeply influential. Defines a “project” as anything needing more than one step, then breaks it down into small actions using a trusted external system.

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
Melissa Orlov (2010)  
➤ Recommends re-prioritizing outstanding tasks weekly with your partner to agree on what matters most — and avoid resentment or confusion.

### 🔁 Prioritization & Planning Tools

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
➤ Use color coding for urgency, delegate/delete non-essential tasks, and plan proactively with daily/weekly check-ins. Emphasizes self-compassion and flexible systems.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
Ju DOMI (2025)  
➤ Offers prompts for breaking tasks into steps, and explains priority frameworks (like decision matrices). Suggests Asana for visual project/task tracking.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD**  
➤ Group similar tasks (task batching), use rewards to incentivize follow-through, and visualize progress to keep momentum going.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
➤ Recommends “structured procrastination” — starting with an easier or more desirable task to activate motivation.

---

### 🧰 Digital & Paper Tools

**Order from Chaos**  
➤ Recommends Bullet Journals (with indexed pages), digital task managers, and having an inbox system for all tasks that need processing.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
➤ Encourages using both digital (Asana, task software) and physical (whiteboards, notebooks) tools. Emphasizes clear category systems and folder structures.

**73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now**  
➤ Highlights Outlook task manager and other digital tools that allow categorization, deadlines, and reminders.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
➤ Carry a small notebook for tasks on the go, then move them to a planner or app. Use voice assistants like Google Home or Siri to capture tasks quickly.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
➤ Recommends combining paper planners for daily/weekly views with digital systems for project management. Planning requires a hybrid approach.

### 🫶 Mindset, Self-Compassion & Follow-Through

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell & Ratey (2024)  
➤ Writing out to-do lists strengthens focus and memory. Keeps your priorities visible and reinforces motivation.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
➤ Highlights how unrealistic to-do lists can become emotional weapons. Recommends reframing tasks to feel more manageable and aligned with your energy.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
➤ Encourages accountability systems (e.g., sharing goals with someone). Planning with ADHD often requires external scaffolding, not just internal willpower.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
➤ Planning is a high-calorie task for ADHD brains. The system doesn’t need to be perfect — just something you can return to after falling off track.
