## 📚 Sources for Budgeting & Tracking

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources support the ADHD-friendly strategies behind money management, time awareness, and tracking systems — helping reduce overwhelm, clarify priorities, and reclaim control of resources.

---

### 💵 ADHD & Financial Budgeting

- **Alexander-Grout, Maddy.** _Mad About Money: Managing Finances (and Life) with ADHD_  
    Offers direct, ADHD-informed budgeting advice, including how to start a budget, track progress, and use accountability to stay consistent.
    
- **Gilkey, Charlie.** _Start Finishing_  
    Introduces the idea of project-based budgeting with categories and constraints that act as positive boundaries — applicable to both time and money.
    

---

### 🕓 Time Tracking & Awareness

- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_  
    Recommends attaching time estimates to tasks and comparing estimated vs actual time — building time awareness for better planning and tracking.
    
- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!_  
    Suggests a “Daily Time Log” to track productive periods and manage task grouping, plus use of calendars and organizers to stay on schedule.
    
- **DOMI, Ju.** _Productivity With ADHD: Complete Guide_  
    Covers digital time-tracking apps like Toggl and RescueTime and includes exercises on perception vs reality in time use — ideal for ADHD time blindness.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Provides detailed guidance on **Zero-Based Time Budgeting**, tracking distractions, and logging emotion + energy patterns. Promotes aligning time use with values.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_  
    Includes strategies for tracking time, identifying patterns that lead to success or failure, and adjusting routines accordingly.
    

---

### 📊 Tracking Systems & Tools

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Shares practical ADHD-friendly systems like the Kanban board, priority matrix, and “accountability betting” — all rooted in visual progress tracking.
    
- **Forte, Tiago.** _Building a Second Brain_ & _The PARA Method_  
    Offers frameworks for organizing digital and financial life using folders, trackers, and categorized archives. Promotes regular reviews and data-driven reflection.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    Advocates for tools like Toodledo and Google Calendar for tracking tasks, recurring bills, and time-sensitive routines. Supports checklists and habit systems.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_  
    Encourages personal tracking systems like journals or apps, and reframes tracking as a way to observe behavior with curiosity, not shame.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Suggests creative tracking tools like coloring in a progress bar or using “time pillars” for routine-building. Encourages reflection and adaptive structure.
    

---

### 🧠 Self-Monitoring & Values-Driven Planning

- **Lakshmin, Pooja.** _Real Self-Care_  
    Promotes using logs and reviews to assess whether your time and energy align with your values. Supports the idea of resource tracking as emotional self-care.
