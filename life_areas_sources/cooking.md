## 📚 Sources for Cooking

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the ADHD-specific challenges behind cooking — like task sequencing, time blindness, decision paralysis, executive overload, and shame — while offering strategies like batch cooking, easy-prep meals, sensory adaptations, and emotional support.

---

### 🍳 ADHD-Friendly Cooking Systems

- **Dee, Lisa.** _Healthy Happy ADHD_  
    Introduces **“ADHD Easy Meals”** — fast, low-prep, flexible meals built around protein and frozen pantry staples. Offers tips like one-pan dinners, microwave pouches, and backup plans for “can’t cook” days. Also addresses **hyperfixation**, **food burnout**, and **gut health links** to mood and attention.
    
- **Carder, Meredith.** _It All Makes Sense Now_  
    Covers **batch cooking**, **food aversions**, **ADHD decision fatigue**, and meal planning hacks like “deal-a-meal” index cards. Reframes shame around food spoilage, takeout habits, and nutrition overwhelm.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Provides a list of **“Meals to feed yourself when you just can’t anymore.”** Encourages appliance shortcuts (air fryer, Instant Pot), one-dish meals, and lowering the bar when energy is low.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_  
    Validates that **cooking takes more cognitive energy** for ADHDers. Recommends ready-made meals and delivery services as valid alternatives.

### 🛠️ Time, Sequencing & Executive Function

- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Emphasizes the need for **checklists, workflows, and scaffolding** in tasks like cooking. Highlights **hyperfocus risk**, recommends time tracking for better planning, and suggests using alarms to manage multi-step tasks.
    
- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_  
    Points out **underestimation of cooking time**, distractibility, and the need for **task simplification and visual aids** — especially for step-by-step recipes or prep-heavy meals.
    
- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_  
    Addresses **divided attention issues** in the kitchen and notes how memory and attention deficits can lead to burning food, forgetting steps, or mismanaging prep timelines.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Describes **working memory overload** in cooking and why a recipe is usually necessary. Normalizes fallback meals and fridge clutter from past cooking plans.
    
- **Levrini, Abigail.** _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_  
    Recommends designated **“home office hours” for weekly meal planning**, reinforcing the importance of prep structure and space design.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_  
    Suggests assigning time blocks (e.g., “Tuesdays at Ten”) for recurring tasks like meal prep. Validates the strain of grocery shopping and household planning on ADHDers.

### 🧠 Nutrition & Health for the ADHD Brain

- **Hallowell, Edward M. & Ratey, John J.** _ADHD 2.0_  
    Recommends **whole foods, healthy fats, and avoiding additives**. Emphasizes the neurological benefits of nutrient-dense, balanced meals for focus and emotional regulation.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_  
    Offers a full chapter on **nutrition**, advocating for **batch cooking, meal planning, and freezer use** to support ADHD time and energy cycles. Highlights **mindful eating** and the connection between digestion and brain function.
    
- **Miller, Lucinda.** _Brain Brilliance_  
    Provides **60 simple, ADHD-friendly recipes** with a focus on **protein, choline, and omega-3s**. Prioritizes brain-boosting breakfast ideas and shares the author’s personal experiences with ADHD food overwhelm.
    
- **EndeavorOTC.** _Research Roundup: Best Foods for ADHD_  
    Compiles dietary insights (e.g., sugar, protein, artificial dyes) that inform ADHD-aware cooking and grocery choices.
    

---

### 🧰 Kitchen Organization & Planning Support

- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    Offers **kitchen-specific organizing tips**, including checklist-based routines and storing items where they’re used to reduce steps and friction.
    
- **University of Rochester Medical Center.** _73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now_  
    Suggests **strategic kitchen layouts** (e.g., storing knives next to cutting boards), **recipe software for recall**, and reducing friction for cooking routines.
    

---

### 💬 Emotional Support & Shame Reduction

- **Solden, Sari.** _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_  
    Describes how **meal prep, hosting, and cooking for others** can be uniquely overwhelming and shame-inducing for ADHD women. Validates **non-linear planning**, the need for visual reminders, and reducing self-judgment.
    
- **Daily Routine Examples from Adults with ADHD**  
    Highlights real-world adaptations like **meal kit subscriptions and planning for leftovers** — described as “paying the ADHD tax upfront” to avoid future overwhelm.
