## 🧺 Sources for Laundry

Explore the books and strategies that shaped this page. These resources explain why laundry can feel so overwhelming with ADHD — from task initiation and transition paralysis to shame, perfectionism, and executive dysfunction — and offer strategies like moral neutrality, simplified systems, sensory-friendly routines, and self-compassion.

---

### 🧠 Reframing Laundry as a Functional Task

**How to Keep House While Drowning: A Gentle Approach to Cleaning and Organizing**  
_Davis, KC. (2022). Simon Element._  
➤ Reframes laundry as a **neutral care task**, not a moral failure. Emphasizes **self-compassion**, even for large laundry piles, and offers permission to **wash everything together on cold**, skip folding, and live out of clean baskets when needed. Suggests systems like **"family closets," wear-again baskets, fixed wash days**, and **laundry resets** (packing excess clothes away). Advocates **outsourcing** when possible.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
_Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy. (2006). Scribner._  
➤ Encourages **checklists and clear family task breakdowns** to reduce forgetfulness. Frames household labor as part of **mental hygiene**, urging gentleness toward yourself and embracing practical (not perfect) systems.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
_Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne. (2024). Vintage Publishing._  
➤ Recommends using a **"wear-again shelf"** for clothes that aren't clean or dirty. Connects laundry shame to childhood beliefs around being a burden, encouraging a more flexible and compassionate mindset.

### 🧩 Executive Function & Environment Design

**The Disorganized Mind: Coaching Your ADHD Brain to Take Control**  
_Ratey, Nancy A. (2008). St. Martin’s Griffin._  
➤ Suggests scheduling a **realistic routine** for laundry with backup days. Emphasizes **completing the full cycle** (folding and putting away). Encourages doing laundry during **low-effort multitask times**, like watching TV, and recommends **delegating** or outsourcing as valid options.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD: The 7-Pillar Plan for Focus, Productivity, and Inner Peace**  
_Kustow, James. (2024). Ebury Publishing._  
➤ Offers practical strategies like **only owning two sock types**, using **multi-bin hampers**, setting **timers and notifications**, and scheduling wash/iron days. Suggests decluttering with **hanger tricks** and applying the **one-year rule** to simplify decision-making.

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018). ADHD Homestead Press._  
➤ While not laundry-specific in this excerpt, her broader philosophy of **externalizing systems** and reducing friction aligns with many of the practical laundry strategies shared elsewhere (e.g., placing supplies where they’re used, batch organizing).

### 🎧 Motivation, Transitions & Habit Pairing

**How to ADHD: An Insider’s Guide to Working with Your Brain**  
_McCabe, Jessica. (2024). Potter/Ten Speed/Harmony/Rodale._  
➤ Shares a personal example of **bartering editing for laundry**, highlighting how **preferences vary** — and some ADHDers may find laundry soothing when it’s structured or paired with other tasks.

**Mad About Money: Managing Finances (and Life) with ADHD**  
_Alexander-Grout, Maddy. (2025). John Wiley & Sons._  
➤ Discusses **transition paralysis**, which can block progress through multi-step chores like laundry (washer → dryer → fold → put away). Reinforces the importance of simplifying and minimizing friction between stages.

**Building a Second Brain**  
_Forte, Tiago. (2022). Profile Books._  
➤ While focused on digital organization, suggests **habit pairing** — like folding laundry while listening to a podcast — to make chores less burdensome. Introduces the idea of **integrating low-stimulation tasks** with enriching ones.

---

### 🛠️ Invisible Labor & Emotional Load

**Real Self-Care: A Transformative Program for Redefining Wellness**  
_Lakshmin, Pooja. (2023). Penguin Publishing Group._  
➤ Describes the **“invisible labor”** of household management — from remembering wash schedules to managing clothing inventory. Helps reframe laundry overwhelm as a **cognitive burden**, not a personal flaw.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success: How to Use Your Brain to Get More Done**  
_Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard. (2015). Guilford Press._  
➤ Explains that chores like laundry feel more or less effortful depending on how they **tap into specific executive skills**. Identifying laundry as a “high-effort” task allows for targeted support or simplification.

**The Power of Moments: Why Certain Experiences Have Extraordinary Impact**  
_Heath, Chip & Heath, Dan. (2017). Simon & Schuster._  
➤ Shares an example of **outsourced laundry with luxury-level presentation** (lavender-wrapped bundles). Reminds us that **laundry systems can feel good**, not just get done — and **delight and design** can change how we experience tasks.
