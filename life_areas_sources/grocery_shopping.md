## 🛒 Sources for Grocery Shopping

Explore the books, strategies, and real-life experiences that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD makes grocery shopping overwhelming — from decision fatigue and sensory overload to impulsivity, time blindness, and executive dysfunction — and offer solutions like preplanning, convenience foods, online shopping, and habit-friendly layouts.

---

### 🧠 Understanding the ADHD Grocery Gauntlet

**It All Makes Sense Now: Embrace Your ADHD Brain to Live a Better Life**  
_Carder, Meredith. (2024). Hay House, Inc._  
➤ Describes the **overwhelm of the produce aisle**, especially when trying to plan meals for multiple people. Notes how decision fatigue, rumination, and low dopamine can make grocery shopping feel like an impossible task. Recommends shortcuts like **pre-chopped onions**, **visible food storage**, and avoiding crisper drawers to counteract **“out of sight, out of mind.”**

**The Disorganized Mind: Coaching Your ADHD Brain to Take Control**  
_Ratey, Nancy A. (2008). St. Martin’s Griffin._  
➤ Suggests attaching keys to shopping lists or essential items to avoid forgetting them. Recommends organizing the kitchen by food categories (like a grocery store) to ease inventory tracking. Emphasizes **treating grocery shopping as a non-negotiable routine**, and using **pre-printed lists** to reduce memory strain.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD: The 7-Pillar Plan for Focus, Productivity, and Inner Peace**  
_Kustow, James. (2024). Ebury Publishing._  
➤ Recommends **shopping with a list** and avoiding grocery runs when hungry. Highlights how ADHD affects **nutrition habits, impulse control, and preparation**, making healthy shopping choices more difficult without structure.

**The Anti-Planner: How to Get Sh*t Done When You Don’t Feel Like It**  
_Donovan, Dani. (2023). Penguin Life._  
➤ Validates that cooking takes massive executive function. Burnout often leads to **wasted groceries or skipped meals**. Offers tips like **fridge redesigns**, **reordering storage zones**, and using **grocery delivery timing** as a motivational nudge.

**Mad About Money: The Honest Guide to Navigating Life on a Budget**  
_Alexander-Grout, Maddy. (2024). Maddy’s Guide Publishing._  
➤ Describes how grocery shopping became a **dopamine coping strategy** during depression. Warns about the lure of **“yellow sticker” markdowns** and encourages **online shopping or sending someone else** to minimize impulse buys and decision fatigue.

### 🧩 ADHD-Friendly Food Systems

**Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything**  
_Fogg, B.J. (2019). Houghton Mifflin Harcourt._  
➤ Explains how small obstacles like **unwashed lettuce** or **missing lids** derail follow-through. Recommends building a **“SuperFridge”** with ready-to-eat items. Suggests simple actions like **adding one new veggie to your list** or starting in the produce aisle.

**Brain Brilliance: Unlock Your Potential Through Nutrition**  
_Miller, Lucinda. (2020). Short Books._  
➤ Recommends a **colorful, vegetable-rich diet**. Encourages **engaging with food environments** like farmers markets and pick-your-own farms to increase sensory familiarity — especially useful for ADHD children and adults.

**Healthy, Happy ADHD: Transform How You Move, Eat, and Feel**  
_Dee, Lisa. (2024). Self-published._  
➤ Shares how **routine-based grocery stops** (e.g., butcher after dog walk) reduce decision fatigue. Uses **online apps and recurring subscriptions** to reduce impulse spending and energy drain. Notes the mental health benefits of **community-based shopping** and food rituals.

### 🛠️ Design & Environment Tweaks

**Feel-Good Productivity: How to Do More of What Matters to You**  
_Abdaal, Ali. (2024). Penguin Life._  
➤ Cites a **Dutch study by Huitink**, which found that **adding a green inlay to shopping carts increased vegetable purchases by over 50%**. Suggests using **environmental cues** and **intentional design** (cart layout, kitchen zones) to reduce cognitive load and support healthy decisions.

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018). ADHD Homestead Press._  
➤ While not directly quoted in this section, aligns with core principles of **externalizing systems**, creating **launchpads**, and using **visual anchors** to reduce overwhelm in preparation-based tasks like grocery shopping.

### 💸 Economics & Access Realities

**Lazy, Crazy, and Disgusting: Stigma and the Undoing of Global Health**  
_Brewis, Alexandra & Wutich, Amber. (2019). Johns Hopkins University Press._  
➤ Explains how **low-cost, highly processed foods** dominate in food deserts and under-resourced communities. Discusses how **limited time, money, and access** intersect with health behaviors like grocery shopping and food prep — especially for marginalized populations.

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia: A Practical Guide to Food, Diet and Supplementation**  
_Derbyshire, Emma. (2025). Jessica Kingsley Publishers._  
➤ Provides a comprehensive list of **cost-effective, brain-friendly pantry staples**, including protein sources, healthy fats, and easy-to-prepare grains and veggies. Promotes **affordable brands** with similar nutrient profiles to premium versions and offers tips for creating ADHD-friendly shopping lists.
