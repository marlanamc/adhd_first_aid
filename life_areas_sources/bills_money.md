## 📚 Sources for Bills & Money

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of financial avoidance and paperwork overwhelm — and offer strategies for executive function support, external systems, mindset shifts, and ADHD-friendly organization.

---

### 💵 ADHD & Finances

- **Alexander-Grout, Maddy.** _Mad About Money: Managing Finances (and Life) with ADHD_  
    Directly addresses ADHD and finances. Covers accountability, dopamine-based motivation (like the debt snowball), and making progress visual.
    
- **University of Rochester Medical Center.** _73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now_  
    Offers practical suggestions like switching to online banking, using a calendar for financial reviews, and reducing paper inflow — aligned with automation and externalization strategies.
    

---

### 🧠 Executive Function & Task Management

- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_  
    Supports task breakdown, checklist use, accountability, environmental design, and the use of reminders and technology to manage working memory and time blindness.
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Shares ADHD-friendly techniques like microtasking, Kanban boards, the Eisenhower Matrix for urgency vs importance, and visual progress tracking.
    
- **Fogg, B.J.** _Tiny Habits_  
    Offers the foundation for “chunk it down” and “start small” techniques, helping build momentum and reduce paralysis around paperwork tasks.
    
- **Gilkey, Charlie.** _Start Finishing_  
    Encourages breaking down larger projects into manageable steps and setting up an environment that supports productivity.
    
- **Levrini, Abigail L.** _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_  
    Discusses how executive function challenges like planning and working memory create difficulty with tasks like bills. Offers tips like highlighting instructions, organizing by outline, and automating reminders.
    

---

### 📦 Externalization & Systems

- **Forte, Tiago.** _Building a Second Brain_ & _The PARA Method_  
    Advocates for external systems of organization like PARA and encourages sorting “for action” over category — aligning with visual systems, action-based file organization, and regular reviews.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Offers a full planning framework that includes workboxes, task managers, buffers, reminders, and the separation of reference materials. Also reinforces mindset shifts around shame and memory limitations.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    Promotes digitization of paperwork, use of Bullet Journals, and visual checklists. Encourages minimizing paper inflow and reframing mistakes as neutral data.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_  
    Recommends creating visible “to-do” and “to-read” bins for papers, a centralized task dump system, and externalization of all paperwork steps.
    

---

### 💬 Mindset, Motivation & Emotional Regulation

- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!_  
    Offers emotional validation around paperwork struggles, highlights paper pile management strategies, and addresses time distortion and working memory limitations.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_  
    Explains why paperwork is hard for ADHDers and offers strategies grounded in self-compassion, task initiation support, and help-seeking as a strength.
    
- **Lakshmin, Pooja.** _Real Self-Care_  
    Offers mindset tools for boundary-setting, guilt reduction, and self-compassion — aligning with “Forms ≠ Morality” and reframing avoidance without shame.
    
- **Le Cunff, Anne-Laure.** _Tiny Experiments_  
    Emphasizes embracing imperfection, small experiments, and curiosity around procrastination — helping reframe guilt and initiate avoided tasks.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Provides ADHD-specific strategies for flexibility, permission slips, externalization, and reframing mistakes — supporting all mindset components of the guide.
    
- **Miller, William R. & Rollnick, Stephen.** _Motivational Interviewing_  
    Shares non-judgmental methods for supporting change and making plans that anticipate obstacles — foundational to the guide’s gentle and shame-free tone.
    
- **Kegan, Robert & Lahey, Lisa.** _Immunity to Change_  
    Helps unpack internal resistance to tasks like paperwork by surfacing hidden assumptions and commitments.
