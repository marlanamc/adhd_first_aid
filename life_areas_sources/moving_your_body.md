## 🧠 The Science Behind Movement & ADHD

**Spark: The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the Brain**  
_John J. Ratey, M.D., and Eric Hagerman (2008)_  
➤ Exercise is described as **a necessary dose of medicine** for the ADHD brain. It boosts **dopamine, norepinephrine, and endorphins**, enhances **reaction speed**, and improves **focus, attention, and cognitive function**. Even **20–30 minutes** of moderate activity can have powerful effects.

**ADHD 2.0**  
_Edward M. Hallowell, M.D., & John J. Ratey, M.D. (2022)_  
➤ Emphasizes **movement as a daily strategy** to smooth brain networks and stimulate the **frontal cortex**, improving attention and executive function. It recommends short, regular bouts of exercise as essential to thriving with ADHD.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail L. Levrini (2023)_  
➤ Describes exercise as a **“cheap and proven” intervention** with no side effects. It helps with **focus, sequencing, memory, and mood**, and may be as effective as medication for some individuals.

## 💃 Everyday Movement as Lifestyle

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ Encourages **shifting from strict workouts to daily movement**, especially for women with ADHD. Highlights how small physical actions help with **emotional regulation, anxiety, and internal restlessness**. Movement is framed as **soothing, not punishing**.

**The Blue Zones**  
_Dan Buettner (2012)_  
➤ Identifies **natural, embedded movement** (e.g. walking, gardening, standing while working) as the secret to long life. This insight supports the ADHD-friendly idea of **incorporating movement into daily life** rather than relying on formal workouts.

## 🧘‍♀️ Regulation Through Movement & Sensory Input

**The Body Keeps the Score**  
_Bessel A. van der Kolk, M.D. (2014)_  
➤ Movement-based practices like **yoga and breathwork** are shown to be deeply regulating. These methods can help ADHDers reduce **emotional overwhelm**, reconnect with the body, and **regulate arousal states**.

**Fidget to Focus**  
_Roland Rotz & Sarah Wright_  
➤ Normalizes and encourages **intentional fidgeting** (pacing, doodling, gum chewing) as sensory strategies to stay **stimulated and alert**. Movement doesn’t have to be big to be powerful.

## 🛠️ Habit-Building & Lifestyle Integration

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Acknowledges how difficult it can be to **follow through with exercise**, even when it's known to help. Emphasizes integrating **small, consistent habits** for long-term physical and mental health.
