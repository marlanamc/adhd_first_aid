## 📚 Sources for Cleaning

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack the real ADHD barriers behind cleaning struggles — from executive dysfunction to shame — and offer practical, compassionate strategies like body doubling, scaffolding, visual systems, and redefining what “clean enough” looks like.

---

### 🧽 Cleaning-Specific Guides & Systems

- **Davis, KC.** _How to Keep House While Drowning_  
    The core inspiration for the “Gentle Cleaning Hierarchy.” Emphasizes that care tasks are morally neutral and encourages functionality over perfection. Offers practical systems for laundry, dishes, and outsourcing help.
    
- **White, Dana K.** _(5-Pile Decluttering Method)_  
    Her decluttering strategy is a method that reduces overwhelm, decision fatigue, and shame — ideal for ADHD brains.
    

---

### 🧠 Executive Function & Task Breakdown

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Provides ADHD-friendly methods for breaking tasks into verbs, using timers, and layering in sensory rituals to spark motivation for dreaded chores.
    
- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_  
    Highlights time management, chunking, self-regulation, and using environment-based cues — foundational for building cleaning routines that stick.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Introduces concepts like “start stupid small,” customized systems, and judgment-free planning. Reinforces the importance of energy awareness and scaffolding — especially for tasks like dishes and laundry.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_  
    Shares visual support tools, chunking advice, and the power of body doubling — all ideal for getting started with cleaning tasks.
    
- **Levrini, Abigail L.** _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_  
    Includes a full chapter on “Organizing Your Space,” with realistic strategies and the reminder that progress matters more than perfection.
    

---

### 🧼 Organizing & Decluttering with ADHD

- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    Provides a wealth of ADHD-specific tips for managing home environments. Focuses on externalizing systems, minimizing shame, and storing supplies where they’re needed.
    
- **Kohlberg, Judith & Nadeau, Kathleen.** _ADD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life_  
    Practical organizing tools designed around ADHD thinking patterns — including how to deal with clutter and cleaning flow.
    
- **Carder, Meredith.** _It All Makes Sense Now_  
    Emphasizes automations, templates, and burnout recovery to make recurring tasks like cleaning more doable with ADHD.
    

---

### 💬 Emotional Reframing & Shame Reduction

- **Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne.** _Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies_  
    Tackles the “I’m lazy” myth and explains why cleaning feels impossible when dopamine is low. Advocates for strategies like body doubling and “done is better than perfect.”
    
- **Solden, Sari & Frank, Michelle.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_  
    Centers self-compassion, shame resilience, and support-seeking for ADHD women struggling with home care expectations.
    
- **Solden, Sari.** _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_  
    Explores disorganization as a pervasive issue and encourages external support systems to fill in executive function gaps.
    
- **Brewis, Alexandra & Wutich, Amber.** _Lazy, Crazy, and Disgusting_  
    Highlights how stigma around cleanliness intersects with mental health and neurodivergence, reinforcing the importance of reframing shame-based narratives.
    

---

### 🧠 ADHD Foundations That Shape Cleaning Struggles

- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Offers a toolkit of small-step strategies and “permission slips” for managing overwhelm. Encourages externalizing reminders and anchoring cleaning habits to movement or music.
    
- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!_  
    Focuses on disorganization, erratic memory, and the need for forgiveness — reframing cleaning struggles as neurological, not personal failures.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_  
    Offers coaching strategies for tackling prioritization issues and repetitive home tasks, like laundry. Encourages support, self-coaching, and systemization.
    
- **Ramsay, J. Russell.** _Rethinking Adult ADHD_  
    Addresses perfectionism and cognitive distortions that fuel avoidance and shame — key for understanding why starting (or finishing) cleaning is so hard.
