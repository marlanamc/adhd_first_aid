## 📚 Sources for Decluttering

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources highlight why decluttering is especially difficult for ADHDers — and how to break through shame, perfectionism, executive overload, and “doom piles” using compassionate strategies, systems that fit your brain, and one small step at a time.

---

### 🧠 Executive Function & Cognitive Load

- **Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard.** _Smart but Scattered Guide to Success_  
    Explains how a cluttered environment drains executive function. Recommends **breaking tasks into small checklisted actions**, using reminders, and turning open-ended goals into “closed” tasks to reduce overwhelm.
    
- **Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique.** _The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook_  
    Validates that organizing is an ADHD struggle, not a moral failure. Suggests **supportive scaffolding**, **challenging unrealistic expectations**, and hiring help like **decluttering coaches or virtual assistants**.
    
- **Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D.** _Unapologetically ADHD_  
    Offers ADHD-specific frameworks like the **Workbox**, **Brain Dump**, and microtasking. Promotes function over aesthetics: **“Can you find what you need when you need it?”** Encourages systems that reduce overwhelm, not fuel it.
    
- **Ducommun, Jules.** _Productivity With ADHD_  
    Frames organization as a tool for **freeing up cognitive resources**, not an aesthetic ideal. Offers **clear digital and physical decluttering strategies**, and stresses progress over perfection.
    
- **Levrini, Abigail.** _Succeeding With Adult ADHD_  
    Describes **chronic disorganization** as a core ADHD trait. Recommends working in **short bursts**, designating places for items, labeling, and challenging avoidance-based shame. Includes a detailed decluttering action plan.

### 🧹 Practical Decluttering Strategies

- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    A decluttering powerhouse. Challenges the myth that “creative = messy.” Offers step-by-step systems: **scan & shred paper**, **designated inboxes**, **emptying one item at a time**, and organizing info outside the brain. Recommends starting with your personal “why.”
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_  
    Provides a **decluttering sequence** (floors → surfaces → drawers), and recommends **labels**, **body doubling**, music, rewards, and visible whiteboards. Suggests reframing tidying as a “fun challenge.”
    
- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Promotes breaking down decluttering into **hyper-specific micro-steps** (“take hamper to laundry room”), writing tasks on **index cards**, and building momentum with creativity and play.
    
- **University of Rochester Medical Center.** _73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now_  
    Offers actionable tips like **"one in, one out"** rules for closets, **labeling containers**, storing “like with like,” sorting mail daily, and hiring outside help like students to scan documents.
    
- **McCabe, Jessica.** _How to ADHD_  
    Advocates for **owning less** to reduce cognitive burden. Suggests “point of performance” reminders like whiteboards and visible bins. Promotes functional minimalism and visual supports.

### 💬 Emotional Reframing & Shame Reduction

- **Solden, Sari & Frank, Michelle.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_  
    Reframes messiness as a brain-based trait, not a moral failing. Validates the emotional burden of shame and **encourages individualized support systems** that fit your life, not someone else’s template.
    
- **Ratey, Nancy A.** _The Disorganized Mind_  
    Normalizes chaotic environments as an ADHD symptom. Encourages **structure and delegation**, emotional distance from possessions, and **accountability support** for follow-through.
    
- **Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy.** _You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!_  
    Addresses the **emotional toll of clutter**, unrealistic to-do lists, and “time and space” struggles. Suggests external structure, note-taking tools, and **ruthlessly eliminating the unnecessary**.
    
- **Pink, Richard & Roxanne.** _Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies_  
    Introduces ADHD terms like **“DOOM piles”** and **“floordrobes”** to normalize messy behavior. Encourages body doubling, fun naming systems, and focusing on what works instead of forcing perfection.
    
- **Solden, Sari.** _Women with Attention Deficit Disorder_  
    Describes how ADHD leads to “constant expansion” of clutter due to **idea generation + poor organizational skills**. Encourages using “disorganization” creatively to problem-solve rather than trying to “fix” it entirely.

### 🧰 Systems, Tools & Digital Decluttering

- **Forte, Tiago.** _The PARA Method_  
    Offers a digital decluttering system that categorizes all information into **Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives**. Designed to reduce mental clutter and help ADHDers find what they need, fast.
    
- **Paul, Jaclyn.** _Order from Chaos_  
    Also addresses **digital clutter**: scan/shred paper, use search-friendly file names, and regularly process inboxes. Recommends Bullet Journal for externalizing ideas and tasks.
    
- **Ducommun, Jules.** _Productivity With ADHD_  
    Includes a full system for **digital organization**, from folder naming conventions to regular inbox review. Frames digital clutter as just as taxing as physical mess.
