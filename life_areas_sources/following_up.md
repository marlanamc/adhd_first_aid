## 🧾 Sources for Following Up

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain why following up is so hard with ADHD — from working memory gaps and low motivation to disrupted reward systems — and offer strategies like accountability systems, progress tracking, body doubling, structured workflows, and external supports to help you follow through.

---

### 🧠 Understanding the ADHD Follow-Up Struggle

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018)_  
➤ Defines follow-up as an executive function linchpin — not just initiating tasks, but completing the loop. Emphasizes that **the responsibility to ensure follow-through lies with the person who started the request**, not the one being asked. Suggests creating a **"pending actions" container** to avoid lost tasks. Reframes failure as a “data point” — following up on what works and what needs adjusting.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Levrini, Abigail L. (2025)_  
➤ Highlights how **motivation and follow-through** break down over time. Recommends **daily tracking logs, process-based goals, and external rewards** to keep momentum. Suggests following up on failed attempts by adjusting the plan or seeking help — a key concept in sustained ADHD success.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)_  
➤ Introduces the A-N-S-W-E-R model with **"Repeat and Reinforce"** as essential for ADHD brains that forget goals in the moment. Recommends **end-of-day review routines** to reflect and reset. Advocates charting symptoms and reviewing medication impact as structured forms of follow-up.

### 🧩 Systems for Sustained Action

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024)_  
➤ Notes that most planning tools fail without a **workflow for follow-up**. Introduces **Daily Detours and Weekly Focus Sessions** as built-in review points. Encourages **tracking completion times** and using accountability metrics to create tangible proof of progress.

**Productivity With ADHD: Complete Guide**  
_Domi, Ju. (2025)_  
➤ Offers a full **Progress Tracking System** with ADHD-friendly metrics, frequency schedules, and review checkpoints. Includes a **Recovery Protocol** for when you fall off — with re-entry steps and self-forgiveness baked in.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
_Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard. (2015)_  
➤ Shows that **check-ins from others** dramatically increase the odds of follow-through. Suggests scheduling someone to "check back" and help track progress. Recommends self-monitoring like **logging reps at the gym** — but for goals.

### 🤝 The Role of Support & Accountability

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Solden, Sari & Frank, Michelle. (2019)_  
➤ Names **internalized shame and fear of judgment** as drivers of apparent follow-through failure. Encourages addressing those first, then layering on support systems and structure.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Carder, Meredith. (2024)_  
➤ Emphasizes how **community acts as a social follow-up mechanism**. ADHD brains crave interest and feedback — and peers can help you return to long-term goals. Their goal worksheets include planning for **unpreferred tasks** and anticipating roadblocks.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
_Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne. (2024)_  
➤ Shares how **body doubling, initial task scripting, and shared admin tasks** are practical, non-judgmental ways to support ADHD follow-through. Focus is on **unlocking action**, not pushing perfection.

**How to ADHD**  
_McCabe, Jessica. (2024)_  
➤ Validates **frequent check-ins** as a real accommodation. Notes that **external reminders and written communication** are essential — even between two ADHDers. Forgetting isn’t flakiness; it’s biology.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_Kustow, James. (2024)_  
➤ Stresses the importance of **responding to follow-up** rather than hoping it disappears. Urges ADHDers to **“never ignore a chase”** — even if it feels overwhelming in the moment.

### 🗣️ Coaching & Change Psychology

**Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change and Grow**  
_Miller, William R., & Rollnick, Stephen. (2023)_  
➤ While not ADHD-specific, this book outlines how **persistence support, accountability conversations, and evaluating learning** are all forms of follow-up. Describes post-consultation follow-up and proactive reconnection as crucial for long-term change — especially for neurodivergent folks.
