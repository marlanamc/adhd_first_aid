## 🍱 Sources for Meal Prepping

Explore the books, guides, and practical tools that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD affects meal prep — from decision fatigue and executive dysfunction to sensory overwhelm and emotional resistance — and offer strategies like batch cooking, ADHD Easy Meals, low-mess tools, freezer resets, and flexible prep routines that make food feel doable again.

---

### 🧠 Rethinking Prep as Permission, Not Perfection

**Healthy Happy ADHD: Transform How You Move, Eat, and Feel**  
_Dee, Lisa. (2025). Harmony/Rodale/Convergent._  
➤ Introduces **ADHD Easy Meals**: fast, flexible, low-prep meals built around protein and convenience. Reframes prep as a permission slip to make meals easier. Suggests stocking pre-chopped veggies, frozen meals, and condiments to reduce work. Emphasizes **one-pan meals**, nontraditional combos (e.g., oatmeal for dinner), and **functional nourishment** over food rules.

**The Anti-Planner**
_Donovan, Dani. (2022). Penguin Life._ 
➤ Validates burnout and offers **100 no-cook meal ideas**, sorted by appliance. Prioritizes **"something over nothing"** and **easy food over perfect meals**. Encourages **low-mess prep**, appliance hacks (e.g., air fryers), and keeping instant options on hand.

**Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything**  
_Fogg, BJ. (2020). HMH Books._  
➤ Encourages building prep habits by starting tiny. Use existing anchors (“After I start the kettle…”) and attach **mini prep actions** like pre-chopping or pulling ingredients. Reframes habit-building as behavior design, not discipline.

### 🧩 Executive Function & Simplified Systems

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD: The 7-Pillar Plan for Focus, Productivity, and Inner Peace**  
_Kustow, James. (2024). Ebury Publishing._  
➤ Emphasizes **batch cooking, freezing, and labeling leftovers**. Encourages **standardizing dishes and crockery** to reduce visual clutter. Suggests **rotating recipe boards**, prepping protein in bulk, and using **prep kits** to minimize overwhelm. Recommends cleaning as you go and using meal kits for support.

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia: Unlocking the Potential**  
_Miller, Lucinda & Derbyshire, Emma. (2025). Jessica Kingsley Publishers._  
➤ Provides **20 nutrient-dense, simple recipes** with overlapping ingredients. Encourages batch cooking for stability and **Mediterranean-style eating** (frequent small meals, balanced macros). Highlights **snack breaks during brain work** for blood sugar stability.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique. (2024). New Harbinger Publications._  
➤ Recommends **outsourcing food prep** (meal kits, frozen meals) when executive function is low. Encourages prepping around dietary needs and budget, and emphasizes **building systems that reduce internal demands**.

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018). ADHD Homestead Press._  
➤ Advocates using **Bullet Journals to track meal planning and prep**, especially when executive memory fails. Suggests prepping in response to life rhythms — not the calendar — and prioritizing **retrievable, reusable systems**.

### 🍳 Mise en Place, Planning, and Workflow

**Building a Second Brain**  
_Forte, Tiago. (2022). Profile Books._  
➤ Applies the **mise en place concept** to cooking and productivity. Encourages moving "recipes" from resource folders into active projects (“Cooking”) so you can **act without thinking**. Suggests using prep time to **externalize memory**, automate routines, and reduce start-up energy.

**The PARA Method**  
_Forte, Tiago. (2023). Profile Books._  
➤ Reframes kitchen and food systems as **Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives**. Meal prep becomes a short-term project with **rotating focus**, with Recipes as Resources to draw from when needed.

**Tiny Experiments: How to Live Freely in a Goal-Obsessed World**  
_Le Cunff, Anne-Laure. (2025). Avery._  
➤ Encourages **treating meal prep like a life experiment** — test, tweak, and reflect. Remove shame around failure and iterate toward systems that feel good. Prep doesn’t have to look the same every week.

### 🍴 Emotional Load & ADHD-Friendly Routines

**The Disorganized Mind: Coaching Your ADHD Brain to Take Control**  
_Ratey, Nancy A. (2008). St. Martin’s Griffin._  
➤ Suggests prepping **ingredients the night before**, storing them in labeled bags for fast cooking the next day. Recommends treating **grocery shopping as a non-negotiable routine**. Advises meeting with a nutritionist and **simplifying the cooking process into discrete tasks**.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
_Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy. (2006). Scribner._  
➤ Links meal overwhelm to poor planning. Suggests using a **"Planning Notebook"** to list recipes, prep routines, and grocery checklists. Recommends turning repetitive prep into habits and batching where possible.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen. (2019). New Harbinger Publications._  
➤ Acknowledges how **routines like meal prep can fall apart under burnout**. Recommends shifting from guilt to curiosity and using **predictable systems** to reduce overwhelm. Encourages asking for help, simplifying, or delaying prep without shame.

### 🧠 Energy, Motivation & Habit Loops

**Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change and Grow**  
_Miller, William R. & Rollnick, Stephen. (2023). The Guilford Press._  
➤ Encourages exploring **your “why” behind food changes** before pushing the “how.” Suggests using **Ask–Offer–Ask** and **visual decision maps** (e.g., bubble sheets) to weigh prep strategies. Useful for self-motivated change without shame.

**How to ADHD: An Insider’s Guide to Working with Your Brain**  
_McCabe, Jessica. (2024). Potter/Ten Speed/Harmony/Rodale._  
➤ Recommends **improving recall with visual cues and processing time**. Suggests **writing down steps for prep** to reduce memory loss under stress. Uses hyperfixation to streamline repetitive meals.

**Real Self-Care**  
_Lakshmin, Pooja. (2023). Penguin Publishing Group._  
➤ Discusses the **cognitive dimension of household labor**, such as coordinating, remembering, and initiating meal prep. Validates fatigue around repetitive domestic tasks and encourages redistributing this load.
