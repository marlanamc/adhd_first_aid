## 🍽️ Sources for Meal Planning

Explore the books, research, and lived experiences that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD impacts meal planning — from executive dysfunction and time blindness to sensory aversions and motivation slumps — and offer strategies like ADHD Easy Meals, batching, visual tools, and flexible routines to reduce friction and improve follow-through.

---

### 🧠 Why Meal Planning Matters

**Healthy Happy ADHD: Transform How You Move, Eat, and Feel**  
_Dee, Lisa. (2025). Harmony/Rodale/Convergent._  
➤ Introduces **ADHD Easy Meals**: simple, fast, low-cleanup, protein-based. Emphasizes honoring **hyperfixations**, preparing **strategically for low-energy days**, and compromising with takeout when needed. Describes how feeding oneself can be a **complex ADHD challenge** involving decision fatigue and overstimulation.

**How to Keep House While Drowning**  
_Davis, KC. (2022). Simon Element._  
➤ Reminds us that **meal planning exists to make eating easier**, not harder. Suggests flexible routines — if you like a meal, write it down. Endorses **kid food, snacks, and low-effort options** on hard days. Reframes meal planning with **self-compassion and permission to simplify**.

**Mad About Money: Managing Finances (and Life) with ADHD**  
_Alexander-Grout, Maddy. (2025). John Wiley & Sons._  
➤ Discusses **food budgeting** as part of broader ADHD-friendly financial planning. Suggests integrating meal planning with cost-saving strategies and planning for **impulse-resistant shopping**.

**Brain Brilliance: 60 Nourishing Recipes and a Nutritional Plan for Kids and Teens with ADHD and Autism**  
_Miller, Lucinda. (2024). Quadrille Publishing._  
➤ Offers **60 recipes tailored to neurodivergent palates**, with brain-friendly ingredients and minimal prep. Encourages **gradual experimentation**, like trying one new recipe a week. Recommends aiming for **80% home-cooked and 20% convenience**.

### 🧩 Executive Function, Structure & Simplification

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia: A Practical Guide to Food, Diet and Supplementation**  
_Derbyshire, Emma. (2025). Jessica Kingsley Publishers._  
➤ Offers **cost-effective, easy recipes** and emphasizes **scheduling meals** like appointments. Warns against skipping breakfast and promotes **smaller, consistent meals**. Includes a kitchen staples guide and tips for minimizing overwhelm.

**The Disorganized Mind: Coaching Your ADHD Brain to Take Control**  
_Ratey, Nancy A. (2008). St. Martin’s Griffin._  
➤ Encourages **planning meals in advance**, organizing grocery lists by store layout, and **treating grocery shopping as a standing appointment**. Recommends **precooking and freezing** food on weekends and always having snacks on hand.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD: The 7-Pillar Plan for Focus, Productivity, and Inner Peace**  
_Kustow, James. (2024). Ebury Publishing._  
➤ Recommends **weekly/fortnightly meal cycles**, **visual menu boards**, and **recipe folders**. Suggests **batch cooking** and using **meal kits** to reduce prep and avoid food decision fatigue.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Carder, Meredith. (2024). Hay House, Inc._  
➤ Discusses **overwhelm at the grocery store**, **forgotten produce**, and **post-work cooking slumps**. Offers visual tools (e.g., **magnetic whiteboards**, fridge photos), **deal-a-meal index cards**, and simplified batch prep (cook one protein, rotate meals).

### 🛠️ Tools, Templates & Tactical Planning

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018). ADHD Homestead Press._  
➤ Encourages using a **Bullet Journal** for grocery lists, kitchen notes, and tracking meal prep. Stresses indexing and **retrieval systems** to make planning sustainable.

**Productivity With ADHD: Complete Guide**  
_Domi, Ju. (2025)._  
➤ Offers templates for **time-blocking**, **energy management**, and **habit-building**, all of which can support sustainable meal planning routines.

**Feel-Good Productivity: How to Do More of What Matters to You**  
_Abdaal, Ali. (2023). Celadon Books._  
➤ Suggests applying **mise-en-place** principles — prep everything ahead of time to reduce decision-making and streamline execution. Encourages intentionality over hustle.

**Building a Second Brain**  
_Forte, Tiago. (2022). Profile Books._  
➤ Introduces the **kitchen analogy** for PARA: turn resources into action by moving recipes into “Cooking” projects. Emphasizes **low-friction organization** for quick access and smoother execution.

**The PARA Method**  
_Forte, Tiago. (2023). Profile Books._  
➤ Reinforces the idea of **recipe organization** as part of project planning. Helps shift meal prep from an idea into an actionable system.

### 🍳 Mental Energy & "Low-Effort" Food Options

**The Anti-Planner**  
_Donovan, Dani. (2022). Penguin Life._  
➤ Provides **“Ideas to feed yourself when you just can’t anymore”**, categorized by appliance. Includes low-effort, low-mess options like instant noodles, frozen waffles, and toaster meals — validating real-life burnout meals.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024). John Wiley & Sons._  
➤ Frames meal planning as part of **intentional time design**. Encourages using **whiteboards, visual menus, and recipe cards** as anchors for planning. Notes how **time blindness** and energy dips can disrupt even good intentions.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
_Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy. (2006). Scribner._  
➤ Describes **"Mealtime Mania"** and recommends **checklists, planning notebooks**, and **rock-bottom plans** (e.g., cereal for dinner) for overwhelmed days. Encourages sharing responsibilities and building predictable rhythms.

### 🧠 Cognitive Load & Daily Functioning

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique. (2024). New Harbinger Publications._  
➤ Suggests creating meal plans and grocery lists that account for **sensory needs, energy levels, and budget**. Encourages rejecting neuronormative ideals in favor of **systems that work for your brain**.

**Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change and Grow**  
_Miller, William R. & Rollnick, Stephen. (2023). The Guilford Press._  
➤ Shares decision-making tools like **“Focusing”**, **bubble sheets**, and **decisional balance charts**, which can be used to clarify food goals and reduce overwhelm when changing eating habits.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
_Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard. (2015). Guilford Press._  
➤ Emphasizes **externalizing plans** and using **implementation intentions** (e.g., “If I’m hungry at 12:00, I’ll eat the premade burrito”) over vague goals. Encourages using planners and food logs to track behavior and stay on track.

**Tiny Habits**  
_Fogg, BJ. (2020). Houghton Mifflin Harcourt._  
➤ Identifies **meal prep as a habit hurdle** and encourages attaching small food-related behaviors to existing anchors (e.g., "After I open the fridge, I’ll grab a protein shake"). Shows how **tiny changes** can build lasting systems.
