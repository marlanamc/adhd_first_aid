## 🧠 Impulse Control & Spending Strategies

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD or Other Disorders**  
_(Undated, likely early 2010s)_  
➤ Recommends pausing before purchasing: **consider the consequences** to help regulate impulsive buying behavior.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_James Kustow (2024)_  
➤ Suggests placing a **visual reminder (like a blob of Blue-tack) on your bank card** to interrupt automatic spending and encourage mindful reflection at the point of purchase.

**It All Makes Sense Now: Embrace Your ADHD Brain to Live a**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Warns that ADHD brains are **especially vulnerable to retail marketing**. Recommends setting **one mini-goal at a time** and **weekly evaluations** to keep behavior change sustainable.

## 💸 ADHD Tax, Financial Shame & Emotional Triggers

**Mad About Money: Managing Finances (and Life) with ADHD**  
_Maddy Alexander-Grout (2025)_  
➤ Encourages **examining the emotional “why” behind spending**, exploring **cheaper alternatives (e.g., second-hand)**, and avoiding self-blame after impulse shopping. Introduces the concept of **“ADHD Tax”** — extra costs due to executive dysfunction like buying duplicates or forgetting returns.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
_Richard Pink & Roxanne Pink (2024)_  
➤ Defines **“ADHD Tax”** as financial fallout from ADHD traits — such as **buying replacements for misplaced items** or **forgetting to return ill-fitting clothes**. Highlights the need for systems, not shame.

## 🧠 Overload, Executive Dysfunction & Emotional Regulation

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Sari Solden, Michelle Frank & Ellen Littman (2019)_  
➤ Shopping can be **logistically and emotionally taxing** for ADHDers, especially due to **internalized expectations** and **marketing susceptibility**. Recommends **setting small behavioral goals** and tracking progress weekly to manage overwhelm and avoid shame cycles.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Describes shopping (especially grocery shopping) as a **stifling executive function task**. Suggests that **prioritization and structure** can help make these everyday responsibilities feel more manageable.
