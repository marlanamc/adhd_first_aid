## Sources for Writing Emails

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD impacts email-related tasks — from overthinking and inbox paralysis to missed follow-ups and digital clutter — and offer strategies like templates, batching, body doubling, and workflow design to help ADHD brains write, send, and manage emails with more ease.

---

### 🧠 Understanding ADHD & Email Challenges

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Highlights how ADHD brains often **overthink emails**, trying to "sound just right," which leads to task avoidance and burnout. Recommends **using templates** to bypass perfectionism and save mental energy.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018)  
➤ Emphasizes **Inbox Zero is possible** with structured habits. Advises processing emails **one at a time**, immediately acting on tasks under 2–5 minutes, and moving longer ones to a project manager. Suggests **frequent inbox clearing** and seeking **body doubling** support if overwhelmed.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Suggests **using folders, flags, and workflows** to sort email types. Recommends checking priority folders daily instead of relying on your inbox. Encourages setting boundaries like **"Do Not Disturb"** for focused work.

### 🛠️ Practical Strategies for Writing & Managing Email

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Recommends **email follow-ups after meetings** to confirm action steps. Suggests **dumping tasks from emails into a master task list** and acting on follow-up emails **immediately** to avoid dopamine-driven distractions.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2025)  
➤ Encourages **checking email only at scheduled times** to prevent derailment. Suggests using email as a **reward after focused work** and **requesting written instructions** for better clarity and memory support.

**The Anti-Planner**  
Donovan, Dani (2022)  
➤ Promotes creating **copy/paste email templates** and **labeling/archiving** messages after responding. Emphasizes writing tasks using **clear verbs, specificity, and time limits** for better follow-through.

**The Coaching Habit**  
Stanier, Michael Bungay (2015)  
➤ Suggests **asking clarifying questions** in response to long emails to avoid crafting overly long replies. Focuses on saving time and staying efficient in written communication.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Recommends drafting emails with a **specific tone** (lighthearted, professional, etc.) to support emotional clarity and intention in communication.

### 💡 Communication & Self-Advocacy

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
Solden, Sari (2013)  
➤ Advocates for **requesting written instructions** at work to prevent forgetfulness and reduce shame about memory lapses. Offers scripts for clearly communicating personal needs in professional settings.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Encourages **redefining what "hard work" looks like** with ADHD. Validates email avoidance due to overthinking and promotes embracing natural rhythms and using tools to simplify.

---

### 🗂️ Digital Organization & System Support

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Highlights the importance of **digital systems** for email and message management as part of reducing overwhelm and increasing mental clarity. Aligns communication strategies with neurodivergent thinking patterns.

**73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now**  
ADDitude Experts (2012)  
➤ Suggests **sorting incoming items (emails or paper) into action categories** and managing with a two-list system: “**Action List**” for urgent items and a “**Parking Lot**” for the rest.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD or Other Disorders**  
The Authors (n.d.)  
➤ Offers general productivity tips, but **does not include email-specific strategies**.
