## 📚 Sources for Cleaning Out the Fridge

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources support strategies for tackling emotionally loaded, repetitive tasks like fridge cleaning — with practical tips, emotional validation, and ADHD-friendly momentum hacks.

---

### 🧊 Fridge-Specific Guides

- **Donovan, Dani.** _The Anti-Planner_  
    Provides a detailed **“Fridge Overhaul Challenge”**, with ADHD-friendly steps like playing music, using before/after photos, throwing out moldy containers without guilt, and scheduling tasks around grocery delivery. Emphasizes sensory strategies, accountability tricks (like filming a time-lapse), and flexible reorganization of fridge zones to reduce future waste.
    
- **Kustow, James.** _How to Thrive with Adult ADHD_  
    Recommends a **“fridge cleanse every couple of months,”** offering clear steps for removing, cleaning, drying, and restocking food. Encourages realistic expectations (e.g., avoiding harsh cleaners, full defrosting only as needed) and treating this as part of broader home maintenance, not a one-off event.
    
- **"100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD or Other Disorders"**  
    Suggests **dedicating a regular day** (like trash day or before grocery shopping) to clean out old food — reinforcing the idea of habit-stacking and environmental anchoring.

### 🧠 Transferable Strategies from Similar Tasks

- **Solden, Sari, Frank, Michelle & Littman, Ellen.** _A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD_  
    Describes how tasks like cleaning out closets — often overwhelming to start — can bring relief and lightness after completion. Supports the **emotional payoff** and reframing of repetitive, low-reward tasks like fridge cleaning.
    
- **Carder, Meredith.** _It All Makes Sense Now_  
    Notes the burnout from recurring home care like dishes and cooking. Recommends prepping freezer-friendly meals to reduce load — an indirect but helpful connection to fridge strategy, meal planning, and clearing space.
