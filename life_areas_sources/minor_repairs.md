## 🔧 Sources for Minor Repairs

Explore the books, guides, and real-life strategies that shaped this page. These resources reveal how ADHD makes minor home repairs extra challenging — from tool chaos and task initiation issues to forgotten steps and emotional overwhelm — and offer support strategies like checklists, breakdowns, visual tools, and permission to get help or outsource when needed.

---

### 🧠 Home Management with ADHD in Mind

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018). ADHD Homestead Press._  
➤ Introduces a **“trouble ticket” system** to log home maintenance issues and repairs. Suggests **breaking down small tasks** (e.g., hanging a painting) into steps, keeping **duplicate tools in different rooms**, and **placing supplies where they’re needed**. Encourages partnering on projects and tracking progress over time.

**How to Keep House While Drowning**  
_Davis, KC. (2022). Simon Element._  
➤ Frames repairs as part of **functional care**, not moral failure. Suggests using **adaptive equipment** (e.g., drill scrubbers, grab bars), **monthly maintenance lists**, and gentle mindset tools like **labels and “half-done is done”** logic. Encourages asking for or hiring help as a valid option.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Kemp, Jennifer & Mitchelson, Monique. (2024). New Harbinger Publications._  
➤ Encourages seeking **outside labor or handymen** when tasks are too overwhelming. Offers mindset reframes like “Let’s find a way to fix this” and emphasizes building **external scaffolds** for physical or executive support.

### 🧩 Breakdowns, Checklists & Outsourcing

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
_Dawson, Peg & Guare, Richard. (2015). Guilford Press._  
➤ Suggests **breaking down repairs into checklist steps**, even citing medical studies on the power of checklists. Offers examples of ADHD-friendly breakdowns like “building a cabinet” and encourages **modifying the environment** to make tasks easier (e.g., storing tools in visible, reachable locations).

**Start Finishing: How to Go from Idea to Done**  
_Gilkey, Charlie. (2019). Sounds True._  
➤ Lists home tasks like **“Organizing the Closet of Doom”** and **“Yard Work”** as legitimate projects. Suggests batching similar tasks and **outsourcing where it makes sense**. Emphasizes scheduling time for cleanup and tool maintenance as part of the task.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024). John Wiley & Sons._  
➤ Encourages breaking vague tasks like **“Organize Garage”** into **tiny actions**. Recommends using **binders, lists, and “Area folders”** for home projects (e.g., Repairs, Remodeling). Promotes regular check-ins to keep things moving forward.

**The PARA Method**  
_Forte, Tiago. (2023). Profile Books._  
➤ Offers a case study of a **bathroom renovation** to show how to organize repair info: quotes, measurements, photos. Suggests organizing repairs under a **“Home” Area**, distinct from passive Resources, and **digitally tagging to-do items** across systems.

### 🛠️ Tools, Task Initiation & Setup Support

**How to ADHD**  
_McCabe, Jessica. (2024). Potter/Ten Speed/Harmony/Rodale._  
➤ Recommends investing in **tools you actually enjoy using**, and asks “Do we do this? Do we let it go? Do we get help?” as a way to navigate household upkeep. Encourages **removing wallpaper** only if it’s worth it — don’t waste executive energy on guilt.

**73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now**  
_ADDitude Experts. (2012). University of Rochester Medical Center._  
➤ Suggests setting up **tool-specific zones**, organizing **painting, gardening, or repair tools together**, and adding them to a chore file. Mentions **filing repair receipts**, setting up **weekly task rotation**, and asking for help when a job is too big.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_Kustow, James. (2024). Ebury Publishing._  
➤ Recommends breaking tasks like **“sort kitchen”** into **micro steps**. Suggests **trading services with friends** for disliked tasks (e.g., ironing or repairs). Emphasizes building routines for general home care.

### 🔩 Emotional & Relational Aspects of Repairs

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
_Orlov, Melissa. (2010). Independent Publishers Group._  
➤ Discusses **conflict over chores and repairs**. Suggests determining what you're not good at and **outsourcing where possible** (e.g., hiring a carpenter to fix porch steps). Frames shared home care as a **relationship dynamic**, not just a task.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
_Pink, Richard & Pink, Roxanne. (2024). Vintage Publishing._  
➤ Highlights that **IKEA assembly and home tasks can feel insurmountable**, and that ADHDers often need external support or guided steps to complete these. Validates emotional fatigue around "simple" tasks.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen. (2019). New Harbinger Publications._  
➤ Encourages **asking a friend to keep you company while working on tasks** — including cleaning or minor repairs. Emotional support can bridge the activation gap.

### 🧠 Mindset, Space Design & Mess Management

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
_Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy. (2006). Scribner._  
➤ Offers practical strategies for **mess management and “permanent homes” for tools and random objects**. Recommends using **home organization systems**, keeping **records of repairs**, and managing overwhelm from **“remodel the whole house” syndrome**.

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
_Solden, Sari. (2013). eBookIt.com._  
➤ Recommends visual tools like **diagrams for where things go**, family support, and reframing perfectionism. Encourages **spring cleaning the internal world** to help manage outer chaos.

**The Anti-Planner**  
_Donovan, Dani. (2022). Penguin Life._  
➤ Embraces disorganization as a starting point. Offers playful strategies to **“make cleaning entertaining”**, and validates using support books like _How to Keep House While Drowning_ for tackling blocked home tasks.

**ADHD 2.0**  
_Hallowell, Edward M. & Ratey, John J. (2021). Ballantine Books._  
➤ Reinforces that **completing even small chores** offers dopamine and self-esteem boosts. Encourages building **scaffolding to finish tasks** — whether it’s tools, routines, or cheerleading from others.
