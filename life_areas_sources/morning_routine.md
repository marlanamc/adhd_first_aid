## ☀️ Sources for Morning Routines

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain the neurological roots of ADHD morning struggles — from activation delays and time blindness to working memory gaps — and offer strategies like sleep hygiene, transition rituals, protein-based breakfasts, external prompts, and personalized rhythms that support ADHD brains.

---

### 🧠 Understanding the ADHD Brain & Routines

**ADHD 2.0** — Hallowell, Edward M., & Ratey, John J. (2021)  
➤ Recommends structure and writing down tasks to reinforce priorities neurologically. Encourages backup reminders and systems.

**How to ADHD** — McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Highlights the trial-and-error nature of routines. Emphasizes using alert periods strategically and tailoring your morning setup.

**The Disorganized Mind** — Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Warns that routines must be customized — other people’s strategies may backfire. Advocates for start/stop times and scheduled breaks.

**Unapologetically ADHD** — Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Emphasizes shame-free use of external supports like alarms and whiteboards. Encourages daily self-check-ins (Daily Detours) and resilient planning workflows.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!** — Kelly, Kate, & Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ Reframes self-blame and promotes meditative approaches to calm the "adrenalized" brain and support emotional regulation.

### 🌅 Morning Routine Building Blocks

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD or Other Disorders**  
➤ Encourages creating a structured morning routine and maintaining consistent wake-up and hygiene habits.

**Daily Routine Examples from Adults with ADHD** — ADDitude Editors (2025)  
➤ Shares practical examples like mental rehearsals in the shower, prepping clothes, and avoiding social media upon waking.

**Productivity With ADHD – Complete Guide** — DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Introduces a “Morning Launch Sequence” and “Power Block” (90–120 min) for deep work. Recommends flexible time blocks and foundational self-care.

**Order from Chaos** — Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Recommends placing easier tasks in the early morning and using logs to track progress. Emphasizes lifelong process over perfection.

**Healthy Happy ADHD** — Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Suggests avoiding phones first thing and syncing with natural light. Prioritizes consistency and trusting personal rhythms.

**Tiny Habits** — Fogg, BJ. (2020)  
➤ Promotes anchoring new habits to existing ones. Encourages small wins and instant celebration to reinforce behavior.

**Tiny Experiments** — Le Cunff, Anne-Laure (2025)  
➤ Suggests “morning pages” to process thoughts and mentally clear the path for the day.

---

### 🌙 Night Prep for Better Mornings

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD** — Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Recommends wind-down routines to improve sleep and cautions against rigidity when routines fail.

**Start Finishing** — Gilkey, Charlie (2019)  
➤ Emphasizes “downcycling” through bedtime rituals (e.g., water, brushing teeth, journaling). Suggests “work warm-ups” for smoother daily starts.

**Feel-Good Productivity** — Abdaal, Ali (2023)  
➤ Encourages using nighttime rituals to support a productive “Morning Power Block” and reduce self-regulatory strain.

---

### 🍳 Sleep, Nutrition, and Energy Regulation

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia** — Derbyshire, Emma (2025)  
➤ Recommends a **protein-rich breakfast** for dopamine support, energy, and focus. Stresses the brain-body connection.

**It All Makes Sense Now** — Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Offers strategies like waking during light sleep, protein at breakfast, and visual “routine boxes” to track progress.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD** — Levrini, Abigail L. (2023)  
➤ Recommends tracking time spent on tasks to correct time blindness. Affirms that most adults with ADHD thrive with structure.

---

### 💬 Emotional Reframing & Self-Motivation

**How to Keep House While Drowning** — Davis, KC (2022)  
➤ Replaces rigid routines with compassionate “rhythms” and flexible structure. Encourages re-entry after setbacks without shame.

**Real Self-Care** — Lakshmin, Pooja (2023)  
➤ Reframes routines as personal choices grounded in internal motivation, not external performance.

**Motivational Interviewing** — Miller, William R., & Rollnick, Stephen (2023)  
➤ Highlights the importance of intrinsic motivation and collaborative change — essential for building sustainable morning habits.

**The Anti-Planner** — Donovan, Dani (2022)  
➤ Emphasizes starting small, embracing systems, and letting go of shame about needing external supports.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success** — Dawson, Peg, & Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Recommends checklists to support working memory and build executive skills foundational to daily routines.
