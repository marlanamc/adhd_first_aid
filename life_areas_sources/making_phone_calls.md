## 📞 Sources for Making Phone Calls

Explore the books, research, and lived experiences that shaped this page. These resources explain why making phone calls is so hard with ADHD — from working memory challenges and sensory overwhelm to phone-phobia and emotional avoidance — and offer strategies like scripting, batching, accountability, and self-compassion to reduce resistance and increase follow-through.

---

### 🧠 Understanding Phone Avoidance & Phone-Phobia

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
_Kelly, Kate & Ramundo, Peggy. (2006). Scribner._  
➤ Introduces **"Phone-a-Phobia"** as a common ADHD struggle — not irrational, but rooted in **real processing challenges** (no visual cues, distractions, impulsivity). Suggests using **scripts**, call prep, and designated quiet time. Encourages scheduling calls, tracking call attempts, and viewing phone use ergonomically (e.g., keeping directories and steno pads within reach).

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen. (2019). New Harbinger Publications._  
➤ Highlights how **shame and fear of sounding disorganized** can lead to deep avoidance of calls. Recommends writing down info before and during calls, using **low-stakes voicemails to build confidence**, and reframing call-based connection. Encourages **"one-minute" first steps**, planning around meds, and **avoiding over-apologizing** when reconnecting.

**The Disorganized Mind: Coaching Your ADHD Brain to Take Control**  
_Ratey, Nancy A. (2008). St. Martin’s Griffin._  
➤ Warns that **unreturned calls hurt relationships** and offers batching, voicemail screening, and segmented call lists as ways to reduce resistance. Suggests acknowledging missed calls rather than ghosting. Promotes using voice memos to avoid multitasking hazards.

**Mad About Money: Managing Finances (and Life) with ADHD**  
_Alexander-Grout, Maddy. (2025). John Wiley & Sons._  
➤ Names **transition paralysis and hold-time frustration** as major phone barriers. Shares real-life examples of calling all debt collectors in one morning to get it over with. Suggests **doing hard calls with someone nearby** or authorizing someone else to speak on your behalf.

### 🛠️ Systems, Scripts & Call Organization

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD: The 7-Pillar Plan for Focus, Productivity, and Inner Peace**  
_Kustow, James. (2024). Ebury Publishing._  
➤ Offers a **structured phone call template**: prep notes, summarize current understanding, clarify actions, and follow-up by email. Advises batching calls, scheduling durations, and **adding tasks immediately to your planner**. Validates asking people to send requests via text or email when needed.

**Unapologetically ADHD: A Step-by-Step Framework for Everyday**  
_Kinzer, Nikki & Wright, Pete D. (2024). John Wiley & Sons._  
➤ Emphasizes **systems over memory**. Highlights that new info during calls (like dates and tasks) must be **captured immediately**. Reframes voicemails, DMs, and inboxes as “unprocessed input” that need a defined system. Encourages **verb-based task phrasing** (e.g., “Call José”) to improve clarity and follow-through.

**Order from Chaos: The Everyday Grind of Staying Organized with Adult ADHD**  
_Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018). ADHD Homestead Press._  
➤ Leverages **ADHD inertia** — if you're already in phone mode, **batch more calls**. Recommends tools like **Bluetooth landlines**, voice memos, or call prep stations to reduce friction. Encourages externalizing call-related information to minimize overwhelm and forgotten details.

### 🗣️ Communication Skills & Mental Load

**Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change and Grow**  
_Miller, William R. & Rollnick, Stephen. (2023). The Guilford Press._  
➤ Offers conversation models like **Ask–Offer–Ask (AOA)** to foster clarity and avoid lecturing. Encourages **asking permission to share**, using **curiosity** before advice, and **tailoring information** to what the other person wants to hear.

**The Coaching Habit**  
_Stanier, Michael Bungay. (2015). Page Two Books._  
➤ Notes how quickly people interrupt during conversations. Recommends **asking short, open-ended questions** to stay grounded and gain time when mentally overloaded during a phone conversation.

**The Art of Gathering**  
_Parker, Priya. (2018). Penguin Publishing Group._  
➤ Describes using **pre-call workbooks and strategic questions** to facilitate connection — even over distance. Shows that **meaningful conversation over the phone is possible with intention** and thoughtful structure.

**Tiny Experiments: How to Live Freely in a Goal-Obsessed World**  
_Le Cunff, Anne-Laure. (2025). Avery._  
➤ Quotes Virginia Woolf on how **phone calls can sap creative energy**, validating that not everyone functions well with synchronous voice communication.

### 🧠 Emotional Support & Energy Preservation

**How to ADHD: An Insider’s Guide to Working with Your Brain**  
_McCabe, Jessica. (2024). Potter/Ten Speed/Harmony/Rodale._  
➤ Acknowledges that certain tasks (like phone calls or laundry) may be bartered, outsourced, or avoided based on personal energy. Reinforces the need to **adapt tasks to your brain, not the other way around**.

**Real Self-Care: A Transformative Program for Redefining Wellness**  
_Lakshmin, Pooja. (2023). Penguin Publishing Group._  
➤ Discusses the **mental and emotional labor** of being the one responsible for initiating and managing logistics (like scheduling calls). Reinforces boundaries and **permission to protect your energy**.
