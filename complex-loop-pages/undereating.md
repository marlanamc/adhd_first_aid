## 🍽️ Undereating 

Undereating often flies under the radar,  especially with ADHD. You may forget meals, avoid effort-heavy food, or lose track of hunger cues entirely. It’s not about control,  it’s **low interoception**, **executive dysfunction**, and **dopamine imbalance** disrupting your basic needs.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're not **lazy or careless** for forgetting to eat, **ADHD can mute hunger cues** and **disconnect you from your body**. You might **not feel hungry until it's too late**. Try **pre-set eating times**, **visual cues**, or **prepping simple, no-decision meals**. **Nourishment isn't indulgent**, it's **necessary**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're **starving your brain** and expecting it to **function**. **Skipping meals isn't self-discipline**, it's **sabotage**. **You don't need to "earn" food.** **Eat a real meal**, **on time**, **every day**. **Set alarms**. **Prep it in advance**. No more **running on caffeine and guilt**.


### 🧠 Why Eating Regularly is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Forget to eat until you're dizzy, shaky, or irritable
  - Feel "too busy" or overwhelmed to bother with food
  - Eat one small meal and realize it was your only one all day
  - Get grossed out by textures, smells, or the idea of food altogether

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 📅 **Time blindness** makes it hard to notice when hours have passed without food  
  - 💡 **Hyperfocus** causes you to block out hunger signals completely  
  - 🤔 **Working memory struggles** mean you forget to eat or can't hold the steps of a meal plan in your head  
  - 🍽️ **Sensory sensitivity** makes food textures or smells feel unbearable  
  - 🙃 **All-or-nothing thinking** means if it's not a "perfect" meal, you might skip it entirely  
  - 😪 **ADHD medications** can suppress appetite during your most productive hours


### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Eating

- **Make eating easier than skipping**: Remove barriers and prep in advance  
- **Externalize reminders**: Your brain can't be trusted to remember meals  
- **Honor sensory needs**: Keep safe foods available for difficult days  
- **Progress over perfection**: Any food is better than no food

### 🛠️ Strategies for Regular Eating

#### 🍼 Make Eating Easier Than Skipping It

- **Keep easy, high-protein foods visible and ready** (e.g., boiled eggs, yogurt tubes, microwaveable meals)
- **Prep in batches**: Cook one big protein source (like grilled chicken or tofu) for the week
- **Repeat meals**: Eat the same breakfast/lunch every day if it simplifies decisions
- **ADHD "easy meals"**: Prioritize low-effort, low-cleanup meals you can assemble in 5 minutes
- **Use smoothies**: Blend protein powder, nut butter, fruit, and milk when solids are unappealing
    

#### ⏰ Externalize and Automate Reminders

- **Set 3 daily meal alarms** (and label them with emojis to make them friendly!)
- **Leave snacks in visible spots** (desk, nightstand, car) to catch your attention
- **Use a fridge whiteboard or sticky notes** for reminders like "Eat something!" or "Lunch in bag"
    

#### 🥘 Reduce Barriers to Eating

- **Minimize dishes**: Keep paper plates or one favorite bowl clean and ready
- **Use visual cues**: Store fresh food at eye level instead of the crisper drawer
- **Stock sensory-friendly options**: Keep a go-to list of safe foods for texture-sensitive days
- **Divide big food tasks** into tiny steps: "Pull pan" → "Crack egg" → "Turn on stove"
    

#### ⚡ Optimize Your Energy and Patterns

- **Front-load your calories**: Eat a solid breakfast before stimulant meds suppress appetite
    
- **Prep meals during low-focus periods**: Use evenings or weekends for meal prep
    
- **Set a visual timer for 5 minutes** if you're avoiding food, just sit with it, no pressure to eat
    
- **Pair eating with dopamine boosts**: favorite show, cozy lighting, gentle music
    

#### 💪 Support Systems Make It Easier

- **Body double meal prep** with a friend or watch a cooking YouTuber while you prep
- **Use shared meal reminders** with roommates, partners, or accountability buddies
- **Talk to a dietitian or ADHD-informed therapist** if restrictive patterns are harming your health
    


### 🧭 Eating ≠ Morality

- Forgetting to eat doesn't make you careless or broken
- Your brain processes hunger cues differently, and that's neurological, not personal

> You're not flaky or fragile, your brain is just wired for a different rhythm. Nourishing yourself isn't about perfection. It's about working _with_ your brain, not against it.

- Remember that any food is better than no food
