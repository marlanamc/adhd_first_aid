# 📺 Screen Time 

Screen time can become a trap when your ADHD brain is chasing **dopamine**, avoiding **boredom**, or just too exhausted to switch tasks. It’s not just about “too much tech”,  it’s your nervous system **self-soothing**, **zoning out**, or avoiding harder transitions.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
You're **overstimulated and running on empty**. Screen binges offer a **pause your brain craves**, but they're **not true rest**. You don't need to **cut it all out**. Start with a **break**: **one less episode**, **one hour of phone-free time**, **one evening off the screen**. You deserve **better rest than endless content loops**.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
You're not **relaxing**, you're **zoning out**. **Bingeing screens** all day or night is a **dopamine trap disguised as self-care**. **Shut it down**. **Go outside**. **Move your body**. You are not made for **backlit dissociation**.



## 🧠 Why Screen Time Is Hard with ADHD
- You might:
	Binge-watch shows until 3 a.m. and hate yourself in the morning
	- “Check one thing” on your phone and lose an hour
	- Get lost in games or scrolling and forget to eat or shower
	- Use screens to soothe overstimulation or boredom,  and then feel guilty
	- Feel totally shut down when it's time to transition off a screen

- Here’s what’s really going on:
	- 💥 **Dopamine dysregulation** makes screens intensely rewarding and hard to stop  
	- ⏰ Time blindness causes you to lose track of how long you’ve been online  
	- 🎮 Hyperfocus makes it feel impossible to disengage,  even when you want to  
	- 🧠 Understimulation leads your brain to seek excitement and novelty via screens  
	- 🔁 Executive dysfunction makes it hard to pause and shift to something else  
	- 😵 Sensory overload or emotional overwhelm makes screen time an escape hatch


## ❤️ Shift Your Mindset

Let go of shame. Build systems that support your actual brain.

- **Radical Acceptance**: You’re not lazy or addicted. You have ADHD,  your brain seeks stimulation and struggles with transitions.
    
- **Self-Compassion**: It’s okay to struggle. You’re not failing,  you’re gathering data.
    
- **Progress Over Perfection**: You don’t need a 0% screen day to succeed. Cutting down is still a win.
    
- **Personalization is Power**: Some people thrive with screen-free mornings. Others unwind with a show. Find _your_ balance.
    


## 🔧 Build Your ADHD-Friendly Screen Time System

### ⏳ Create Structure & Boundaries

- **Time Block Screen Use**: Schedule intentional screen time so it doesn’t bleed across your day.
    
- **Screen-Free Zones**: Keep phones out of the bedroom or bathroom if those are problem areas.
    
- **Evening Shut-Down Routine**: Power down devices 1 hour before bed. Use low light and low stimulation.
    
- **"Do Not Disturb" & Flight Mode**: Give yourself permission to unplug,  the world will still be there.
    
- **Morning Delay Rule**: Avoid phone scrolling for the first 30 minutes after waking.
    

### 🧠 Externalize & Increase Awareness

- **Visual Timers**: Set a timer when you start watching/scrolling,  even better if it’s visible.
    
- **Sticky Notes**: Leave gentle reminders like “Still hungry? Still tired?” on your laptop or remote.
    
- **Time Tracking Tools**: Use Screen Time/Digital Wellbeing to monitor use and set limits.
    
- **"What’s My Goal?" Cue**: Before unlocking a screen, ask “What am I here to do?”
    


## 📱 Tech That Helps

- **App Blockers**: Use Brick, One Sec, Freedom, or Forest to limit access to black hole apps.
    
- **Hide Apps**: Move distracting apps off your home screen or into folders with funny names (“Dopamine Drain”).
    
- **YouTube Sub-Free Mode**: Log out or use a separate “no-subs” account for focused viewing.
    
- **Shortcuts**: Set up routines or Focus Modes to auto-mute distractions during work or sleep hours.
    
- **Ambient Replacements**: Replace aimless TV with calming playlists, rain sounds, or lo-fi channels.
    


## 🧠 Dopamine Management & Transitioning

- **Pre-Decide Limits**: Decide how many episodes or how long to scroll before you start.
    
- **Create a Stop Ritual**: End screen time with a stretch, water refill, or brain dump to help your brain transition.
    
- **Gamify Alternatives**: Replace binge-watching with puzzle apps, crafts, or walking podcasts,  still dopamine, just less hijacking.
    
- **Build Boredom Tolerance**: Practice doing _nothing_ for a minute before switching tasks. Boredom is a skill.
    
- **Make Screen Time Less Convenient**: Unplug your TV, hide your controller, leave your phone in another room.
    


## 🤝 Support Systems & Accountability

- **Body Doubling**: Have someone else present (IRL or virtually) while you work or rest to stay grounded.
    
- **Accountability Buddy**: Check in with someone before and after screen use.
    
- **Weekly Review**: Reflect on how screen time felt,  energizing or draining? What worked? What didn’t?
    
- **Screen-Free Dates**: Plan meals or outings without screens,  just people and real connection.
    
- **ADHD Coaching & Peer Support**: Get help designing structure that fits your life.
    


## 📌 Bonus Tips

- **Designate a Fun Slot**: Save screens you _love_ (gaming, YouTube deep dives) for an end-of-day reward.
    
- **Delay the Dopamine**: Instead of a dopamine snack _before_ your task, use it _after_ to build incentive.
    
- **Low-Energy Defaults**: On shutdown days, swap doomscrolling for shows or podcasts that feel comforting but intentional.
    


## ✨ Encouragement to Take With You

- If you watched one less episode, that’s a win.  
- If you moved your phone across the room, that’s progress.  
- If you turned off autoplay,  that’s structure.
> You’re not powerless,  you’re learning what works for _you_.
