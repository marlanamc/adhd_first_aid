## 💼 Job Searching

Job hunting with ADHD can be a rollercoaster of **rejection sensitivity**, **executive dysfunction**, and **burnout**. You might get stuck on résumés, freeze on applications, or avoid checking your inbox altogether. It's not that you don't want to work, it's that the process itself is a maze of mental traps.

**🧸 Gentle Advice:**  
Looking for a job with ADHD is a **full-time emotional rollercoaster**. Rejection sensitivity, motivation dips, shame... it's a lot. **You're not lazy, you're overwhelmed**. Try **micro-goals**. **One step a day is movement**. You don't have to apply to 20 jobs today.

**🔥 Stern Advice:**  
**This isn't going to fix itself**. You can't keep saying you want change and then **avoiding the first step**. **Open the doc. Update the resume. Send the thing**. You don't need motivation, you need movement.

### 🧠 Why Job Searching Is Hard with ADHD

- You might:
  - Feel dread every time you think about applying
  - Open a job posting and immediately feel overwhelmed
  - Rewrite your resume 37 times and never send it
  - Struggle with imposter syndrome or Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)
  - Ghost your inbox after one rejection

- Here's what's really going on:
  - 🧩 **Executive dysfunction** makes planning, sequencing, and task initiation difficult
  - ⏰ **Time blindness** causes missed deadlines and unrealistic estimates
  - 🔁 **Procrastination** and shame trap you in avoidance
  - 💥 **Emotional dysregulation** and RSD make feedback feel painful or unbearable
  - 💡 **Decision paralysis** leaves you not knowing where to start or how to keep going

### ⚓ Core Principles for ADHD-Friendly Job Searching

- **Your brain isn't broken**: You're wired differently, and that's okay
- **Progress over perfection**: A partial application is better than none
- **Structure reduces overwhelm**: External systems equal less brain clutter
- **Your strengths matter**: ADHD brains are creative, adaptable, and resilient
- **Rejection ≠ failure**: It's information, not identity

### 🛠️ Strategies for Job Searching

#### 🧠 Self-Compassion & Mindset

- **"I am not lazy, I'm navigating executive dysfunction"**
- **Perfectionism leads to paralysis**: Aim for "good enough and sent"
- **Rejection is hard**: but it doesn't mean you're unqualified
- **Shame spirals aren't productive**: kindness helps you try again

#### 🗂️ Planning & Organization

- **Break down big tasks**: "Apply to job" → Find job → Tailor resume → Write cover letter → Submit
- **Externalize your brain**: Use a digital tracker, paper checklist, or whiteboard to track apps
- **Use a Digital Filing Cabinet**: Save resumes, cover letters, and job descriptions in one folder
- **Optimize your space**: Quiet space, good lighting, noise-canceling headphones. Standing desk or pacing breaks are totally valid

#### ⏰ Time Management

- **Time blocking**: Create "Application Power Blocks," "Networking Hour," or "Follow-Up Friday"
- **Track time**: Use Clockify or write it down to learn your true pacing
- **Pomodoro**: 25-minute bursts + 5-minute breaks equal focus & momentum
- **Visual timers & alarms**: Keep track of transitions or cutoffs
- **Build in buffer time**: Avoid stacking too much in a day, leave space to breathe

#### 🎮 Motivation & Procrastination

- **NICE framework**: Make tasks Novel, Interesting, Challenging, or an Emergency
- **Reward system**: Treat yourself after each step or milestone
- **Find your "why"**: What's driving you to apply? Focus on that motivation
- **Body doubling**: Work on resumes or apps while someone else is present (IRL or virtually)
- **Do it imperfectly**: You can revise later. The first version just needs to exist

#### 💬 Communication & Self-Advocacy

- **Ask for things in writing**: If verbal instructions don't stick
- **Clarify expectations**: "I work best with deadlines and reminders"
- **Script your asks**: "I have trouble tracking verbal instructions, could you send that via email?" or "Thanks for the opportunity. I'm most effective when I prepare in writing"
- **Prepare for feedback**: Ask for written notes instead of verbal critiques
- **Accommodations**: ADHD qualifies for job accommodations in many countries

#### ⚙️ Tech Tools That Help

- **Task managers**: Notion, Trello, Asana, Goblin Tools
- **File organization**: Create a "Job Search" folder with subfolders per company
- **Email templates**: Save drafts for follow-ups, thank-yous, and cover letter skeletons
- **AI tools**: Use ChatGPT to brainstorm strengths, clean up bullet points, or tailor resumes
- **Automation**: Password managers, autofill tools, calendar reminders equal less cognitive effort

### 🧭 Job Searching ≠ Your Worth

- Your job search pace doesn't define your value
- Some people apply to 30 jobs in a day. Others need two weeks to send one
- You are not behind. You are building a path that works with your brain
> You bring real strengths to the table, even if they're hard to quantify on paper.

### ✨ Encouragement to Take With You
- If you saved a job posting, that's progress
- If you sent a rough resume, that's success
- If you opened your inbox today, that's courage
> You're allowed to do this slowly. You're allowed to ask for help. You're not failing, you're finding your way forward.
