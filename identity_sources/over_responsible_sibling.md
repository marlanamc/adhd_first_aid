## Sources for The Overly Responsible Sibling

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD intersects with family roles, shame, boundary issues, and overfunctioning — and offer strategies for self-advocacy, emotional regulation, shared responsibility, and releasing the internalized pressure to carry everything alone.

---

### 🧠 Family Pressure, Shame & Overfunctioning

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Encourages releasing internalized shame and the drive to “handle it all alone.” Emphasizes boundary-setting, resisting perfectionism, and letting go of people-pleasing rooted in cultural and familial expectations.

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
Solden, Sari (2012)  
➤ Discusses how family dynamics and gender roles can lead to over-responsibility. Offers frameworks for adjusting support levels, fostering communication, and restructuring tasks to avoid burnout while still showing up with care.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Explores how shame-based beliefs like “I’m a burden” or “I have to fix this” form in ADHD families. Encourages rejecting judgment and using supportive tools (like body doubling) instead of blame.

### 💬 Boundaries, Shared Responsibility & Communication

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
Orlov, Melissa; Hallowell, Edward M. (2010)  
➤ While about romantic relationships, its lessons apply to sibling roles — especially around shifting out of “parent-child” dynamics. Encourages equitable task-sharing, self-reflection, and working with strengths rather than enabling.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018)  
➤ Normalizes the emotional toll of shouldering invisible responsibilities (like “kinkeeping”). Teaches how to set limits, say no, and externalize tasks without guilt — especially when family dynamics make delegation feel taboo.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Dawson, Peg; Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Promotes collaboration rather than enablement. Encourages understanding executive function differences without excusing harmful patterns. Offers practical frameworks for supporting someone with ADHD _without_ absorbing all the responsibility.

---

### 🌿 Burnout Recovery & Self-Permission

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Centers neurodivergent burnout and the emotional cost of chronic over-responsibility. Offers tools for identifying overwhelm, creating support structures, and asking for help — even small kinds — to lighten the load.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Encourages radical self-acceptance and the release of internal “shoulds.” Helps readers embrace their ADHD planning style, use tools unapologetically, and resist overcompensating for neurotypical standards — or others’ expectations.
