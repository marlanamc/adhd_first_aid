## Sources for the Sick or Chronically Ill

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how chronic illness and ADHD interact — from nervous system overload to burnout and misunderstood symptoms — and offer compassionate, holistic strategies that center pacing, nourishment, self-kindness, and support.

---

### 🧬 ADHD, Health, and the Body

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ Highlights the link between untreated ADHD and physical illness, including IBS, asthma, allergies, fibromyalgia, and hypertension. Emphasizes that treating ADHD holistically — with meds, coaching, self-care, and nervous system regulation — can improve overall health.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Details ADHD’s connections to chronic illness: fatigue, inflammation, immune dysregulation, pain syndromes, and sleep disruption. Offers a comprehensive, body-based strategy including gut health, toxicity reduction, and integrative care for long-term healing.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Addresses the biological underpinnings of ADHD and chronic illness, including hormonal dysregulation, PMDD, vagus nerve tone, and MTHFR gene mutations. Advocates for self-compassion, nervous system regulation, and honoring your body’s limits — especially for women with ADHD.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Explores the overlap between neurodivergence and chronic illness (like Central Sensitivity Syndromes) and offers compassionate tools to prevent and recover from neurodivergent burnout. Encourages pacing, boundaries, and gentle expectations.

### 🩺 Hormones, Fatigue, and Burnout

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
Solden, Sari (2013)  
➤ Highlights how ADHD symptoms intensify with hormonal shifts (PMS, pregnancy, menopause) and how overworking leads to burnout and chronic fatigue. Recommends adjusting life structures to conserve energy and stop the drain on cognitive and physical resources.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Challenges the belief that needing rest makes you a burden. Reinforces that support, time, and care are essential for thriving — not indulgent. Encourages readers to reject shame and recognize their symptoms and struggles as valid.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Describes burnout as inevitable when trying to meet neurotypical standards. Urges women with ADHD to aim for “total imperfection” and prioritize their wellbeing over performance — a vital perspective for anyone managing chronic illness alongside ADHD.

### 🍲 Lifestyle, Nutrition, and Holistic Tools

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia**  
Derbyshire, Emma (2025)  
➤ Focuses on how nutrition impacts brain function and physical health in ADHD. Covers gut health, inflammation, and the role of food in regulating mood, energy, and focus — especially important for ADHDers navigating chronic fatigue or illness.

**Productivity with ADHD: Complete Guide**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Asserts that self-care is non-negotiable for ADHD management. Cites research showing that consistent rest and nutrition can reduce symptoms by 40%. Emphasizes sustainable routines and adaptive systems over rigid perfection — a must for chronic illness.

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2011)  
➤ Promotes exercise, sleep, and omega-3s (like OmegaBrite) for improving brain health and focus. While not illness-specific, it presents a strengths-based, body-inclusive ADHD management approach that’s adaptable to health fluctuations.
