## Sources for Working Multiple Jobs

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD intersects with the demands of juggling multiple roles — from executive function overload and burnout to time management and identity — and offer strategies for energy conservation, boundaries, personalized planning, and sustainable success.

---

### 🔄 Energy Management, Burnout & Work-Life Boundaries

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen (2019)  
➤ Highlights the “recipe for burnout” ADHDers face and encourages dismantling shame and building personalized support. Stresses the importance of identifying tipping points and setting boundaries, especially when managing multiple work commitments.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Emphasizes burnout recovery, sustainable coping, and aligning work with values. Encourages self-compassion and asking for help when managing overload or perfectionism across multiple job roles.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Encourages honoring energy levels, using intuitive strategies, and recognizing entrepreneurial instincts. Promotes self-care and nervous system regulation to support a busy, demanding lifestyle.

**Scattered Minds**  
Maté, Gabor (2019)  
➤ Offers a personal account of managing multiple demanding roles with ADHD, highlighting the internal chaos and deadline-driven patterns that often accompany professional overload.

---

### ⏳ Time Management & Prioritization Strategies

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Offers tools for task breakdown, time-blocking, and digital organization. Encourages adaptive systems to work with energy fluctuations and supports managing multiple job roles with clarity and flexibility.

**How to Manage Your Time with ADHD**  
EndeavorOTC (2023)  
➤ Provides time management techniques like Pomodoro timers, task batching, and visual reminders. Reinforces the need for tech-based tools to structure time and reduce chaos when balancing work demands.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Teaches time blindness awareness and planning systems like the “Workbox.” Promotes a shame-free approach to productivity and encourages personalized, flexible strategies that support complex work lives.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Dawson, Peg; Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Breaks down executive function into trainable skills. Helps ADHDers build time management, planning, and prioritization tools to handle competing job demands.

---

### 🧠 Planning, Structure & Systems That Stick

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail (2025)  
➤ Provides evidence-based strategies for work success, including task initiation, self-regulation, and workplace accommodations. Useful for navigating the shifting demands of multiple job roles.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ A coaching-based guide to creating external structure. Emphasizes systems for scheduling, delegation, and proactive problem-solving — essential for juggling multiple roles.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Advocates for externalizing memory and limiting overcommitment. Offers practical strategies like single-action tasks, visual tools, and calendar systems to maintain balance and clarity.

**Tiny Habits**  
Fogg, BJ (2019)  
➤ Promotes behavior change through small, achievable routines. Helps ADHDers establish consistent micro-habits to reduce decision fatigue across demanding roles.

### 💼 Work Alignment, Entrepreneurship & Self-Advocacy

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Encourages aligning tasks with brain energy, using an external toolbox of supports, and seeking accommodations. Validates ADHD approaches to multitasking, remote work, and career adaptation.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Reframes productivity through the lens of ADHD strengths. Encourages flexible work models and remote/entrepreneurial paths. Promotes cognitive offloading and environmental tailoring.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Discusses ADHD-friendly entrepreneurship and building sustainable income streams. Encourages leveraging strengths, prioritizing rest, and designing a supportive business ecosystem.

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2022)  
➤ Frames ADHD as a strength when matched with the “right kind of difficult.” Encourages finding work that engages curiosity and creativity, which may involve multiple roles or ventures.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Dismantles internalized shame and promotes reframing “quitting” or job-switching. Encourages ADHDers to explore flexible paths and validate their support needs without guilt.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Offers a 7-pillar plan for well-being and personalized success. Encourages ADHDers to map their “signature” traits and build adaptive strategies for work-life balance, especially in complex careers.
