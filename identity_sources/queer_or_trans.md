## Sources for Queer or Trans ADHDers

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources acknowledge the complex intersections between ADHD, gender identity, and queerness — offering validation, identity-affirming insights, and strategies for navigating a world not built for neurodivergent or LGBTQIA+ folks.

---

### 🌈 Intersectional ADHD Experiences

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Highlights how cultural expectations tied to gender roles, queerness, and neurodivergence compound shame and masking. Encourages readers — especially queer and trans individuals — to reject perfectionism, embrace their identity, and design a life that works _with_ their ADHD, not against it.

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Includes real stories from LGBTQIA+ ADHDers, such as navigating a “double coming out” and managing sensory issues related to binding. Explores how queer/trans ADHDers may face compounded stigma and need tailored support. Affirms the importance of identity-first language and inclusive community care.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ A neurodiversity-affirming workbook with compassionate support for those facing marginalization. Includes experiences from a trans Autistic ADHDer, explores microaggressions, and helps readers build self-compassion while resisting neurotypical and cisnormative standards.

---

### 🧠 Identity, Self-Compassion & Community Belonging

**The Neurodiversity Edge**  
Dunne, Maureen (2024)  
➤ Centers identity-first language and affirms that neurodivergence is a core part of who someone is — not a flaw to fix. Advocates for inclusive systems that embrace cognitive and identity diversity, including AuDHDers and queer/trans individuals.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ While centered on ADHD and finances, the book touches on the author’s dream for “neurodivergent pronouns” and visibility — mirroring conversations in queer spaces. Reinforces the importance of identity pride and destigmatizing hidden disabilities.

**Real Self-Care**  
Lakshmin, Pooja (2023)  
➤ Argues that true self-care must address structural oppression — including ableism, transphobia, and toxic capitalism. Especially affirms the exhaustion experienced by queer and trans women, advocating for rest, boundaries, and reclaiming agency.

### 💬 Gender, Sexuality, and ADHD

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ Includes sections on gender, ADHD, and sexuality. References research on how ADHD intersects with societal gender norms and sexual identity, affirming that ADHD shows up in every corner of life — including relationships, intimacy, and self-expression.
