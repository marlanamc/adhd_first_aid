## Sources for The Burned Out Professional

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD contributes to burnout — from energy depletion and perfectionism to shame spirals, masking, and workplace overwhelm — and offer strategies for recovery, sustainability, and building a career that works with your brain, not against it.

---

### 🔥 Understanding ADHD Burnout

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Describes burnout as the result of constant "micro challenges" and nervous system depletion. Recommends honoring your bandwidth, redefining rest, and managing capacity instead of pushing through with shame. Emphasizes foundational care: nutrition, movement, and nervous system regulation.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Defines “neurodivergent burnout” and explains how masking, overstimulation, and executive demands at work contribute to long-term depletion. Offers self-advocacy scripts, simplification strategies, and compassion-based recovery tools.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Normalizes burnout as part of living with ADHD, especially in high-functioning professionals. Encourages readers to reject self-shaming and align their lifestyle with how their brain actually works. Centers rest, capacity planning, and sustainable rhythms.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Tackles internalized beliefs like “I’m lazy” or “I’m not trying hard enough” that drive overwork and exhaustion. Redefines productivity through the ADHD lens and validates asking for help.

### 🧠 Managing Energy, Time & Tasks

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Describes the “constant firefighting” and emotional fatigue of professional life with ADHD. Encourages building support systems and routines that prevent overwhelm. Validates burnout as a nervous system response, not personal failure.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Provides a coaching model for organizing time, tasks, and talents. Helps burned out professionals design systems that conserve energy and offload cognitive load — especially through delegation and external structure.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Frames productivity as energy-based, not effort-based. Offers practical guidance for digital organization, time blindness, and managing hyperfocus. Reinforces that **self-care is non-negotiable** for ADHD success.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Helps professionals externalize systems and simplify routines. Teaches how to say “no,” clarify priorities, and preserve mental energy. A go-to for long-term overwhelm and executive function overload.

### 🧩 Career Misalignment, Advocacy & Recovery

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2023)  
➤ Focuses on workplace accommodations, choosing ADHD-compatible careers, and regaining control over daily life. Includes tools for managing performance anxiety and procrastination — two key drivers of burnout.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen (2019)  
➤ Encourages radical self-acceptance and support without shame. Speaks directly to professionals struggling with perfectionism, overfunctioning, and internalized pressure. Advocates for building power-based systems of support.

**Tiny Experiments**  
Le Cunff, Anne-Laure (2025)  
➤ Critiques toxic productivity and "system tweaking" as a form of avoidance. Promotes intentional imperfection and long-term excellence over perfection. A guide for neurodivergent professionals stuck in burnout loops.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Redefines planning as a high-energy activity that requires support. Encourages realistic systems and letting go of “shoulds” that feed shame and exhaustion. Normalizes alarms, tools, and accommodations as necessary, not optional.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Connects financial stress to professional burnout. Shares how ADHD can derail traditional employment and why some professionals may thrive more in self-employment. Validates redefining your work path to fit your neurotype.
