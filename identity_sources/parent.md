## Sources for The Parent

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD impacts parenting — from executive dysfunction and shame to burnout, boundary struggles, and self-judgment — and offer strategies for emotional regulation, personalized routines, self-compassion, and support systems to help you show up for your kids without losing yourself.

---

### 🧠 Parenting with ADHD: Challenges, Not Character Flaws

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Encourages parents to release perfectionism and cultural pressure. Promotes task evaluation, realistic expectations, and acceptance of help to avoid burnout and build a life that accommodates your ADHD and your family’s needs.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018)  
➤ Offers validation for ADHD parents — especially stay-at-home moms — struggling with clutter, distraction, and unrealistic expectations. Teaches system-building that works with your reality, not against it.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Validates that planning as a parent with ADHD is inherently hard. Urges parents to stop apologizing for using tools, forget the “shoulds,” and build customized systems that actually support your brain and your parenting style.

### 💬 Communication, Family Dynamics, and Shared Responsibility

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
Orlov, Melissa; Hallowell, Edward M. (2010)  
➤ Discusses how untreated ADHD affects family roles. Offers tools for creating healthier dynamics and boundaries, especially when both a parent and child have ADHD. Promotes co-ownership of symptom management in relationships.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Explains how generational shame and judgment shape beliefs like “I’m a bad parent.” Reframes ADHD struggles with compassion and promotes child-focused curiosity over discipline. Encourages ADHD parents to heal their own story to better support their child’s.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Dawson, Peg; Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Offers practical strategies for managing executive dysfunction and reducing overfunctioning by aligning tasks with family members’ strengths. Great for both ADHD parents and those raising ADHD kids.

### 🌿 Burnout Prevention & Self-Permission

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Teaches ADHD parents how to recognize burnout, ask for help, and reject unrealistic ideals. Includes strategies for self-compassion and accommodation so parents can parent without martyring themselves.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Written by an ADHD mom for ADHD moms. Normalizes needing help, forgetting school pickups, and struggling with transitions — then provides flexible, realistic tools to reduce the chaos and shame.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Highlights the importance of prioritizing your needs, even as a parent. Encourages honest conversations with kids about ADHD, building self-acceptance in the whole family. Emphasizes that rest is non-negotiable.

**Brain Brilliance**  
Miller, Lucinda (2024)  
➤ Offers a parent’s perspective on supporting complex kids with ADHD while managing your own neurodivergence. Promotes the “oxygen mask first” model for resilience, including nutrition, sleep, and lifestyle tips.

### 💡 Mindset, Support, and Reframing

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Helps parents stop striving for neurotypical normalcy and start building sustainable, functional lives. Reminds readers ADHD requires tools, support, and grace — not grit alone. Also provides scripts for family members (“Hearts”) to help support ADHD loved ones.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail (2025)  
➤ Speaks directly to ADHD parents struggling to keep up, organize, or manage family life. Offers daily tools for getting unstuck and simplifying big parenting tasks into manageable, low-overwhelm steps.

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2020)  
➤ Offers practical and optimistic advice for ADHD parents, including strategies like "being the bumper" for ADHD kids. Frames ADHD as a different wiring, not a deficit, encouraging a mindset of growth and self-trust.

**Scattered Minds**  
Maté, Gabor (2019)  
➤ Explores how ADHD develops in family systems. Encourages deep self-reflection and healing from intergenerational patterns, helping ADHD parents better understand both their children and themselves.
