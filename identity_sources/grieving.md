## Sources for The Grieving or Emotionally Raw Individual

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources speak to the experience of grief with ADHD — whether it's the loss of a loved one, the mourning of an undiagnosed life, or the emotional weight of unmet potential — and offer tools for emotional processing, radical self-acceptance, and rebuilding identity from a place of compassion.

---

### 💔 Grief, Diagnosis, & Emotional Release

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Defines “GROY” as the equal parts grief and joy that often follow an ADHD diagnosis. Validates the pain of lost time, failed systems, and harsh self-perception. Offers pathways for rebuilding identity and seeking joy without minimizing the loss.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Acknowledges the "weird grief" of discovering ADHD later in life and mourning what could’ve been. Encourages moving forward by embracing one’s strengths and designing a future based on authenticity and neurodivergent needs.

**You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ Encourages readers to reframe the past, forgive themselves, and stop carrying shame for struggles that weren’t their fault. Recommends self-education and compassionate system-building as part of the healing process.

---

### 🧠 Healing Through Self-Acceptance

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Explores grief as part of the ADHD journey — not just over diagnosis, but over lost time, missed dreams, or masking. Promotes shedding shame and creating personalized support systems rooted in authenticity, not performance.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Offers tools for radical acceptance of ADHD and navigating shame spirals. Encourages readers to design lives that center emotional truth and build in rest, softness, and recovery time as part of long-term self-trust.
