## Sources for The Job Seeker

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD intersects with job searching — from executive dysfunction and rejection sensitivity to career misalignment and burnout — and offer strategies for self-advocacy, sustainable planning, and building a career that works with your brain.

---

### 💼 Self-Understanding, Shame & Career Clarity

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Unpacks internalized shame like “I’m lazy” or “I’ll never succeed,” and reframes productivity around ADHD energy patterns. Encourages job seekers to unlearn toxic beliefs and advocate for strengths like creativity, empathy, and hyperfocus.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Helps job seekers understand perfectionism and overthinking as energy drains. Recommends using automations, curiosity, and structure to support clarity and forward momentum in the job hunt.

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Emphasizes building a strategy “toolbox” and practicing self-advocacy. Normalizes non-linear paths and encourages job seekers to find roles where problem-solving, curiosity, and neurodivergence are strengths — not obstacles.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Encourages letting go of masking and self-silencing in professional spaces. Offers a path toward career fulfillment through identity integration and bold self-definition.

---

### 🧠 Systems, Strategies & Sustainable Action

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Validates using alarms, templates, and other ADHD-friendly tools without shame. Encourages building a flexible job search system that matches energy cycles and avoids neurotypical comparison traps.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Reframes “organization” as external support for a chaotic mind. Offers concrete strategies for saying no, avoiding burnout, and creating application systems that work _for_ ADHD brains — not against them.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Emphasizes that ADHD is a disability and self-care is survival. Encourages exploring flexible roles or self-employment that match your problem-solving skills, curiosity, and need for autonomy.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Focuses on assertive communication and self-advocacy in the workplace. Encourages job seekers to find roles where their strengths are honored — not just tolerated — and to release the pressure to perform neurotypically.

---

### 💡 Matching ADHD Profiles to Career Paths

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2025)  
➤ Offers evidence-based strategies for aligning jobs with ADHD traits. Recommends careers with novelty, flexibility, and creativity — and provides guidance on disclosure and accommodations.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Encourages understanding your “ADHD signature” to find meaningful work. Ideal for high achievers who masked symptoms for years and now feel misaligned or burnt out in their career path.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Reframes ADHD as a professional advantage when matched with the right roles. Offers exercises to align creativity and hyperfocus with opportunity, while reinforcing that self-care is essential to sustainable success.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Encourages looking for neurodivergent-friendly employers and support systems. Validates that paperwork, admin tasks, and job apps are genuine barriers — and offers alternatives like entrepreneurship or niche work that supports ADHD rhythms.

---

### 📣 Reframing, Resilience & Advocacy

**You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ A foundational reframe of ADHD. Encourages job seekers to forgive past struggles and move forward with self-awareness. Highlights lived experience as a professional strength and supports medication as a tool.

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
Orlov, Melissa; Hallowell, Edward (2010)  
➤ Though focused on relationships, the book’s “try differently, not harder” mindset applies to job seekers. Reinforces the value of ADHD-informed professionals, coaching, and structure to support big life transitions like job changes.
