## Sources for The Neurodivergent Adult

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources highlight the lived experience of neurodivergent adults — including those with ADHD, autism, dyslexia, and more — and offer strategies for embracing identity, managing executive function, finding community, and building a life that works _with_ your brain.

---

### 🧠 Identity, Reframing & Self-Acceptance

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Helps untangle shame, perfectionism, and overfunctioning. Promotes self-acceptance as a foundation for living authentically, especially in a world that demands conformity.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Identifies toxic beliefs neurodivergent adults internalize — like “I’m lazy” or “I’m broken.” Offers reframes rooted in compassion and advocacy, encouraging readers to unmask and reimagine their strengths.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Reframes ADHD as a difference, not a defect. Guides readers through self-compassion, structure, and redefining success. Encourages finding neurodivergent-affirming professionals, especially around finances and life planning.

**You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ A foundational ADHD resource that validates lived experience, encourages forgiveness, and empowers readers with tools, support networks, and realistic goal-setting.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Promotes radical self-compassion, burnout recovery, and self-advocacy. Offers practical exercises for executive functioning, emotional regulation, and navigating societal pressure to mask.

### 🔧 Daily Life, Structure & Planning

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Provides a flexible framework for ADHD-friendly planning, prioritizing, and self-management. Encourages readers to live “unapologetically” and build systems that reflect their actual needs.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Coaching-based strategies for managing time, tasks, and talents. Helps neurodivergent adults create external scaffolding and course-correct when overwhelmed.

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ A toolbox of strategies grounded in neuroscience, self-compassion, and community. Reinforces that it’s okay to do things differently — and that functionality isn’t one-size-fits-all.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ A real-life guide to getting organized with ADHD. Encourages accepting reality, using external systems, and building flexible routines that allow for disruption and re-entry.

**How to Keep House While Drowning**  
Davis, KC (2022)  
➤ Promotes “care tasks” over “chores,” focusing on rhythm over rigidity. A kind, trauma-informed approach to home management for neurodivergent brains.

### ⚙️ Tools for Productivity, Health & Success

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Offers personalized systems for time management, creativity, and sustained momentum. Emphasizes self-care and dynamic thinking as cornerstones of success.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2023)  
➤ Evidence-based strategies to manage emotions, build routines, and align careers with your neurodivergent strengths. Includes tips for finding low-cost care.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD or Other Disorders**  
(Anonymous Guide)  
➤ Practical, real-life strategies for managing daily life, relationships, routines, and mindset — including hygiene, planning, tech use, and habit formation.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Dawson, Peg; Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Breaks down executive skills and provides a diagnostic-style quiz to assess strengths and weaknesses. Includes targeted strategies for growth in key areas.

**The Anti-Planner**  
Donovan, Dani (2022)  
➤ A creative, shame-free workbook designed for those who reject traditional planning. Ideal for visual thinkers and those needing flexible productivity tools.

### 🌱 Wellness, Support & Community

**Real Self-Care**  
Lakshmin, Pooja (2023)  
➤ Offers a systemic, values-based redefinition of care. Includes practical steps for accessing affordable therapy and navigating healthcare barriers.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Demystifies financial management through the lens of neurodivergence. Encourages self-advocacy, debt recovery, entrepreneurship, and rebuilding trust with money.

**Brain Brilliance**  
Miller, Lucinda (2024)  
➤ Naturopathic nutrition strategies for supporting neurodivergent brains. Offers brain-friendly recipes and tips for energy, focus, and mood regulation.

**Driven to Distraction**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2011)  
➤ A foundational ADHD text that introduced the “inside-out” experience of ADHD. Validates emotion, identity, and possibility in the face of lifelong struggle.

**Taking Charge of Adult ADHD**  
Barkley, Russell A. (2010)  
➤ Grounded in clinical expertise, this guide offers detailed techniques to improve daily living. Focuses on executive function, impulse control, and goal execution.

**Scattered Minds**  
Maté, Gabor (2019)  
➤ Connects ADHD to trauma and environmental stress. Encourages compassionate insight, nervous system regulation, and healing the inner child.

**The Neurodiversity Edge**  
Dunne, Maureen (2024)  
➤ Explains the organizational benefits of neuroinclusion. Highlights unique strengths — like lateral thinking, creativity, and resilience — as workplace assets.

**The Body Keeps the Score**  
van der Kolk, Bessel A. (2015)  
➤ Explores the lasting impact of trauma on mind and body. Offers insight into emotional regulation, self-awareness, and body-based healing — relevant for many neurodivergent adults.

**Tiny Experiments**  
Le Cunff, Anne-Laure (2025)  
➤ Encourages long-term excellence over short-term optimization. Ideal for neurodivergent thinkers who need to prioritize sustainable, imperfect progress.

**Tiny Habits**  
Fogg, BJ (2019)  
➤ Offers a habit-building model that reduces activation energy and encourages momentum — especially effective for brains that struggle with consistency or overwhelm.
