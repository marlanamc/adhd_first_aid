## Sources for Recovering Perfectionists

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources examine how ADHD and perfectionism intertwine — often creating cycles of shame, avoidance, and burnout — and offer practical, compassionate strategies to break free from unrealistic expectations and embrace progress over perfection.

---

### 💡 Understanding Perfectionism with ADHD

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Encourages readers to “aim for total imperfection,” rejecting internalized pressure to conform to neurotypical standards. Emphasizes that ADHD strategies should make life easier — not serve as tools to “fix” brokenness — and warns that comparison is “an act of violence against the self.”

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Validates how perfectionism often shows up as self-criticism or shame, like “Why did you do it that way? You know better.” Shifts the goal from flawlessness to functionality — building personalized toolkits that support, not punish, the ADHD brain.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Reframes “failures” as data, not personal flaws. Promotes recovery from perfectionism through course correction, not punishment — encouraging readers to get back on track without shame and build systems that serve their real lives.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2025)  
➤ Identifies perfectionism as the most common cognitive distortion in adults with ADHD. Offers tools for goal-setting and time limits, advising readers to abandon “rigid standards” and embrace the motto: “It’s progress — not perfection.”

### 🔄 Progress Over Paralysis

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Confronts “the failed perfectionist” archetype and shows how shame leads to overcompensation and inaction. Encourages readers to practice imperfect action and stay curious about what’s possible — even if it’s messy or nontraditional.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Frames perfectionism as a mask for rejection sensitivity. Introduces “radical acceptance” as the antidote, affirming that “Perfect doesn’t exist.” Helps ADHDers build life systems that support their real needs and celebrate “done” over “ideal.”

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Identifies “if it’s not the best, it’s the worst” thinking as an ADHD lie. Counters this with the empowering truth that “done is better than perfect,” encouraging radical self-acceptance and progress-focused action.

### 🧠 Self-Compassion & Systems That Work

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Reminds readers that “speed bumps are not roadblocks.” Reinforces that ADHD is not a one-size-fits-all journey and that self-compassion is essential for navigating setbacks without falling into perfectionist spirals.

**Productivity with ADHD: Complete Guide**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Affirms that “perfection is not the goal.” Focuses on sustainable progress and system-building that prioritizes function over appearance — tools that actually work with your brain, not against it.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Challenges unrealistic expectations and internalized shame. Helps readers embrace “spiky profiles” and prioritize strategies that increase completion rates — even if they’re not perfect or consistent every time.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ Connects perfectionism to the ADHD shame spiral. Offers a three-step recovery process: reframe, forgive, and move forward — shifting from self-punishment to compassionate self-understanding.
