## Sources for The Recently Diagnosed

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources are tailored to individuals who’ve just discovered they have ADHD — offering validation, emotional support, and actionable strategies to help you process your diagnosis, understand your brain, and build a life that works with your neurodivergent wiring.

---

### 🧠 Understanding Your Brain & Diagnosis

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2021)  
➤ Frames ADHD as a powerful engine that needs the right tools and understanding. Explains the neurological foundations of ADHD, offers behavioral strategies, and encourages embracing your unique cognitive style.

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Offers an ADHD-friendly "user’s guide" for newly diagnosed adults. Combines science, validation, and community support to help readers build a personalized strategy toolbox and move forward with self-awareness and pride.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Written for those just diagnosed. Combines ADHD-friendly storytelling and jump-around structure with strategies, reframes, and emotional validation. Encourages curiosity and compassion in exploring life post-diagnosis.

**Scattered Minds**  
Maté, Gabor (2019)  
➤ Explores how trauma, childhood stress, and environment shape ADHD. Encourages a compassionate, holistic understanding of ADHD beyond genetics or medical labels.

**Driven to Distraction**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2011)  
➤ A foundational ADHD text that introduced the lived experience of ADHD to the public. Validates symptoms and offers therapeutic pathways for treatment, growth, and understanding.

### 💬 Reframing, Self-Acceptance & Emotional Healing

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Dispels internalized beliefs like “I’m lazy” or “I’m stupid.” Encourages reframing ADHD as a difference, not a defect — and supports the emotional mix of grief and joy often felt after a diagnosis.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen (2019)  
➤ Guides readers through untangling shame, perfectionism, and cultural expectations. Offers emotional and identity-centered support to help you stop hiding and start living fully as your authentic self.

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
Solden, Sari (2013)  
➤ A pioneering book that explores the unique challenges women face with ADHD, especially in the early stages of understanding the diagnosis. Focuses on redefining competence, value, and visibility.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ A classic self-help book that helps reframe past experiences and forgive yourself. Encourages a fresh start rooted in understanding, support, and practical ADHD strategies.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Offers structured exercises for building self-compassion, reducing burnout, and understanding your neurotype. Perfect for grounding yourself post-diagnosis in clarity and confidence.

### 🛠 Systems, Structure & Daily Support

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Provides an organizing blueprint for adults with ADHD, with a compassionate tone and flexible structure. Helps newly diagnosed readers shift from shame to self-trust by building systems that _actually_ work for their brain.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Helps newly diagnosed adults create routines that honor creativity, hyperfocus, and energy variability. Offers mindset shifts and practical tools to build momentum without burnout.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Coaching-based strategies for managing time, tasks, and talents. Encourages personalized scaffolding and continuous adjustment — ideal for those starting fresh after a diagnosis.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2025)  
➤ Breaks down ADHD into digestible parts and provides daily strategies to navigate work, relationships, emotions, and organization. Supports understanding your strengths and using them intentionally.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Dawson, Peg; Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Includes a quiz to identify executive function strengths and struggles. Offers skill-building techniques for improving time management, self-regulation, and goal execution.

### 🌱 Lifestyle, Relationships & Self-Care

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ A lifestyle guide written by and for women with ADHD. Focuses on self-compassion, body awareness, nervous system support, and rejecting one-size-fits-all wellness advice.

**Brain Brilliance**  
Miller, Lucinda (2024)  
➤ A nutrition and lifestyle guide for neurodivergent brains. Offers practical recipes and health strategies for improving energy, focus, and mood — especially relevant in early healing after diagnosis.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Addresses the financial fallout of undiagnosed ADHD. Offers support for managing debt, understanding neurodivergent spending patterns, and building financial resilience post-diagnosis.

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
Orlov, Melissa; Hallowell, Edward (2010)  
➤ Explores the impact of ADHD on relationships — especially when diagnosis arrives in adulthood. Encourages both partners to approach challenges with curiosity, not blame, and shift from "trying harder" to "trying differently."
