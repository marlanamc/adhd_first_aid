## Sources for the Student

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD impacts academic life — from studying, reading, and writing to managing time, memory, and motivation — and offer strategies for building personalized, sustainable systems that support learning and success.

---

### 🧠 Studying, Note-Taking & Academic Tools

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail (2025)  
➤ Offers practical, evidence-based strategies for studying, reading, writing papers, and taking exams. Helps students develop realistic goals, manage time, and regulate frustration when things go off track.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Dawson, Peg; Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Breaks down executive skills like planning, time management, and self-control into teachable strategies. Helps students set goals and take step-by-step actions toward them.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Provides coaching tools to help students build personalized structures. Emphasizes the importance of accommodations and external supports for managing tasks and expectations.

**Taking Charge of Adult ADHD**  
Barkley, Russell A. (2010)  
➤ A comprehensive guide to managing ADHD symptoms across academic, professional, and personal domains. Translates research into clear, actionable recommendations for student success.

### 🧰 Planning, Productivity & Support Systems

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ A user’s guide full of ADHD-specific strategies. Validates different learning styles, supports tech integration, and encourages community connection. Helps students build consistent, personalized systems.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Focuses on radical acceptance, time blindness, and resilient planning systems like the “Workbox.” Encourages flexible structures that fit the ADHD brain, not neurotypical norms.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Offers simple, proven tools for managing time, tasks, and distractions. Encourages students to harness creativity, hyperfocus, and dynamic thinking to build sustainable productivity systems.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Presents ADHD insights in a modular, ADHD-friendly format. Encourages students to embrace curiosity, use templates, and avoid perfectionism in order to take action and protect cognitive space.

---

### 🧠 Reframing ADHD & Building Self-Worth

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ Helps students release shame and reframe their past experiences. Teaches that ADHD is an explanation, not an excuse, and encourages moving forward with tools and understanding.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Confronts the core beliefs that block progress (“I’m lazy,” “I’m stupid”). Reframes hard work, emphasizes strengths, and reinforces the truth that ADHD requires support — not self-blame.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Offers tools for self-compassion, emotional regulation, and assertive communication. Helps students manage burnout and advocate for accommodations in school environments.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen (2019)  
➤ Empowers female students to stop masking and embrace neurodiversity. Encourages self-worth, customized strategies, and dismantling harmful societal expectations.

---

### 📚 ADHD, Organization, and School Systems

**ADD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life**  
Kolberg, Judith; Nadeau, Kathleen (2002)  
➤ Offers practical systems specifically tailored for ADHD brains. Helps students externalize memory, manage paperwork, and create physical and digital order.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
Kustow, James (2024)  
➤ Maps a 7-pillar plan for managing ADHD, including optimizing study habits, choosing the right environment, and understanding the impact of nutrition, stress, and tech overload on focus.

**Tiny Experiments**  
Le Cunff, Anne-Laure (2025)  
➤ Warns against over-optimization and encourages “intentional imperfection.” Helps students avoid burnout and use micro-actions to build sustainable routines that work long-term.
