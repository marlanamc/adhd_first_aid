## Sources for Parent of a Child with ADHD

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources offer compassionate insight into parenting kids with ADHD — from school and behavior challenges to executive functioning, self-esteem, and family dynamics. They provide science-backed tools, lived experiences, and hopeful strategies for building a connected, resilient, ADHD-friendly home.

---

### 👶 Understanding Your Child's Brain

**Taking Charge of ADHD**  
Barkley, Russell A. (2000, 2020)  
➤ A foundational manual for parents, offering science-backed education, behavior strategies, and a clear roadmap to support children with ADHD effectively.

**Superparenting for ADD**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Jensen, Peter (2008)  
➤ Focuses on finding and nurturing the unique strengths of kids with ADHD, helping parents shift from a deficit model to one of empowerment and connection.

**8 Keys to Parenting Children with ADHD**  
Goldrich, Cindy (2015)  
➤ A skill-based, relationship-first approach rooted in emotional regulation, communication, and proactive discipline.

**A Bird’s-Eye View of Life with ADD and ADHD**  
Zeigler Dendy, Chris A.; Zeigler, Alex (2003)  
➤ Shares insight directly from teens and young adults with ADHD, giving parents a first-person look at their kids’ emotional world.

**Scattered Minds**  
Maté, Gabor (2019)  
➤ Explores the emotional and developmental roots of ADHD and challenges assumptions about parenting. Offers hope, empathy, and long-term healing strategies.

---

### 📚 School, Learning, and Executive Function Support

**Bright Kids Who Can’t Keep Up**  
Braaten, Ellen; Willoughby, Brian (2014)  
➤ Explains how processing speed impacts learning, homework, and emotional resilience. Offers tools to help kids thrive without shame.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Dawson, Peg; Guare, Richard (2015)  
➤ Provides frameworks for building executive function skills—like planning, time management, and task initiation—in both kids and parents. Based on the authors’ popular books for parents of ADHD children and teens.

**Organizational Skills Training for Children with ADHD**  
Gallagher, R.; Abikoff, H. B.; Spira, E. G. (2014)  
➤ A structured, empirically supported approach to building routines, checklists, and systems for children struggling with organization.

**How to Reach and Teach Children with ADD/ADHD**  
Rief, Sandra F. (2005)  
➤ A practical handbook for educators and parents, filled with strategies for academic success and classroom behavior support.

**Making the System Work for Your Child with ADHD**  
Jensen, Peter S. (2004)  
➤ Teaches parents how to advocate in schools, therapy, and medical settings. Helps navigate systems that can feel overwhelming or dismissive.

---

### 🧠 Parent Self-Care & Understanding Your Own ADHD

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn L. (2018)  
➤ Written by a mother with ADHD, this book validates the invisible labor of neurodivergent parenting. Encourages letting go of perfection and doing what works for your specific family.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ A heartfelt reflection from a parent who discovered her own ADHD through her child. Offers strategies for co-regulation, transitions, and self-compassion.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Normalizes the chaos of neurodivergent parenting and advocates for rest, honesty, and finding humor. Includes conversations with kids about ADHD and what it means to be “different and okay.”

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
Orlov, Melissa (2010)  
➤ Highlights how parenting pressures can intensify ADHD symptoms in adult relationships. Encourages parents to care for their relationship while raising kids with support and clarity.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Helps parents identify and prevent burnout, manage guilt, and regulate their own nervous system while parenting kids with high needs.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Co-written by a parent of an ADHD child, this book offers tools to support yourself without shame. Promotes radical acceptance, values-based living, and family systems designed for real brains, not ideal ones.

### 💛 Emotional Regulation & Connection

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Teaches parents to ditch judgmental language and nurture their child's self-image with curiosity and strength-based praise. Challenges myths like “lazy” and “manipulative.”

**Bloom**  
Kenney, Lynne; Young, Wendy (2015)  
➤ Offers practical, brain-based strategies to help parents respond to anxious, angry, or overwhelmed kids with emotional intelligence and grounded calm.

**Raising Resilient Children**  
Brooks, Robert; Goldstein, Sam (2002)  
➤ Promotes optimism, problem-solving, and inner strength in children. Encourages consistent parenting that builds self-efficacy and resilience.

**Mind in the Making**  
Galinsky, Ellen (2010)  
➤ Breaks down the seven essential life skills every child needs, including focus, self-control, and perspective-taking—skills that ADHD kids often struggle with and benefit from explicit support.

---

### 🧃 Nutrition, Environment, and Daily Life

**Brain Brilliance**  
Miller, Lucinda (2024)  
➤ Offers ADHD-friendly recipes and nutritional guidance from a neurodivergent parent. Encourages feeding both brain and body while prioritizing parental self-care.

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia**  
Derbyshire, Emma (2025)  
➤ Translates scientific research into practical dietary changes that support attention, memory, and mood in children with ADHD and dyslexia.

**Tiny Habits**  
Fogg, BJ (2019)  
➤ Provides a simple method to make family routines easier and more consistent—one tiny step at a time. Great for parents trying to implement new strategies without overwhelm.
