## Sources for The Entrepreneur

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources examine how ADHD fuels creativity, innovation, and risk-taking — while also challenging consistency, structure, and self-trust. They offer frameworks for building a business that works _with_ your unique brain wiring and honors your strengths, energy cycles, and need for autonomy.

---

### 🚀 Creative Drive & Entrepreneurial Strengths

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2022)  
➤ Frames ADHD as a “Ferrari engine” of potential. Encourages leveraging creative energy and rapid thinking for entrepreneurial success — especially in roles that allow freedom, innovation, and rapid ideation.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Describes ADHD as rocket fuel for entrepreneurial thinking. Celebrates ADHDers as natural coaches, creatives, and business-builders, and encourages holistic support systems to sustain that output.

**The Neurodiversity Edge**  
Dunne, Maureen (2024)  
➤ Highlights research on divergent thinking, innovation, and problem-solving in neurodivergent individuals. Encourages entrepreneurs to embrace "unconstrained creative generation" and use neurodiversity as a business advantage.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Spotlights successful ADHD entrepreneurs and emphasizes energy-based productivity, not rigid systems. Encourages structured brainstorming, hyperfocus windows, and embracing creativity as a leadership strength.

---

### 🧠 Building a Business That Works With Your Brain

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Offers flexible, research-backed tools for managing ADHD in real life. Ideal for entrepreneurs designing systems from scratch. Encourages leaning into your strengths like hyperfocus and redefining success on your terms.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Written for trailblazers, creatives, and those rejecting neurotypical molds. Encourages designing your business and life _around_ your ADHD — not in spite of it — using self-acceptance and personalized strategies.

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
Solden, Sari (2013)  
➤ Reframes disorganization as a creative advantage. Advises partnering with “farmers” (detail-focused people), using structure as scaffolding, and embracing your natural role as a disruptor, innovator, or thought leader.

**You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ A foundational ADHD reframe. Encourages entrepreneurs to design lives that align with their energy, thinking style, and idea flow. Validates self-employment as a great fit for ADHDers when structured support is in place.

### 💡 Reframing Work, Rest, and Identity

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Advocates for building a life and business from a place of power, not shame. Encourages personalizing strategies, seeking external help, and designing success systems that reflect your strengths — not neurotypical standards.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Dispels myths like “You’re lazy” or “You quit too soon.” Affirms that creativity, big ideas, and vision are valid paths to fulfillment. Suggests outsourcing struggles and doubling down on strengths for sustainable success.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Encourages ADHDers to stop forcing themselves into traditional systems. Offers coaching tools to help entrepreneurs design their own paths — with external structure, self-awareness, and strategic delegation.
