## Sources for The Caretaker

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources address how ADHD intersects with caretaking — whether parenting, supporting a loved one, or managing a household — and offer strategies for boundary-setting, sustainable systems, emotional regulation, and compassionate self-advocacy.

---

### 🧠 Managing ADHD While Supporting Others

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen (2019)  
➤ Encourages caretakers to reject shame and embrace their own ADHD needs without guilt. Emphasizes that you can't do ADHD alone — support, boundaries, and personalized systems are essential for avoiding burnout.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Dee, Lisa (2025)  
➤ Written for ADHD women often in caretaking roles. Offers wellness-based support through lifestyle design, nervous system regulation, and self-compassion. Helps reframe exhaustion and self-neglect as signs that your systems need adjusting — not personal failure.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Explores the specific stressors and burnout caretakers with ADHD face. Offers tools for emotional regulation, executive functioning, and self-advocacy, including when and how to ask for help in parenting, partnership, or household roles.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Normalizes overwhelm for ADHD caretakers and reframes rest, transitions, and authenticity as core strategies for managing both caregiving and personal needs. Validates the importance of structuring life around your energy.

---

### 💡 Emotional Labor, Boundaries & Self-Care

**Real Self-Care**  
Lakshmin, Pooja (2023)  
➤ Offers a therapeutic model for redefining self-care. Encourages caretakers to create internal clarity, say no without guilt, and make values-based decisions to protect time, energy, and identity.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Tackles the inner critic many caretakers struggle with — beliefs like “I’m lazy” or “I’m a burden.” Offers validation and tools for self-compassion, and guidance for supporters learning how to help without judgment.

**You Mean I'm Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
Kelly, Kate; Ramundo, Peggy (2006)  
➤ A foundational ADHD text that speaks to caretakers managing children or adults with ADHD. Offers reframes, forgiveness, and practical steps for moving forward with self-understanding and community support.

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
Solden, Sari (2013)  
➤ Addresses the invisible labor and exhaustion experienced by women with ADHD in caregiving roles. Advocates for restructuring your life, delegating household duties, and designing distraction-free environments.

### 🧩 Tools for Home, Family, & Parenting

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Offers a wide range of practical tools, including a dedicated section for partners and parents. Encourages caretakers to understand misbehavior as communication and ADHD as a chronic condition needing layered support.

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
Orlov, Melissa (2010)  
➤ Explores relationship dynamics when one partner has ADHD. Offers guidance for navigating the parent-child dynamic, improving communication, and developing ADHD-sensitive routines and responsibilities.

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell, Edward M.; Ratey, John J. (2021)  
➤ Provides frameworks for family systems, emphasizing the importance of structure, routines, and early diagnosis. Encourages caretakers to be compassionate guides — not enforcers — for both themselves and their children.

**Brain Brilliance**  
Miller, Lucinda (2024)  
➤ Nutrition-focused guide written by a mother of neurodivergent kids. Offers practical strategies and recipes to support brain health in ADHD families, addressing the daily chaos and emotional load of caretaking.

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Written by an ADHD mom and business owner. Offers whole-life strategies that include ADHD parenting, managing family dynamics, and supporting neurotypical partners. Encourages playful self-care and ADHD-aware communication.

---

### 🛠 Home Systems & Organization

**How to Keep House While Drowning**  
Davis, KC (2022)  
➤ Reframes household tasks as care tasks and advocates for flexible rhythms instead of rigid routines. A powerful mindset shift for ADHD caretakers managing household overwhelm.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Offers realistic organization tools for ADHD households. Emphasizes externalizing tasks, containing clutter, and saying no to preserve energy and mental clarity in a caretaking role.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Provides coaching-based strategies to manage time, space, and relationships. Includes sections on living with and loving someone with ADHD, relevant for caretakers balancing personal needs and others’ expectations.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
DOMI, Ju (2025)  
➤ Offers practical planning tools built for ADHD brains. Reinforces that systems need to reduce—not increase—mental effort. Self-care is framed as essential maintenance, not indulgence.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2025)  
➤ Shares goal-setting and time management techniques for ADHD adults navigating caregiving and family responsibilities. Promotes flexibility over perfection and building momentum through progress, not pressure.

**Scattered Minds**  
Maté, Gabor (2019)  
➤ Offers a compassionate view of ADHD in families. Encourages empathy for both children and parents with ADHD, and promotes holistic, trauma-aware strategies for managing stress and building resilience.
