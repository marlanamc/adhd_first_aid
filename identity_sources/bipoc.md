## Sources for BIPOC with ADHD

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources examine how ADHD intersects with racial identity, cultural stigma, misdiagnosis, and systemic barriers — and offer strategies for healing, advocacy, and living authentically as a neurodivergent person of color.

---

### 🧠 Intersectionality, Identity & Diagnosis

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle; Littman, Ellen (2019)  
➤ Features a composite character who is a woman of color with ADHD. Emphasizes how cultural and familial dynamics can reinforce shame. Encourages living authentically and designing support systems from a place of empowerment, not pathology.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Addresses racial disparities in ADHD diagnosis. Encourages BIPOC individuals to unlearn toxic self-beliefs and advocate for their needs. Emphasizes that neurodivergent struggles are real and valid, despite systemic invalidation.

**Scattered Minds**  
Maté, Gabor (2019)  
➤ Reframes ADHD as a condition shaped by both biology and environment. Urges readers to consider how systemic and cultural pressures contribute to ADHD symptoms, especially in marginalized communities.

**Order from Chaos**  
Paul, Jaclyn (2018)  
➤ Notes inequalities in ADHD diagnosis and treatment. Encourages embracing ADHD as it shows up in your real life, and developing strategies that work despite systemic barriers and cultural stigma.

---

### 🧩 Culture, Stigma & Systemic Pressure

**Lazy, Crazy, and Disgusting**  
Brewis, Alexandra; Wutich, Amber (2019)  
➤ Explores stigma around health and neurodivergence. Challenges harmful labels and highlights how racism and ableism intersect in the experience of being BIPOC with ADHD. Promotes a neurodiversity-affirming mindset.

**Real Self-Care**  
Lakshmin, Pooja (2023)  
➤ Written by a psychiatrist focused on marginalized communities. Frames true self-care as a tool of resistance and power. Encourages BIPOC individuals to set boundaries and reject self-sacrifice as a survival strategy.

**The Neurodiversity Edge**  
Dunne, Maureen (2024)  
➤ Discusses disparities in education, employment, incarceration, and mental health among neurodivergent people. Encourages building systems of psychological safety and community support that acknowledge intersectional oppression.

---

### 💡 Empowerment & Neurodivergent Advocacy

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Addresses coexisting identities and systemic stigma. Emphasizes building inclusive, validating communities and supports. Urges neurodivergent individuals to find personalized strategies and reject one-size-fits-all expectations.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Kinzer, Nikki; Wright, Pete D. (2024)  
➤ Promotes radical acceptance and personalized planning. Particularly relevant for BIPOC individuals who face extra layers of stigma and pressure to conform. Encourages rejecting perfectionism and embracing one’s strengths.
