## Sources for The Low-Income Individual

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources examine how ADHD intersects with financial hardship — from impulsive spending and job instability to the ADHD tax and lack of access to care — and offer strategies for managing money, accessing affordable support, and releasing shame around financial struggle.

---

### 💸 Financial Struggles & the ADHD Tax

**Mad About Money**  
Alexander-Grout, Maddy (2025)  
➤ Centers lived experience with debt and ADHD. Explains the ADHD tax and how executive dysfunction leads to extra costs. Offers encouragement, shame-free financial guidance, and business-building strategies even without a formal diagnosis.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies and How to Stop Believing Them**  
Pink, Richard; Pink, Roxanne (2024)  
➤ Highlights the hidden financial toll of ADHD and the shame that keeps people from seeking help. Normalizes self-diagnosis and promotes accessible education for those facing care disparities or long waitlists.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Ratey, Nancy A. (2008)  
➤ Connects executive dysfunction with poor financial outcomes like late fees and bad credit. Encourages strategic delegation, using employment supports like JAN, and understanding financial overwhelm as a symptom — not a moral failure.

### 🧠 Budgeting, Career Stability & Accessible Support

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
Solden, Sari; Frank, Michelle (2019)  
➤ Offers realistic options for finding affordable help. Recommends sliding-scale services, community mental health clinics, and learning evidence-based strategies when cost is a barrier to traditional care.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Levrini, Abigail L. (2023)  
➤ Addresses how ADHD symptoms can harm work performance and financial stability. Recommends university clinics, job coaching, and self-advocacy to reduce cost and gain access to ADHD-informed care.

**How to ADHD**  
McCabe, Jessica (2024)  
➤ Acknowledges how wealth buffers ADHD symptoms and highlights inequality in care access. Offers free and low-cost resources, community guidance, and validation for those navigating ADHD without financial privilege.

**Real Self-Care**  
Lakshmin, Pooja (2023)  
➤ Encourages redefining wellness to include accessible care. Provides concrete strategies for finding affordable therapy, including sliding-scale options and university-based providers.

### 💡 Reframing, Self-Compassion & Sustainable Strategy

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Kemp, Jennifer; Mitchelson, Monique (2024)  
➤ Connects ADHD with systemic risk factors like underemployment and incarceration. Encourages workplace self-advocacy and practical accommodations to improve job retention and income stability.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Carder, Meredith (2024)  
➤ Reframes financial struggles through a neurodivergent lens. Emphasizes the importance of working with ADHD-informed financial professionals and releasing shame around past money missteps.
