# Time & Transition Loop Examples

## 🕰️ Waiting Mode
### ⏰ The Time Trap: How This Works

**The Setup:** You have something scheduled later (appointment, meeting, social event)

**The ADHD Response:** Your brain can't hold "now" and "later" simultaneously
- **Time blindness** makes 2 hours feel infinite or instant
- **Executive dysfunction** can't plan around the appointment
- **Working memory** drops current tasks to "hold space" for later

**The Cascade:**
1. You become hyperaware of the clock but lose sense of actual duration
2. Short tasks feel "not worth starting" because they might make you late  
3. Long tasks feel impossible because you can't focus with something looming
4. You're frozen between now and then, unable to engage with either

**The Loop:** The more you check the clock, the more fragmented your time sense becomes, making you feel even more stuck

## 🌙 Bedtime Procrastination  
### ⏰ The Time Trap: When Night Becomes Day

**The Setup:** You're tired but can't stop scrolling, watching, or doing "just one more thing"

**The ADHD Response:** Nighttime amplifies time blindness and dopamine-seeking
- **Time blindness** makes you underestimate how late it is
- **Hyperfocus** keeps you locked into stimulating activities
- **Transition trouble** makes shifting to bedtime feel like hitting a wall
- **Revenge bedtime** happens when you're reclaiming "me time" after a stressful day

**The Cascade:**
1. Your brain seeks stimulation right when it should be winding down
2. Each "10 more minutes" erases awareness of actual time passing
3. Racing thoughts ramp up just when you try to be still
4. Tomorrow's overwhelm makes tonight feel like your last chance for fun

**The Loop:** Poor sleep makes the next day harder, increasing your need for nighttime escape

## ⏰ Chronic Lateness
### ⏰ The Time Trap: When "Plenty of Time" Becomes None

**The Setup:** You know you need to leave at 2:00 PM, it's 1:45 PM, you think "plenty of time"

**The ADHD Response:** Time estimation fails catastrophically
- **Time blindness** makes 15 minutes feel like an hour
- **Executive dysfunction** can't sequence getting-ready tasks
- **Transition paralysis** makes stopping current activity nearly impossible

**The Cascade:**
1. You underestimate how long each step takes (shower, find keys, drive there)
2. "One more thing" syndrome kicks in - just answer this email, just tidy this
3. Suddenly it's 2:10 and you're not even dressed
4. Panic mode creates chaos, making you forget items and move slower

**The Loop:** Being late creates shame and anxiety, which makes time management even harder next time

## 🔔 Sleeping Through Alarms
### ⏰ The Time Trap: When Your Brain Rejects Morning

**The Setup:** You set 5 alarms but somehow sleep through all of them

**The ADHD Response:** Your circadian rhythm and executive function are out of sync
- **Circadian rhythm disruption** makes your natural sleep-wake cycle irregular
- **Sleep inertia** from poor sleep quality makes waking feel impossible
- **Executive dysfunction** impairs the decision-making needed to get out of bed
- **Time blindness** distorts your sense of morning urgency

**The Cascade:**
1. Late bedtime (from bedtime procrastination) creates sleep debt
2. ADHD brain doesn't produce clear "wake up" signals at alarm time
3. Each snooze feels like "just 5 more minutes" but becomes 30
4. You wake up groggy and behind schedule, rushing through morning

**The Loop:** Poor morning routine makes the whole day feel chaotic, leading to more bedtime procrastination
