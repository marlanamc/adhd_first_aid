## 📚 Sources for Double Booking

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD contributes to double booking — through time blindness, impulsive yeses, overcommitment, disorganization, and unrealistic scheduling — and offer strategies like external calendars, boundaries, time buffers, and recovery plans to help ADHDers manage their time more sustainably.

---

### 🧠 Why ADHD Leads to Double Booking

**ADHD 2.0**  
_Edward M. Hallowell, John J. Ratey (2021)_  
➤ Recommends **backup systems, reminders, and alarms** to prevent missed or overlapping appointments. Emphasizes using **external tools** like calendars and lists for planning and follow-through.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Nikki Kinzer, Pete D. Wright (2024)_  
➤ Introduces a **complete planning system** to unify fragmented tools. Advises using **time limits, alarms, partner check-ins**, and transition planning to prevent hyperfocus-related schedule overlap. Encourages **resilient recovery**, not perfection.

**Order from Chaos**  
_Jaclyn Paul (2018)_  
➤ Stresses that your brain is not a planner. Recommends putting **every commitment on an external calendar** and **saying no** when already stretched thin.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
_Ju Domi (2025)_  
➤ Highlights how **time blindness and disconnection from future consequences** can cause scheduling conflicts. Recommends **flexible templates, visual planners**, and **buffer zones** to reduce risk.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Describes how **time mismanagement and impulsivity** lead to double booking. Recommends **personalized systems** and scheduled stop/start points to prevent hyperfocus from crowding out other tasks.

### 🗓️ External Systems & Time Awareness

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Encourages **externalizing commitments** using visual or auditory tools. Recommends building a **personalized toolbox** that considers working memory struggles and attention gaps.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Jennifer Kemp, Monique Mitchelson (2024)_  
➤ Focuses on **executive skills** like organizing, prioritizing, and working memory. Encourages **assertive communication and boundary-setting** to prevent overcommitment and missed plans.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
_Peg Dawson, Richard Guare (2015)_  
➤ Suggests using **checklists and task tracking** to support working memory and reduce accidental overlap in commitments.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
_Kate Kelly, Peggy Ramundo (2006)_  
➤ Explains how **overloaded to-do lists and poor time estimation** lead to unrealistic schedules. Normalizes “time troubles” and supports reframing failure with compassion.

---

### ✋ Boundaries & Saying No

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Sari Solden, Michelle Frank (2019)_  
➤ Advocates for **strategic no’s** and scheduling based on **energy rhythms**, not pressure. Notes that understanding **executive function limitations** is key to preventing burnout and overbooking.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Recommends **guardrails and decision boundaries** to avoid impulsive yeses. Encourages saying no to low-energy-time commitments to protect future capacity.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD**  
_(Unspecified Author)_  
➤ Empowers saying no as a **core self-care skill**, helping ADHDers resist overcommitting.

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
_Melissa Orlov (2010)_  
➤ Explains how **missed commitments** can erode trust. Recommends developing **shared cues** and communication tools between partners to keep expectations clear.

### ✅ Accountability, Recovery, and Clarity

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_James Kustow (2024)_  
➤ Recommends placing **messages to self** at points of action and reflecting on **your ADHD signature** to create better scheduling systems.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail L. Levrini (2023)_  
➤ Offers tools for **creating time-sensitive goals**, **asking for written clarification**, and setting **self-imposed deadlines** to boost follow-through and reduce accidental conflicts.

**Mad About Money**  
_Maddy Alexander-Grout (2025)_  
➤ Notes that **forgetting dates and double booking** are common ADHD symptoms. Recommends acknowledging patterns as the first step toward change.
