## Sources for Text Message Avoidance

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack why ADHD brains avoid even simple messages — from emotional overwhelm and executive dysfunction to shame spirals and broken communication systems — and offer strategies for responding with more ease, structure, and self-compassion.

---

### 📵 Overwhelm, Avoidance & Emotional Load

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)  
➤ Acknowledges that many ADHDers have **“at least one text you dread opening”**. Recommends **structured workflows**, dedicated message-checking times, and **phone tools like Do Not Disturb** to reduce notification fatigue and improve follow-through.

**Small Talk**  
Richard Pink & Roxanne Pink (2024)  
➤ Connects **text avoidance** to broader ADHD overwhelm — including doom piles and unread messages. Urges readers to **unlearn the internalized “ADHD lies”** (e.g., "I'm lazy") and replace them with **self-compassion**, reframing avoidance as a neurological response, not a personal failure.

---

### 🧠 Executive Function & Communication Management

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Abigail L. Levrini (2025)  
➤ Suggests **scheduled check-in windows** for messages — with phones silenced during focus time and messages treated as a **timed task or post-task reward**. This turns scattered communication into a structured, manageable routine and reduces the guilt spiral of avoidance.
