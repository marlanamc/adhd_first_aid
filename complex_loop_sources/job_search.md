## 📚 Sources for Job Search

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD impacts job searching — from executive function struggles, poor time estimation, and shame spirals to career mismatches and workplace burnout — and offer strategies for finding work that aligns with ADHD strengths, requesting accommodations, reframing quitting, and building self-worth in the process.

---

### 🧠 ADHD & The Search for Fitting Work

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail Levrini (2025)_  
➤ Includes a chapter on **Finding Job Satisfaction**. Offers tools for identifying **strengths and challenges**, understanding **accommodations**, and communicating needs from a position of **self-advocacy**. Recognizes that ADHDers often **cycle through jobs** without support.

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
_Sari Solden (2013)_  
➤ Shares an **ADHD Work Decision Tree** and emphasizes **non-disclosure scripts** when requesting accommodations. Encourages **career planning based on interests, values, and hidden gifts**, especially for women whose work struggles may go unseen.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
_Kate Kelly, Peggy Ramundo (2006)_  
➤ Encourages **forgiving past failures** and using tools like **balance sheets and inventories** to identify ADHD-friendly work. Normalizes nonlinear paths and emphasizes **finding a niche that values your lived experience**.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Discusses **career frustration among high-achieving ADHDers** and encourages building **custom systems** instead of forcing neurotypical ones. Shares job resource links, including **accommodation networks** and disability support.

**Attention Deficit Disorder: A Different Perception**  
_Thom Hartmann (1997)_  
➤ Offers a strengths-based reframe of ADHD traits like **risk-taking, energy, and scanning for novelty** — and how they shine in **nontraditional work environments**.

### 💼 Strength-Based Careers & Self-Employment

**Productivity With ADHD**  
_Ju Domi (2025)_  
➤ Reframes the ADHD brain as **wired for innovation and hyperfocus**. Encourages using your **natural patterns to fuel success** rather than force conformity. Offers tools for building **custom systems** that support ADHD careers.

**Mad About Money**  
_Maddy Alexander-Grout (2025)_  
➤ Offers tips for **starting a business with ADHD**, and recommends looking for **neurodivergent-friendly employers**. Encourages aligning your **values, passions, and strengths** to carve out a fulfilling path.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ Validates **entrepreneurial and creative work** for ADHDers. Encourages **remote and flexible careers**, requesting accommodations, and honoring your energy rhythms.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
_Richard Pink, Roxanne Pink (2024)_  
➤ Reframes quitting as **a valid strategy**, not a failure. Encourages job seekers to **lean into ADHD strengths**, reimagine hard work, and pursue paths that allow for **sustainable bursts of passion and focus**. Promotes **self-advocacy and accommodation**.

**Start Finishing**  
_Charlie Gilkey (2019)_  
➤ While not ADHD-specific, this book encourages **creative careers, project clarity, and realistic timelines** — all crucial for ADHD-friendly work planning.

### 🧠 Advocacy, Identity & Accommodations

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Jennifer Kemp, Monique Mitchelson (2024)_  
➤ Explores **disclosure vs. masking**, offers **assertive scripts** for requesting support, and provides real-world tips like asking for **quieter environments or scheduling buffers**. Emphasizes **self-compassion and personalized pacing**.

**The Neurodiversity Edge**  
_Maureen Dunne (2024)_  
➤ Encourages job seekers to see their **cognitive differences as marketable strengths**. Frames neurodivergence as **economically valuable**, especially in innovation-driven fields.
