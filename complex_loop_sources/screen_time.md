## Sources for Screen Time

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources examine how ADHD brains interact with screens — from hyperfocus and dopamine-seeking to procrastination and burnout. They also offer practical tools to reduce digital overwhelm, use screen time with intention, and create systems that support focus, rest, and emotional regulation.

---

### 📱 Screen Time & Stimulation

**ADHD 2.0**  
Edward M. Hallowell, M.D. & John J. Ratey, M.D. (2021)  
➤ Recommends turning **screens off an hour before bed** to support sleep. Highlights the importance of **digital habit tools** like Habyts for managing screen time in families.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Meredith Carder (2024)  
➤ Explains that phones become **dopamine machines** for ADHDers, leading to exhaustion and overstimulation. Suggests redefining rest and **automating small tasks** to preserve mental energy.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Lisa Dee (2025)  
➤ Describes phone use as “endless dopamine hits.” Urges you to **reclaim your mornings** by reducing early screen exposure and being mindful of mental clutter.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)  
➤ Calls passive scrolling an **ineffective coping tool** for RSD or emotional discomfort. Suggests intentional planning, **muting notifications**, and digital systems with **reminders and searchability** to reduce overwhelm.

**Small Talk**  
Richard Pink & Roxanne Pink (2024)  
➤ Notes that screen use can be a **coping mechanism for sensory overload**. Warns that consuming too much online debate or content can become **ruminative distraction** instead of helpful learning.

### 🧠 Productivity & Digital Tools

**Productivity With ADHD**  
Ju DOMI (2025)  
➤ Encourages ADHD-friendly digital systems for time tracking, file management, and planning. Warns that **time blindness and hyperfocus** can make screen use risky without **boundaries and alarms**.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Abigail L. Levrini (2025)  
➤ Acknowledges screen-based distractions like games and suggests a **“digital toolbox”** with alarms and planning apps. Recommends using **apps like NoodleTools** to support organization.

**How to ADHD**  
Jessica McCabe (2024)  
➤ Explains that **dual monitors** can help working memory, and that for some, screen time (like rom-coms) acts as **co-regulation**. Urges self-awareness when using screens for comfort or support.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
James Kustow (2024)  
➤ Offers screen-reading tips like **light adjustments and short bursts** for focus. Also recommends **pre-programmed reminders** at points of action to reduce impulsivity.

### ⚠️ Passive Use, Overwhelm & Burnout

**Tiny Experiments**  
Anne-Laure Le Cunff (2025)  
➤ Explains how **hyperfocus on productivity systems** can ironically lead to screen-time overload. Warns that time-tracking apps can add pressure rather than solve it.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD**  
(Author & Year N/A)  
➤ Recommends screen time as a form of **intentional relaxation**, like watching a show after a tough day. Also promotes using **apps and digital systems** to support task and time management.
