## 📚 Sources for Intimacy

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD affects intimacy — from shame, rejection sensitivity, hyperfocus, emotional dysregulation, and executive dysfunction to impulsivity and communication struggles — and offer tools for fostering trust, closeness, self-compassion, and connection in romantic and emotional relationships.

---

### 🧠 Why Intimacy Feels Complicated with ADHD

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Nikki Kinzer, Pete D. Wright (2024)_  
➤ Frames **radical acceptance** and **shame reduction** as key to building intimacy. Explains how **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)** leads to fear of vulnerability and avoidance. Encourages using **community support**, **verbal processing**, and permission to unmask as pathways to authentic connection.

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
_Melissa Orlov (2010)_  
➤ Offers a comprehensive framework for navigating **romantic partnership breakdowns caused by unmanaged ADHD**. Emphasizes **empathy**, **verbal cues**, shared responsibility for treatment, and the power of **trying differently**. Promotes **mindful attention as the foundation of romance**.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
_Kate Kelly, Peggy Ramundo (2006)_  
➤ Directly addresses **ADD and sexuality**, encouraging individuals to cultivate a **compassionate relationship with their whole self** — including "oopses." Reframes ADHD from shameful excuse to valid **explanation**, easing intimacy-related self-doubt.

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Discusses how **hyperfixation, forgetfulness, and overwhelm** can damage connection. Encourages partners (“Hearts”) to use **calendars, boundaries, and notepads** to support intimacy and reduce emotional tension. Emphasizes the need for **clear boundaries and mutual care**.

**Order from Chaos**  
_Jaclyn Paul (2018)_  
➤ Identifies **household mess and hyperfocus** as common intimacy stressors. Suggests limiting hyperfocus in shared spaces and **co-reading ADHD resources** to improve relational understanding. Acknowledges that **distraction and disorganization can affect sex and emotional closeness**.

### 💬 Connection, Vulnerability, & Communication

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Sari Solden, Michelle Frank (2019)_  
➤ Encourages **authenticity and internal equality** in relationships. Notes that shame interferes with intimacy and that **connection and mutual support** are non-negotiables for a thriving ADHD life. Promotes the concept that “you can’t do ADHD alone.”

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
_Richard Pink, Roxanne Pink (2024)_  
➤ Dissects toxic beliefs like **“I am a burden”**, reinforcing that **having needs is human**. Urges partners and friends to **reflect back worth**, **interrupt shame spirals**, and build safe, two-way support systems. Reinforces the idea that **true intimacy starts with self-worth**.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Emphasizes that **belonging, unmasking, and shared stories** are essential for intimacy. ADHD can wreak havoc on connection when misunderstood; healing starts with emotional safety and self-expression.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail Levrini (2023)_  
➤ Acknowledges that **zoning out, impulsivity, and memory lapses** hurt intimacy. Offers tools like **paraphrasing, specific listening goals**, and **shared accountability** to improve relational trust and presence.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Talks about **"Loving Someone With ADHD"** and how structure, compassion, and understanding are necessary for partners. Recommends **accountability partners and open feedback loops** to navigate relational tension with clarity.

### 💞 Building Intimacy Through Self & Shared Systems

**73 ADHD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life Now**  
_ADDitude Experts_  
➤ Advises **scheduling sex** as a valid, ADHD-friendly strategy. Argues that **consistent intimacy is more romantic than forgotten intimacy**.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ Identifies deep connection and being “seen” as core ADHD strengths. Connects **physical health, joy, creativity, and authenticity** to stronger intimate bonds.

**Mad About Money**  
_Maddy Alexander-Grout (2025)_  
➤ Urges readers to **reframe intrusive thoughts**, normalize needing support, and dismantle stigma. Affirms that ADHDers wear many relational hats (partner, friend, parent) and all are valid.

**Real Self-Care**  
_Pooja Lakshmin, MD (2023)_  
➤ Frames **boundary-setting and emotional safety** as real self-care. Explores how trauma history can complicate intimacy and encourages **community care** to foster safety in connection.

**Start Finishing**  
_Charlie Gilkey (2019)_  
➤ Recognizes the **collaborative nature of creative and emotional work**. Cites _Financial Intimacy_ as a key relationship resource and affirms that love and support fuel personal growth.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Jennifer Kemp, Monique Mitchelson (2024)_  
➤ Emphasizes **assertive communication, boundary-setting**, and **small, meaningful accommodations** in social and intimate relationships. Encourages **authenticity and being seen** as cornerstones of emotional intimacy.

**Motivational Interviewing**  
_William R. Miller, Stephen Rollnick (2023)_  
➤ While not ADHD-specific, promotes **relational attunement** and collaborative conversation skills — both essential for intimate connection and mutual understanding.

**The Neurodiversity Edge**  
_Maureen Dunne (2024)_  
➤ Advocates for **psychological safety and inclusive communication**, even in personal dynamics. Encourages seeing and respecting neurodivergent identity as central to trust and intimacy.

**Tiny Experiments**  
_Anne-Laure Le Cunff (2025)_  
➤ Introduces the **“intimacy gradient”** — the idea that emotional closeness can be managed through intentional boundaries and space. Validates **isolation and loneliness** as struggles with ADHD that require gentle, creative reconnection.
