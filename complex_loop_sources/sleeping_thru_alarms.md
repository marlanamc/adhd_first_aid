## Sources for Sleeping Through Alarms

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD impacts sleep, waking, and transitions — from time blindness and revenge bedtime procrastination to emotional dysregulation and inconsistent routines. They offer practical tools for improving wake-up consistency using backup systems, sleep hygiene, and ADHD-friendly alarm strategies.

---

### ⏰ Alarm Strategies & Morning Routines

**ADHD 2.0**  
Edward M. Hallowell & John J. Ratey (2021)  
➤ Notes that ADHDers often turn off alarms with good intentions, then get distracted. Recommends using **backup systems**, like a partner or timed alerts, to follow through.

**How to ADHD**  
Jessica McCabe (2024)  
➤ Discusses **delayed chronotypes** and bedtime sacrifice. Encourages **multiple alarms**, **coffee prepped in advance**, **automation tools**, and ensuring **sleep takes priority over productivity**.

**Daily Routine Examples from Adults with ADHD**  
ADDitude Editors (2025)  
➤ One reader shares that setting **eight alarms** is their most unbreakable habit. Avoiding **morning social media** helps get them moving faster.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Jennifer Kemp & Monique Mitchelson (2024)  
➤ Advises setting **alarms for specific morning tasks**, not just wake-up. Suggests **repeated snoozing** as a valid tactic to build momentum.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)  
➤ Challenges shame around alarms. Suggests **rotating alarm sounds**, placing **clocks around the house**, and using alarms for **transitions and wake-ups**.

### 😴 Sleep Prep & Sleep Quality

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Meredith Carder (2024)  
➤ Links **revenge bedtime procrastination** to emotional avoidance. Suggests **tracking your sleep patterns**, maintaining **consistent wake-up times**, and avoiding **alcohol before bed**.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
James Kustow (2024)  
➤ Highlights ADHD-related sleep issues like **restless legs or apnea**. Encourages **wind-down routines**, gentle sleep hygiene, and using reminders at the **“point of action”** to help with transitions.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
Abigail L. Levrini (2025)  
➤ Suggests using alarms **5 minutes before transitions**, and training the brain that **"bed means sleep"** to improve rest and waking patterns.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Nancy Ratey (2008)  
➤ Advises using **three alarms in different rooms**, or **light timers** to encourage bedtime closure. Warns against staying up late on screens and **missing natural sleep cues**.

**Productivity With ADHD – Complete Guide**  
Ju DOMI (2025)  
➤ Recommends a structured **"wind-down sequence"** and **recovery plan** for missed sleep. Ties **alarm use to environmental adjustments** and executive support.
