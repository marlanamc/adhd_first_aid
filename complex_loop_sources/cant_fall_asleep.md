## 📚 Sources for Can’t Fall Asleep

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD impacts sleep initiation — from mental overactivity and hyperfocus to delayed circadian rhythms, sensory needs, and revenge bedtime procrastination — and offer strategies like wind-down routines, environmental adjustments, sleep aids, and mindset shifts to help ADHD brains transition into rest.

---

### 🧠 Why Falling Asleep Is Hard with ADHD

**ADHD 2.0**  
_Edward M. Hallowell, John J. Ratey (2021)_  
➤ Recommends **turning off screens at least an hour before bed** to calm the nervous system. Introduces the **Fisher Wallace Stimulator** as a non-drug option for sleep, and notes **sleep apnea** may mimic or worsen ADHD symptoms.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_James Kustow (2024)_  
➤ Identifies **“initial insomnia”** as common in ADHD. Shares 10 sleep tips, including **wind-down routines**, **sleep hygiene**, and optional supports like **melatonin** or **sedating antihistamines** (under supervision).

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Emphasizes how **transition trouble and late-night distractions** can prevent sleep. Suggests **reminder signs, environment changes**, and recruiting others to support follow-through.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail L. Levrini (2025)_  
➤ Advises **training your brain to associate bed with sleep**, avoiding other activities like reading in bed. Links sleep delay to **task initiation and procrastination** patterns common in ADHD.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Sari Solden, Michelle Frank, Ellen Littman (2019)_  
➤ Frames sleep as foundational to ADHD management. Discusses **teeth grinding (bruxism)** and prioritizing any necessary changes to improve rest and reduce symptom flare-ups.

### 🛌 Environmental & Sensory Supports

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Explains **delayed melatonin release** in ADHD brains. Suggests **engaging but soothing wind-down rituals**, **soft textures**, **weighted blankets**, and **accepting rest even without sleep** as part of recovery.

**Order from Chaos**  
_Jaclyn Paul (2018)_  
➤ Warns against **“hyperfocus traps” in the bedroom** and recommends keeping highly engaging activities outside sleep spaces to preserve sleep cues.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
_Ju Domi (2025)_  
➤ Encourages creating a **“sleep optimization plan”** that includes **environment design**, **routine components**, and **recovery blocks** to calm overstimulation and prep for sleep.

**Women with Attention Deficit Disorder**  
_Sari Solden (2013)_  
➤ Recommends **soothing, consistent bedtime rituals** that invite relaxation instead of forcing it. Suggests discussing medications that might be causing or solving sleep issues.

### 🌀 Emotional Regulation & Wind-Down Strategies

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Ties **revenge bedtime procrastination and rumination** to sleep delays. Recommends **cutting off “hyperfocus rabbit holes”**, replacing them with **defined wind-down routines**, and avoiding substances like **alcohol** that disrupt restful sleep.

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ Shares how **sleep hypnosis audio** can help ADHD brains fall asleep through imaginative trance states. Highlights the downstream effects of poor sleep on **focus, memory, and emotional regulation**.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
_Peg Dawson, Richard Guare (2015)_  
➤ Connects bedtime procrastination to sleep initiation issues. Stresses the importance of a **reasonable, consistent bedtime** and training the brain to associate bed with rest, not stimulation.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy?!**  
_Kate Kelly, Peggy Ramundo (2006)_  
➤ Suggests finding **“ADD-friendly” calming techniques** to settle the **“adrenalized system”**, including alternative approaches to meditation that suit restless minds.
