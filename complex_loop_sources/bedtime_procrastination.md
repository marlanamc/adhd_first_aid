## 📚 Sources for Bedtime Procrastination

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD impacts sleep — from hyperfocus, time blindness, and revenge procrastination to transition trouble and emotional burnout — and offer strategies like wind-down rituals, bedtime alarms, and realistic routines to help ADHD brains rest.

---

### 🧠 Understanding the ADHD-Sleep Connection

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Nikki Kinzer, Pete D. Wright (2024)_  
➤ Explains the **“ADHD Time Zone”** and how **time blindness and hyperfocus** disrupt sleep schedules. Recommends tools like alarms, time warnings, and accountability check-ins to support realistic nighttime transitions.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Details how **difficulty with transitions and hyperfocus** can derail bedtime. Advises identifying your “traps” (e.g., email, phone) and posting reminders to support follow-through on sleep intentions.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
_Peg Dawson, Richard Guare (2015)_  
➤ Labels **bedtime procrastination** as a “particularly insidious form of procrastination” due to its impact on executive function. Suggests making bedtime a consistent, non-negotiable part of daily structure.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
_Ju Domi (2025)_  
➤ Connects **time blindness and poor recovery habits** with bedtime delays. Recommends “anchor points,” **wind-down blocks**, and sleep optimization routines to support executive function through consistent rest.

### 🧰 Practical Tools & Sleep Strategies

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Defines **revenge bedtime procrastination** and explores ADHD's natural **night owl tendencies**. Suggests replacing stimulating habits with **wind-down rituals**, **cutoff times**, and meeting your needs earlier in the day to reduce nighttime reclaiming.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_James Kustow (2024)_  
➤ Offers a **TEN TIPS FOR OPTIMISING SLEEP** framework. Encourages **flexible routines** (5 out of 7 nights), not perfection, and validates the ADHD brain’s struggle with stimulation at night.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Explains how **overwhelm and “salvaging the day”** can lead to late-night work sprints. Suggests avoiding **“hyperfocus rabbit holes”** and replacing them with defined, soothing activities that wind the brain down.

**Order from Chaos**  
_Jaclyn Paul (2018)_  
➤ Recommends identifying **hyperfocus triggers** and removing them from the sleep space. Encourages conscious placement of activities and **boundary-setting around screen time** to reduce late-night overstimulation.

**Tiny Experiments**  
_Anne-Laure Le Cunff (2025)_  
➤ While not sleep-specific, promotes **“intentional imperfection”** to reduce the drive to “finish everything” at night — a key mindset shift for ADHD brains caught in endless cycles of task completion before bed.

### 💡 Mindset Shifts & Emotional Recovery

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ Describes how **internal urgency and overstimulation** delay bedtime. Embraces a **“good enough is good enough”** sleep philosophy, and promotes flexible wind-down routines that reduce shame and support well-being.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Highlights **rumination and emotional spirals** that surface at night. Emphasizes interrupting this loop with **bounded bedtime activities** and reframes late-night energy surges as misdirected coping.

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Encourages getting **emotional needs met earlier in the day**, so that bedtime doesn’t feel like the only time for pleasure, escape, or solitude.
