## 🛒 Sources for Online Shopping

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD affects impulsivity, decision-making, dopamine seeking, and time distortion — and offer strategies like pause rituals, financial filters, external cues, and distraction tracking to help ADHD brains shop more intentionally online.

---

### 💸 Impulsivity & Overspending

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ ADHD brains are **more vulnerable to urgency-driven marketing**. Recommends starting with **one micro habit** to interrupt impulsive buying loops. Warns that without strategies, impulsive spending can spiral into shame and bank account depletion.

**Mad About Money**  
_Maddy Alexander-Grout (2025)_  
➤ Encourages **self-compassion for impulse purchases** and suggests **resale platforms like Vinted** to make ADHD-friendly swaps without the high price tag.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
_Richard Pink & Roxanne Pink (2024)_  
➤ Introduces the **“ADHD Tax”** — missed return windows, forgotten purchases, and overspending due to executive dysfunction. Normalizes these struggles and urges systems to prevent financial fallout.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_James Kustow (2024)_  
➤ Suggests **“watch lists”** to pause before purchasing, and recommends placing **tactile reminders** (like a blob of Blu-Tack) on credit cards as a cue to reflect.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD or Other Disorders**  
_(Author/Date Not Specified)_  
➤ Offers tricks like the **“Cooling-Off Period”** and asking **“Where Will This Live?”** before buying. Recommends setting **alarms to interrupt shopping hyperfocus** and using visual/fidget tools like elastic bands to redirect impulses.

### 🧠 Decision Paralysis & Research Spirals

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)_  
➤ Describes how **product decisions become paralyzing** with ADHD — the more you research, the harder it is to choose. Recommends **tracking distractions** and reducing overwhelm to make aligned choices faster.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ While not directly about shopping, this guide explores the **mental cost of financial disorganization** and suggests delegating or structuring tasks (like managing accounts or budgets) to reduce stress and decision fatigue.

**Overload: Attention Deficit Disorder and the Addictive Brain**  
_David Miller & Kenneth Blum (1996)_  
➤ Frames online shopping behaviors as **dopamine-seeking patterns** tied to the addictive brain. Encourages recognizing compulsive loops as neurobiological, not moral, failures.

---

### 📱 Digital Overstimulation

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ In _“Dethrone Your Phone,”_ explains how **endless dopamine from social media and screen time** fuels impulsive behaviors like shopping. Recommends phone boundaries to reduce exposure to marketing and overthinking.

**ADD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life**  
_Judith Kolberg & Kathleen Nadeau (2002)_  
➤ Foundational book on ADHD organization. While not focused on shopping, its strategies for **reducing digital clutter, tracking records, and decision batching** are helpful for minimizing impulsive online spending.
