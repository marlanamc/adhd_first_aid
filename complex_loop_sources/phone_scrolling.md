## 📱 Sources for Phone Scrolling

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD brains crave stimulation and novelty — and how phones deliver it on demand. But constant scrolling can drain energy, increase stress, and interrupt focus. These books offer tools for managing phone use, reducing digital overwhelm, and creating systems that work _with_ your brain, not against it.

---

### 🔁 Dopamine, Distraction & Digital Habits

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ Phones deliver **"endless dopamine hits"** and flood the brain with others’ noise and urgency. Recommends **reducing phone use, especially first thing in the morning**, to protect energy and focus.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Scrolling often **feels like rest but leaves you more drained**. Reframes it as emotional avoidance and urges **intentional recharging** instead of defaulting to passive scrolling.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)_  
➤ Describes ADHD as **“a brain with all notifications on and no Do Not Disturb.”** Emphasizes your power to **mute distractions**, simplify digital tools, and offload info into **clear systems** that don’t overwhelm.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail L. Levrini (2025)_  
➤ Recommends **limiting check-ins** to specific times. Turn off ringers, let voicemail pick up, and **treat phone breaks as rewards**, not interruptions.

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Calls phone notifications **“cues” that can either help or hijack your attention**. Stresses the importance of managing them mindfully — not removing them, but using them wisely.

### 📵 System Design & Containment

**Order from Chaos**  
_Jaclyn Paul (2018)_  
➤ Phones **derail ADHD organization** when left unchecked. Suggests **"literal phone containment"** — a home base for devices — and emphasizes that **if your system isn’t working, change the system, not yourself**.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
_Ju DOMI (2025)_  
➤ Treats digital clutter like physical clutter. Recommends **structured, searchable digital systems**, inbox protocols, and **single-tasking tools** to reduce mental noise and regain control.

**One Weird Trick to Quit Stupid, Timewasting Content**  
_(Author/Date Not Specified)_  
➤ Says it plainly: **“Stop scrolling.”** Recommends building your **capacity for boredom** by looking around, reading something real, or letting your mind wander. ADHD diagnosis is not a full stop — it’s a **signal to find smarter workarounds**.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD**  
_(Author/Date Not Specified)_  
➤ Recommends **embracing technology** wisely through apps, timers, and digital tools that support structure — but doesn’t directly address scrolling behavior.
