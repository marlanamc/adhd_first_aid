## Sources for Workout Avoidance

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD makes movement feel harder than it “should” — from executive dysfunction and burnout to low motivation, forgotten plans, and sensory barriers. The best approach isn’t discipline — it’s self-compassion, small wins, flexible routines, and redefining what counts as exercise.

---

### 🧠 ADHD, Executive Function & Movement

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Lisa Dee (2025)  
➤ Names **executive dysfunction** as the real barrier to movement — not laziness. Suggests **short bursts of movement** instead of long workouts, and encourages you to **trust your body over rigid plans**.

**Order from Chaos**  
Jaclyn L. Paul (2018)  
➤ Challenges the idea that we need to “feel motivated” to move. Reframes ADHD symptoms like being maxed out or disorganized as energy-drainers that impact movement — and urges us to **lower the bar to free up energy**.

**The Smart but Scattered Guide to Success**  
Peg Dawson & Richard Guare (2015)  
➤ Explains how executive function demands (like getting dressed, choosing a workout, driving to the gym) **drain mental energy fast**. Recommends **building a slow ramp** with smaller steps instead of jumping into big plans.

### 🧸 Self-Compassion, Burnout & Body Trust

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
Jennifer Kemp & Monique Mitchelson (2024)  
➤ Acknowledges **burnout and low internal resources** as reasons for avoidance. Encourages using **“better-than-nothing” strategies** that help you succeed more often — not perfectly.

**Small Talk**  
Richard & Roxanne Pink (2024)  
➤ Debunks the lie that you’re “lazy.” Encourages curiosity and **gentle investigation of what’s _actually_ in the way**, not self-blame.

**You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid, or Crazy?!**  
Kate Kelly & Peggy Ramundo (2006)  
➤ Highlights that there’s **no single “right” way** to exercise. ADHD brains are louder and more restless — we need **flexibility, not perfection**.

---

### 🏃‍♀️ Habits, Routines & Environmental Support

**Tiny Habits**  
BJ Fogg (2019)  
➤ Encourages **breaking habits down into tiny actions**. Success isn’t about intensity — it’s about showing up in the smallest way and building momentum.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
Nancy A. Ratey (2008)  
➤ Links procrastination to **unreliable working memory**. Suggests **sleeping in workout clothes** or keeping a **pre-packed gym bag** to reduce friction.

**100+ Successful Tips for Adults with ADHD**  
(2025)  
➤ Recommends **keeping things you use together in one place** — a helpful cue to keep workout gear visible and ready to go.

---

### ⚡ Motivation, Energy, and Dopamine

**ADHD 2.0**  
Hallowell & Ratey (2021)  
➤ Treats **exercise like medicine** — not optional. Movement improves focus, memory, and mood — making it one of the most powerful ADHD tools.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
Ju DOMI (2025)  
➤ Promotes **minimal workouts for low-energy days** and larger ones when energy allows. It’s about **prioritizing your priorities** — not chasing perfection.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
James Kustow (2024)  
➤ Warns against **setting unrealistic fitness goals** and encourages building momentum with smaller, more doable goals like **once-a-week gym visits**.

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)  
➤ Reframes inconsistency as normal and offers this truth: **“The key to success isn’t consistency — it’s having a recovery plan.”** Long workouts often get skipped, so keep it short and real.
