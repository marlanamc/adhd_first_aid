## Sources for Social Media Spirals

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources examine how ADHD interacts with social media — from dopamine loops and information overload to shame spirals, emotional dysregulation, and time blindness. They offer strategies for reclaiming your attention, protecting your self-worth, and navigating online spaces with intention.

---

### 📱 Dopamine, Distraction & Exhaustion

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
Lisa Dee (2025)  
➤ Describes how checking **email and social feeds first thing** floods the ADHD brain with other people’s "noise and negativity". Suggests **“Dethrone Your Phone”** to reduce stimulation and start the day with intention.

**Mad About Money**  
Maddy Alexander-Grout (2025)  
➤ Acknowledges the role of **“daily dopamine hits”** from social media. Notes that ADHDers may use platforms like TikTok as **coping tools**, which can quickly spiral into overstimulation or avoidance.

**Tiny Experiments**  
Anne-Laure Le Cunff (2025)  
➤ Explains how **hyperfocus on digital content** — even productivity tips — can turn into **unproductive spirals**, adding mental load instead of relieving it. Urges users to **“build your capacity for boredom”** instead of defaulting to social media. Suggests choosing books, observation, or sensory check-ins over endless scrolling.

### 😵‍💫 Shame Spirals & Emotional Dysregulation

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)  
➤ Connects **social media criticism and rejection** to **RSD-triggered shame spirals**. Promotes **radical acceptance** and self-worth beyond online validation.

**Small Talk**  
Richard Pink & Roxanne Pink (2024)  
➤ Warns about **obsessing over online debates** as a distraction from healing. Encourages shifting focus from external noise to **dismantling internalized ADHD “lies”** and building resilience.

**Nutrition for ADHD and Dyslexia**  
Emma Derbyshire (2025)  
➤ Highlights how **social media information overload** can overwhelm parents, contributing to **decision fatigue and mistrust** of advice — a digital spiral of confusion and paralysis.
