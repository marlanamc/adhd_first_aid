## 📚 Sources for Decision Overwhelm

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD brains get overloaded by choices — from perfectionism and overthinking to executive dysfunction and emotional dysregulation — and offer strategies like chunking, externalization, intentional imperfection, and customized systems to help you simplify decisions and reclaim your focus.

---

### 🧠 Why ADHD Makes Decisions So Exhausting

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Nikki Kinzer, Pete D. Wright (2024)_  
➤ Defines **overwhelm and paralysis** as consequences of the ADHD brain’s struggle to **prioritize, plan, and evaluate focus**. Warns against **all-or-nothing thinking** and encourages quick, flexible decisions. Suggests **choosing tools within a week** and trusting what works _for you_ instead of chasing the “perfect” system.

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Identifies **decision paralysis** as a signal-vs-noise problem, where **everything feels equally urgent**. Suggests reducing **cognitive load** through ADHD-friendly tools that externalize and simplify tasks.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
_Ju Domi (2025)_  
➤ Explains how **overthinking, perfectionism, and disorganized systems** fuel decision fatigue. Recommends **task prioritization systems**, **time mapping**, and **flexible routines** to reduce choice pressure and create reliable flow.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Frames overwhelm as a buildup of **“decision points”**. Recommends **moving from thought to action** to calm the nervous system. Suggests **calming strategies** (e.g., nature, box breathing) before problem-solving.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Sari Solden, Michelle Frank (2019)_  
➤ Emphasizes **individualized strategies** and **recognizing tipping points for overwhelm**. Encourages you to identify your **energy cycles** and stop trying to “fix” yourself with rigid systems. There’s no one right way — just your way.

### 🧰 Tools for Reducing Cognitive Load

**Order from Chaos**  
_Jaclyn Paul (2018)_  
➤ Recommends **externalizing all information** to reduce mental overload. Suggests **emptying inboxes**, simplifying inputs, and trusting rest as a valid choice when overload hits.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Explains how **seeing all tasks as equal** and weak memory contributes to decision fatigue. Recommends **narrowing your focus** and creating **personalized systems** that actually work for your brain.

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_James Kustow (2024)_  
➤ Describes ADHD as having a **“brain traffic jam”** when trying to make decisions. Offers **chunking**, streamlining inputs, and **buffer zones** as tools to slow overwhelm and reduce friction.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail L. Levrini (2025)_  
➤ Acknowledges that **micromanagement and perfectionism** create mental fatigue. Offers **challenge-thought reframing**, break-based scheduling, and fear-reducing tools to support more confident decisions.

**Tiny Experiments**  
_Anne-Laure Le Cunff (2025)_  
➤ Warns that over-optimizing can **double the burden of a task**. Recommends embracing **intentional imperfection** and focusing on _long-term consistency_ over short-term polish.

### 💡 Emotional Regulation & Support

**Healthy Happy ADHD**  
_Lisa Dee (2025)_  
➤ Notes that **early-morning tech stimulation** can drain decision-making energy. Suggests focusing on **foundational routines** (e.g., protein, movement) and tuning in to internal instincts over outside expectations.

**Small Talk**  
_Richard Pink, Roxanne Pink (2024)_  
➤ Identifies how **“executive dysfunction” and unrealistic standards** lead to overwhelm. Advocates for **self-acceptance, emotional regulation, and breaking down tasks** to reduce the intensity of decisions.

**Mad About Money**  
_Maddy Alexander-Grout (2025)_  
➤ Normalizes overwhelm in admin-heavy tasks. Recommends **co-deciding with support**, such as having someone walk through forms with you, to make hard decisions easier.

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Jennifer Kemp, Monique Mitchelson (2024)_  
➤ Addresses **burnout from over-decision-making**, advocating for small, compassionate steps. Encourages **value-based planning** and pacing.
