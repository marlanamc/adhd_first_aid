## Sources for Waiting Mode

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources explain how ADHD time agnosia — the brain’s difficulty estimating or perceiving time — creates “waiting mode,” where a future event blocks you from starting anything now. Strategies focus on scheduling with self-awareness, supporting transitions, and using creative communication to reduce pressure.

---

### ⏳ Understanding Time Agnosia & Paralysis

**How to ADHD**  
Jessica McCabe (2024)  
➤ Defines “waiting mode” as one of three key time traps, caused by **time agnosia**. Offers strategies to **identify triggers**, **schedule controllable tasks**, and **communicate creatively** to reduce anxiety about commitments (like texting updates instead of giving a rigid arrival time).

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
Meredith Carder (2024)  
➤ Frames waiting mode as a **natural outcome of time agnosia**. Encourages **self-compassion** and urges ADHDers to build **flexible routines** around known triggers. Also suggests **adjusting how you communicate time to others**, such as avoiding exact times when they add pressure.

---

### 🧠 Time Blindness, Transitions & Self-Compassion

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
Nikki Kinzer & Pete D. Wright (2024)  
➤ Doesn’t use the term “waiting mode,” but deeply explores **time blindness** and **transition struggles**. Recommends gathering **personal data** about how your brain perceives time and using that to build **custom systems that reflect your rhythms**, rather than rigid schedules.
