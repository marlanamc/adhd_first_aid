## 📚 Sources for Friendships

Explore the books, guides, and research that shaped this page. These resources unpack how ADHD impacts friendships — from emotional sensitivity, shame spirals, and executive dysfunction to social forgetfulness, impulsivity, and avoidance — and offer strategies for authentic connection, assertive communication, community building, and boundary-setting that help ADHDers create and sustain meaningful relationships.

---

### 🧠 Why Friendships Are Complex with ADHD

**Unapologetically ADHD**  
_Nikki Kinzer, Pete D. Wright (2024)_  
➤ Centers **radical acceptance** of ADHD as a path to healthy relationships. Addresses **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)**, shame spirals, and the emotional pain that can lead to **social withdrawal**. Encourages **intentional connection, fun, and community support** without the pressure to “fix” yourself first.

**How to ADHD**  
_Jessica McCabe (2024)_  
➤ Validates that **struggling socially is common with ADHD**. Recommends connecting with others who “get it” to reduce shame. Emphasizes the power of **community, collaboration**, and **sharing your story** as a way to reduce isolation and stigma.

**Small Talk: 10 ADHD Lies**  
_Richard Pink, Roxanne Pink (2024)_  
➤ Challenges beliefs like **“I am a burden”** and **“everyone secretly hates me”**. Encourages **naming RSD** in safe friendships and letting others reflect back your value. Offers strategies like **body doubling**, reassurance, and emotional honesty as tools for connection.

**It All Makes Sense Now**  
_Meredith Carder (2024)_  
➤ Emphasizes that **belonging and unmasking** are vital for ADHD well-being. Recommends leaning on trusted community for **values grounding, emotional regulation**, and mutual support during moments of doubt or dysregulation.

**The Disorganized Mind**  
_Nancy A. Ratey (2008)_  
➤ Acknowledges that ADHD-related chaos can affect relationships. Promotes **external systems and emotional separation from ADHD behaviors** to foster clarity, self-trust, and better communication in friendships.

### 💬 Communication, Boundaries, and Social Support

**The Neurodivergence Skills Workbook for Autism and ADHD**  
_Jennifer Kemp, Monique Mitchelson (2024)_  
➤ Encourages prioritizing a **few meaningful relationships** over quantity. Teaches **assertive communication**, asking for accommodations, and honoring your unique social needs without shame.

**Succeeding With Adult ADHD**  
_Abigail L. Levrini (2023)_  
➤ Dedicates a chapter to **improving listening skills** and building intentional social goals. Suggests **paraphrasing**, using a **reminder notebook**, and tracking follow-through on social commitments.

**Order from Chaos**  
_Jaclyn Paul (2018)_  
➤ Notes that **mess, guilt, and mental clutter** often impact friendships. Recommends enlisting **trusted friends for thought processing**, validating the value of safe support in managing daily overwhelm.

**A Radical Guide for Women with ADHD**  
_Sari Solden, Michelle Frank (2019)_  
➤ Encourages **shame reduction, boundary setting, and internal equality** in friendships. Emphasizes asking for help without feeling incompetent and reframing social struggles as part of neurodivergence.

**The ADHD Effect on Marriage**  
_Melissa Orlov (2010)_  
➤ Focuses on **intimate relationships**, but offers tools for all close connections: **verbal cues**, **empathetic communication**, and **shifting from “help” to “partnership”** when managing ADHD challenges together.

### 🌱 Community, Identity, and Support Systems

**How to Thrive with Adult ADHD**  
_James Kustow (2024)_  
➤ Notes that ADHDers often gravitate toward each other for **mutual validation**. Encourages building self-awareness and leaning into supportive communities to improve relationship success.

**Productivity With ADHD**  
_Ju Domi (2025)_  
➤ Frames ADHD traits (like **creativity and big-picture thinking**) as social assets. Stresses that **self-care and support networks** are necessary to sustain connection and prevent burnout in relationships.
